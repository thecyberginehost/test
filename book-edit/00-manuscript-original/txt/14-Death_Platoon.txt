Chapter 14 – Death Platoon
Date: December 16, 2888
Time: 2015 Galactic Standard Time (GTC)
Location: GFC Base: Gladius – War Room
In the dimly lit briefing room, the air heavy with anticipation and the faint hum of machinery, Lieutenant Strickland nonchalantly lit a cigar, the glow briefly illuminating his determined features.
"Strickland, put that out," Captain Orion's voice was firm, betraying a hint of irritation.
With a wry smirk, Strickland exhaled a cloud of smoke, his response laced with sarcasm. "Sure, I'll get right on that.”
Strickland leaned in closer to Captain Orion and blew a puff of smoke towards him.
Orion's scowl deepened, though he couldn't help but admire Strickland's audacity internally. Despite the longstanding rivalry between the OGDF and the Praetorians, a grudging respect existed between the two men. Orion knew that Strickland's disregard for convention often pushed his unit to accomplish what others deemed impossible. This mission, hidden in the shadows of New Eridanus, would be no different.
Wraith's voice was the first to cut through the silence. 
"Let’s cut to the chase. Lieutenant, the mission is critical. You're to secure an ancient relic beneath New Eridanus and ensure there are no survivors. Kill all of the alien bastards you find. We have no need of prisoners this time. Orbital imagery has detected the Obsidians are already excavating."
Strickland's brow furrowed, his gaze shifting to Orion. "Captain, no disrespect, but why not dispatch the Praetorians for this? It's unusual for an OGDF platoon to take orders directly from a Praetorian commander."
Orion's eyes met Strickland's, a spark of respect flickering in their depths. 
"Your platoon's skills in covert operations and the unique methods you employ are precisely what this mission demands. The Praetorians have their strengths, but the methods required to complete the mission could tarnish their reputation. We need a team that operates beyond the usual confines of warfare," he paused, “Besides, GRID thought it best to employ your services for this job.”
A smirk played at the corner of Strickland's mouth. It all made sense now. His eyes sought The Surgeon's across the room. In that fleeting glance, a silent language passed between them—The Surgeon's hand, momentarily pausing in its motion, returned to his task with a slight, almost imperceptible adjustment of his uniform’s wrist cuff. The gesture, mundane to any onlooker, was laden with years of unspoken camaraderie and shared secrets. Around them, the tension of the moment stretched, unnoticed by the others who were absorbed in their own tasks. Yet, it was Wraith who observed the exchange with a quizzical lift of his eyebrow, the only hint of his growing suspicion.
"Understood, sir. You don't need to sugarcoat it for us. We excel in the divine art of killing—no need to dress it up as anything more dignified. I believe your Praetorians call us Death Platoon for a reason." Strickland said proudly.
Orion's expression softened from the earlier sternness as he turned towards Strickland, signaling the shift from reprimand to command. "Strickland, gather your team. You're set to depart within the hour.”
Strickland snapped to attention, offering Orion a salute as crisp as the resolve in his voice. "Don't worry, sir. My team will secure the artifact. We never disappoint."
After the meeting, Wraith and The Surgeon began their descent to Section-47, located two-thousand meters underground within the sprawling confines of Gladius’ GRID Headquarters. The elevator's descent was silent, save for the hum of its mechanisms, until Wraith, unable to contain his curiosity any longer, broke the silence. "What the hell was that back there?"
The Surgeon's laughter echoed ominously within the confined space, a hint of menace lacing his amusement. "I was wondering if you'd pick up on the nuance. Let's just say, Strickland is a very old friend."
Wraith's brows furrowed, pressing for more, "What does that even mean?"
The Surgeon's response was as evasive as it was intriguing. "I'd love to stay and chat, old sport, but again, you do not have the proper classification, and I must get back to my work." He paused, his chuckle resonating with a dark excitement. "Some of the OI fodder that landed in Crete City caught us an Obsidian! How exciting!"
Back in the arms room on the surface of Gladius, the air was charged with anticipation, the walls lined with the weaponry and gear that had seen them through countless engagements. The members of Death Platoon, scattered throughout the space, engaging in last-minute checks and adjustments, froze as Dutch's voice cut through the noise. "Platoon, Attention!" 
Instantly, the room snapped to a uniform stillness, every member turning to face Strickland with an unwavering gaze. The respect they held for him was palpable, a testament to the battles they'd weathered together, and the unspoken bonds forged in the fires of warfare.
"At ease, men. Listen up." Strickland's voice, firm yet infused with an undercurrent of urgency, commanded attention. He paced slowly in front of his team, meeting the eyes of each member as he outlined the gravity of their task. 
The air was thick with anticipation, each soldier attuned to their leader’s presence. Without a word, Strickland activated the Holocom Projector, and an overhead view of New Eridanus materialized in the air before them. The holographic image flickered slightly as it stabilized, showing intricate details of the terrain, marked locations, and possible vantage points.
"As you can see," Strickland began, his pointer tracing lines and circles over the hologram, "the Obsidians have already started their excavation. Our task, gentlemen, is to retrieve an ancient artifact, at all costs." His voice carried a steely determination, the gravity of the mission reflected in his eyes. He paused, letting his words sink in, then added with a certain finality, "And we will be leaving absolutely no survivors."
A smirk briefly crossed his face, a hint of the audacity that had defined so many of their operations. The room was silent, the team absorbing every detail, the reality of their mission settling in their minds.
Strickland's attention then shifted to Dutch, the unit's heavy weapons specialist. His tone shifted, a mix of camaraderie and solemnity as he addressed him. "Dutch, it's time to let Old Faithful out the bag."
At the mention of Old Faithful, a spark of excitement lit up Dutch's eyes, an emotion rarely seen in the battle-hardened warrior. Old Faithful wasn't just any weapon; it was a modified version of the SHR-3663R Portable Chain Gun. Seventeen pounds heavier, with extended range, sub-sonic rounds and a higher rate of fire. It was a testament to human engineering and destructive potential, named in remembrance of Old Earth's turbulent history.
"Hell, it's about time," Dutch replied, his voice thick with anticipation. The room watched as he moved to the weapon's secure cabinet, the air thick with respect and a hint of awe. As he carefully lifted Old Faithful from the weapons rack, cradling the power now at his disposal. Their usual loadout, formidable by any standard, was nothing compared to what they were about to unleash.
This moment underscored the seriousness of their mission and the lengths to which they were prepared to go. Death Platoon was not just a unit; they were a force of nature, ready to unleash their fury on the Obsidians. Strickland's leadership and the unwavering resolve of his team were about to be tested once again, in the shadowy depths of New Eridanus. Most of Death Platoon could care less about the artifact as long as there was something to kill.
Strickland's gaze lingered on the holographic schematic of New Eridanus as he began the OP Brief. His team clustered around, absorbing every detail.
"We’ll be inserting on the outskirts of New Eridanus, at the grid coordinates here and here," Strickland continued, pointing to the locations on the hologram, "there are cave systems that lead directly under the city into the sewers, and other passages that go even deeper than that. That's where we're going. We aren't quite sure where under New Eridanus the artifact is yet, but we're counting on The Surgeon's lovely charm to get us that information from an Obsidian that the OI captured in Crete City."
The team looked at each other, smirking at the thought of The Surgeon tactically extracting information from the alien.
Strickland’s strategic briefing continued, seamlessly weaving in the comparison to past operations. "Remember the insurgents on planet Elysium when we were inserted into the jungle outside Kazmatal city?” He paused, looking for nods of agreement,  “It'll be just like that, but eventually we will be underground. We go in, we stay quiet, quiet kills only, until we either have no choice, or until we retrieve the artifact." His gaze then shifted pointedly towards Dutch, "Once we retrieve the artifact," he shot a glare thick with meaning, "then you can pull out Old Faithful. Understood?"
"Yes, sir!," came the unified response, a chorus of determination.
"We deploy at 2130 hours. We're going to free drop from a stealthed Pegasus Transport from the lower atmosphere. Singular pods this time, gents." Strickland’s voice carried the weight of their mission, underscoring the seriousness of their approach.
He elaborated on the choice of singular pods, their design prioritizing stealth and protection. "Singular pods were chosen for this mission due to the anonymity. Virtually powerless and undetectable on radar, with reflective outer armor providing you with protection and concealment. It's as if the pods were translucent on the way to their drop site."
"Now, are there any questions?" Strickland opened the floor, inviting inquiries.
Marsden, the team's demolition expert, was the first to raise a hand. "What's our contingency if the drop zones are compromised?"
"We have several drop locations mapped out. If one is compromised, we switch to the secondary or tertiary. If we need to use any of them, your pod’s navigation systems will be updated. You won’t need to use manual controls."
Next, Knox, the team's tech specialist, chimed in. "How are we handling comms? Traditional frequencies could get us pinged."
Strickland nodded, "We're using scramblers in conjunction with burst transmissions. Short, encrypted messages for emergencies only. Otherwise, we’ll be close enough at all times for hand and arm signals."
Finally, Axe, known for his heavy loadouts, asked, "What about extra gear that can't fit in the pods?"
Strickland's answer was pragmatic, "What can't fit in your drop pod will be transported in its own pod. We'll recover after we drop. Worst case, we can't. So, prioritize only what is absolutely necessary.”
Dutch, with a grin that could only be described as affectionate, wrapped an arm around Old Faithful, "My baby's staying with me," he declared, his tone a mix of jest and a hint of vengeance, “I have a score to settle. And Old Faithful is gonna be the one to help me do it.”
Lieutenant Strickland, seeing the hurt and anger in Dutch’s face, walked up to him and put his arm on his right shoulder’s armor plate. “Your father was a good warship commander. I knew him, you know. I was stationed on the warship GFC Fringe when Captain Harris was just a Lieutenant. Well before he took command of the The Vindicator. Did he ever tell you that story?”
“No, sir. He did not.” Dutch replied.
“I was on the bridge when the insurgent fleet attacked us. We were in orbit above Crete. The captain got hit by a phasor lance round. Came right through the viewport. Your father immediately took command without hesitation. He destroyed four insurgent warships that day. Not one projectile hit The Fringe after your father took command.  I’ve never seen anything like it.” He lent Dutch a light smile and shifted his thoughts back to the mission.
"We move out in fifteen. Meet up at the launch pad," Strickland commanded, his voice cutting through the chatter and preparations with the precision of a laser. The room sprang into action, each member of Death Platoon methodically checking and double-checking their gear.
The platoon's loadouts were a testament to their varied roles and expertise. Strickland carried a compact, high-powered blaster designed for quick, lethal engagements and a set of stealth-enhancing gadgets. Dutch, of course, had Old Faithful slung over his shoulder, along with an array of explosives and breach charges. Hawkins opted for a lighter setup, focusing on mobility and reconnaissance tools, including a cloaking device and a silenced sidearm. The rest of the team equipped themselves similarly, with specialized gear tailored to their specific functions within the unit.
Axe, however, found himself in a bit of a predicament. His recon kit, packed with scanning devices, drones, and extra communication gear, proved challenging to streamline for the pod's space constraints. As he tried to strap an oversized, multi-frequency antenna to his battle harness, Hawkins couldn't help but quip, "Planning to broadcast the mission live to the whole galaxy, Axe?"
Without missing a beat, Axe shot back, "Oh absolutely. It’ll be live streaming back to Veridian Prime. The civilians are going to love how we work," He said sarcastically.
Laughter filled the room, easing the tension as they completed their preparations. Then, in a display of unity and resolve, they made their way to the launch pad. The door stood open, revealing the Pegasus Transport, its engines humming softly, ready for the mission ahead.
As they formed up in front of Lieutenant Strickland, he made his rounds, inspecting each member with a critical eye. Coming to Axe, Strickland paused, an eyebrow raised at the sight. Attached to Axe's harness was a makeshift, brightly colored umbrella, rigged to deploy in case of rain.
"Really? You're bringing that shit?" Strickland couldn't hide his amusement.
Axe grinned, "Never know when you'll need some shade, sir. Plus, it's camouflage— and you never know when a cave will flood.”
Strickland, after a moment's pause to appreciate the absurdity, grabbed the umbrella from Axe's harness and tossed it aside. "Always a pain in the ass, Axe," he remarked, the corner of his mouth twitching as if he were fighting a smile. 
This ritual was an unspoken game within Death Platoon, a challenge Axe embraced with gusto before every mission. He had taken it upon himself to attach something utterly ridiculous to his gear each time, aiming for the elusive goal of drawing a laugh from their stoic lieutenant.
This ongoing bet among the members of Death Platoon was simple: whoever could make Lieutenant Strickland laugh would win free drinks at The Rig, their favorite dive bar on Veridian Prime, when they are on leave. Despite their best efforts and creative attempts, Strickland had remained unflappable, always noticing but never yielding the sought-after laugh.
As the thrown umbrella clattered to the side, the team shared a look, their expressions a mix of disappointment and amusement. It was another miss, but the attempt itself brought a moment of levity, tightening the bonds of camaraderie and lightening the mood before the gravity of their mission settled back in.
With the umbrella now discarded and a shared chuckle fading away, Strickland signaled for the team to board the transport. "Let's focus on the mission. Eyes forward, Death Platoon. Load up!”
