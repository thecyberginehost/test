Chapter 17 – A Dish Best Served Cold
Date: December 17, 2888
Time: 0815, Galactic Standard Time (GTC)
Location: Heart of New Eridanus – One Kilometer from the Objective
Perched behind the remnants of a shattered high-rise, Fireteam Valor had the enemy transport ships within their line of sight. The security around the transport ships was more relaxed than expected. Kaine's stealth drones, flitting through the ruins like ghostly sentinels, reported minimal enemy security. It seemed the bulk of the enemy forces were pulling out of New Eridanus, a move that puzzled Lieutenant Argus. He speculated whether this retreat was linked to the urgent matter Captain Orion had hinted at needing to discuss.
Argus and Rourke surveyed their bleak circumstances. Most of their comrades had fallen; the simplicity of a full-scale evacuation had evaporated with those losses. Now, it was just Fireteam Valor.
As they synchronized their watches for the assault, a crackle of static burst through their comms. "Valor Six, this is Operative Wraith, how copy, over?"
Argus pressed the communicator, his voice steady. "Loud and clear, Wraith. Go ahead."
Wraith's voice was calm, a stark contrast to the chaos that had engulfed the city. "I’m currently on an inactive orbital defense platform, above your location. I have two additional personnel about to deploy to your objective’s location. We will be performing a combat free drop. We’ll secure those ships, just back us up. Believe me, you’ll know when we’ve landed."
Rourke, overhearing the exchange, rolled his eyes and muttered, "Of course, more GRID operatives."
Ignoring Rourke's comment, Argus stayed focused. "Roger that, Wraith. What’s your ETA?"
"ETA on objective, ninety seconds, give or take a few seconds, dependent on resistance from the enemy."
"Understood, Wraith. We’ll cover your approach. Valor Six out." Argus cut the transmission, turning to his team. "Positions, everyone. This is it."
The team moved silently, finding cover among the debris and fallen architecture. Each member knew the plan; each knew the risks. They positioned themselves strategically, eyes on the skies and the landing zones.
Seventy-five tense seconds ticked by. Each second stretching out as they waited for the promised sign. Suddenly, the air was split by the thunderous roar of descent thrusters. It was almost as if the GRID Operatives were making their descent obvious. High above, three shapes cut through the smoke-filled sky—Wraith and his team, encapsulated in the fiery embrace of atmospheric entry.
As Wraith and his team plummeted toward the planet's surface, the airspace around them erupted into chaos. Enemy fighters swooped in, their weapons blazing trails of deadly energy, while anti-air batteries on the ground thudded, launching salvos that streaked through the sky with lethal intent. Yet, the three GRID operatives maneuvered through the onslaught with breathtaking agility.
To Wraith's right, Operative Tor twisted his body in a sharp barrel roll, an energy projectile whizzing past him by mere inches. The proximity of the blast left a heat trace that Tor could feel against the fabric of his suit, a stark reminder of the narrow line between life and death in their high-stakes descent.
On Wraith's left, Operative Eclipse showcased the new enhancements to her Graviton Arrestors. With a burst of acceleration that seemed to bend the very light around her, Eclipse dodged incoming fire, her movements a blur of precision and speed. They were close now, the looming silhouette of a tall building adjacent to the hovering warship providing a brief cover from the relentless enemy fire.
As they neared their target, Wraith signaled using their helmet’s tactical displays, a targeting reticle appearing in the center of their visors, the reticle moving wherever the eyes look. It was time to fight.
The moment before impact, Tor activated his combat gloves' embedded blowback charge. The technology, fed by the kinetic energy of their descent, hummed to life, a barely visible glow emanating from his gloves as they absorbed the atmospheric friction and converted it into raw power. Then, landing with the force of a meteor, Tor drove his fist into the chest of an unsuspecting Obsidian soldier who had been firing from cover.
The effect was instantaneous and horrifying. The Obsidian soldier was catapulted backward, the force of the blow not just stopping him in his tracks but obliterating the alien’s entire upper body in a gruesome display of the technology's potential. The blowback charge, far more potent than even Tor had anticipated, left nothing but a cloud of dust, the lower half of an alien body and a gruesome reminder of its power.
Catching his breath, Tor couldn't help but let out a low chuckle, the adrenaline of the moment mingling with a surge of triumph. Turning to Eclipse, who was swiftly dispatching another Obsidian soldier with her modified P19-HE Nightstalker pistol, Tor called out, "See, I told you it would work!"
Eclipse, momentarily pausing to glance at the destruction caused by Tor's new tech, allowed herself a brief smirk. Her weapon clicked as she reloaded, her movements smooth and practiced.
"Alright, I'll admit it," she shouted back, her voice cutting through the din of the continuing battle, "That was impressive. Just try not to blow us up in the process next time!"
"Relax, Ec," Tor smiled, wiping a smear of dust from his visor with the back of his gloved hand, "I ensured the design would only affect the objects that come in contact with my combat gloves."
Their exchange was cut short as more Obsidian soldiers converged on their position. With a nod, Wraith directed their formation, pointing towards the heart of the enemy line. They moved as one, a lethal dance of gunfire and energy weapons, each step forward reclaiming ground in the heart of New Eridanus.
As they advanced, the battlefield around them bore witness to the brutal efficiency of GRID technology melded with human determination. Each fallen enemy, a testament to their resolve, each step forward a march towards retribution. This was their domain now, a storm of violence and vengeance, and Fireteam Valor was the eye of that storm.
The battlefield was alive with chaos and fury as Fireteam Valor witnessed the GRID operatives’ brutal efficiency. As Tor's gloves discharged another devastating blowback charge, sending enemy debris scattering across the war-torn landscape, Harlan couldn't contain his astonishment.
"Holy shit! You see that, Rourke? I need some of whatever's in those gloves!" Harlan exclaimed, his eyes wide behind his visor as he ducked a returning volley of enemy fire.
Rourke, ever the stoic warrior, merely grunted in response, his attention fixed on the enemy positions. "Focus on the mission, Harlan. We'll admire the fireworks later," he replied, loading another round into his rifle.
Lieutenant Argus observed the unfolding carnage with a critical eye. The effectiveness of the GRID operatives had given them a significant tactical advantage, one he was keen to exploit. "It's showtime!" he bellowed over the din of the battle, his voice a clarion call to action.
“Harlan, on me. Royce, you’re with Rourke. We bound up. Watch your sectors of fire. Let’s move!"
They bounded from cover to cover, their movements synchronized and precise, while simultaneously laying down a suppressive fire that allowed the GRID operatives to press forward with less resistance.
Argus, leading from the front, set the pace. Each command he issued was calculated to keep the enemy off balance while maximizing the impact of their assault. The rhythmic thud of his boots on the rubble-strewn ground punctuated each order, a relentless drumbeat driving their advance. The air was thick with the smell of ionized energy and burning debris, the soundscape a cacophony of gunfire, explosions, and the distant cries of wounded soldiers.
Above them, the skies had turned a darker shade, the late morning sun obscured by the smoke of burning structures and the looming presence of the enemy warship. Despite the apocalyptic setting, Argus's resolve hardened. “We have to make it,” he thought grimly, his rifle up and tracking as he led his team forward. “Whatever the cost.”
As Fireteam Valor regrouped at the objective, the GRID operatives had already breached the perimeter of the primary enemy transport. Wraith, Tor, and Eclipse moved like shadows, dispatching guards and securing entry points with clinical precision.
Argus watched as Tor, with a deft motion, planted an explosive charge on a sealed hatch. The charge detonated with a sharp crack, the door blowing inward in a shower of sparks and molten metal. "Fireteam Valor, on me! Breach and clear!" Argus commanded, signaling his team to follow the operatives into the heart of the enemy transport.
As they entered the dimly lit corridors of the ship, the sound of their boots echoed ominously. Ahead of them, the GRID operatives moved stealthily, clearing a path through the labyrinthine interior. Each corner turned could spell death, but the combined force of Fireteam Valor and the GRID Operatives was unstoppable.
Argus's voice rang out, steady and commanding, driving his men forward through the chaos. They were the spearhead of humanity's rage, each step a strike against those who had dared to challenge them on their soil.
The dimly lit corridor of the enemy transport echoed with the sounds of distant combat, but in that particular section, a more intimate and brutal struggle unfolded. Tor had managed to isolate an Obsidian soldier, the enemy's dark, armored form stark against the grim backdrop of the ship's interior. His handling of the soldier was both precise and savage—a brutal ballet of control and pain.
Tor's left arm was outstretched, his hand gripping the Obsidian's wrist with such ferocity that the crunch of bone resonated in the confined space. His right arm was locked in a chokehold around the soldier's neck, not enough to cut off air but enough to assert undeniable control. The Obsidian soldier, caught in Tor's iron grip, struggled futilely as the language of his captor filled the air—a series of harsh, guttural tones that seemed to scrape against the metal walls of the corridor.
The conversation between them was cryptic, filled with the harsh syllables of the Obsidian language, which Tor had learned during his covert operations. As the exchange grew more heated, Tor's impatience flared. With a swift, brutal motion, he snapped the Obsidian's wrist. The sound was sharp, a sickening snap that echoed slightly, followed by a pained roar from the soldier.
Tor then threw his opponent to the ground with overwhelming force. He pounced, his knee landing heavily on the Obsidian's collarbone, pinning him to the floor. This position restricted the soldier's movements effectively without entirely cutting off his air supply. Tor's other knee and arms immobilized the Obsidian further, a technique perfected over countless missions.
The Obsidian, under extreme duress, uttered something that shifted the atmosphere—a piece of information or perhaps a plea that seemed to satisfy Tor's immediate objectives. A hint of a grim smile played across Tor's lips as he responded in the Obsidian's tongue, his voice calm but carrying an undertone of cold finality.
Releasing his hold, Tor stepped back and allowed the Obsidian soldier to rise. The soldier, clutching his broken wrist, stumbled forward, his steps uneven and filled with pain. As the Obsidian started to run towards the main corridor, Tor's hand slipped to his sidearm, drawing the custom pistol with a practiced ease. Without a hint of hesitation, he fired a single shot. The bullet, designed to explode on impact, hit the Obsidian in the back of the head, resulting in a gruesome end—a spray of dark matter painted against the corridor wall.
"Well now, you don’t see someone fall for that too often," Tor quipped, turning to glance at his nearby teammates.
"Pity he didn’t put up more of a fight. It was rather disappointing." he mumbled, more to himself than anyone else, as he checked his weapon and, Tor melded back into the formation. The team continued their advance, each step taking them deeper into the heart of the enemy transport ship.
As the eerie silence settled back over the dim corridor of the enemy transport, Rourke eyed Tor with a mix of bemusement and incredulity. They were advancing cautiously, their boots echoing softly against the cold metal floor, but Rourke's mind was still on the confrontation they had just witnessed.
"How the hell do you know their language?" Rourke asked, his tone a mix of curiosity and suspicion.
Tor grinned, a hint of mischief in his eyes as he glanced at Rourke while reloading his weapon. "Well, that's classified," he chuckled, the humor not quite reaching his eyes, veiled as they were by the gravity of their mission.
"Figures," Rourke muttered, shaking his head slightly as he checked the charge on his own rifle. The curiosity was eating at him, though. "Okay, then what the hell did you say to him?"
Tor's smile faded a bit, replaced by a more somber expression as he recalled the exchange. "Well, first I was joking with him, asking for the launch codes. He, of course, had no clue what I was talking about,” he paused, “Begged for his life and blah, blah, blah let me go so I can see my family." Tor's voice was nonchalant, almost detached, as if discussing something as mundane as the weather.
He continued, his gaze now fixed on the corridor ahead, alert for any signs of enemy movement. "Then I told him I'd let him go if he told me where the navigations console was and how to put it on autopilot. My foresight acknowledged he was telling the truth, so I calmly told him to leave. Then I shot him, as you already know." Tor paused, his lips quirking in a half-smile. "Can't have an Obsidian blowing our only chance at success now, can we?"
Rourke let out a low grunt, a sound that conveyed both his acknowledgment of the necessity of Tor's actions and his discomfort with how easily the line between pragmatism and cruelty could blur in the heat of combat. "Right," he said quietly, his eyes scanning the shadowy stretch of corridor ahead.
"Keep your eyes sharp and your foresight grim. I sense you have the ability as well." Tor said, his tone shifting back to the business at hand. "We're not out of this yet."
Rourke nodded, his professional demeanor snapping back into place. The weight of the rifle in his hands felt reassuring, a tangible reminder of the immediate dangers they faced. The corridor stretched ominously before them, each shadow and each silent, foreboding doorway a potential threat.
As they moved forward, the grim reality of their mission pressed down on them, but so did a sense of camaraderie forged in the fires of countless battles. Whatever lay ahead, they would face it together, with every member of Fireteam Valor and the GRID Operatives ready to play their part in the shadowy game of war that danced around the edges of morality.
Wraith and Eclipse swept into the room with the swiftness and precision that marked the elite GRID operatives. Their entry cut through the lingering tension like a knife, shifting the focus back to the urgency of their mission. Wraith’s gaze swept over the assembled members of Fireteam Valor, a nod substituting for any formal greetings.
"No time for formal introductions," Wraith announced crisply, his voice carrying a calm authority that demanded attention. His eyes then locked onto Tor, recognizing the importance of swiftly gaining control of the transport. "Where's the navigations console?" he asked, his tone brooking no delay.
Tor pointed towards a sleek panel adorned with alien script and intricate designs that seemed to pulse subtly with latent energy. Without missing a beat, Wraith pulled a compact device from his tactical vest—the AdaptiSync Modulator—a tool designed for precisely this type of operation. 
As Wraith approached the console, the AdaptiSync Modulator in hand, the device hummed to life. Its sensors emitted a soft blue glow, scanning the configuration of the port which was unlike any standard human technology. Within moments, the device adjusted its own physical structure, morphing to mimic the plug's unique shape. This adaptive technology was one of GRID's latest innovations, allowing operatives to interface with a variety of alien technologies encountered across the galaxy, a big step up from the Neuro-Morphic Interface Pad.
Wraith carefully inserted the now-transformed Modulator into the port. There was a brief moment of silence as the device interfaced with the ship's systems. Then, almost like a being awakening from a deep slumber, the ship stirred to life. Overhead lights flickered on, bathing the interior in a sterile white light. Holo-panels activated with a low hum, casting soft glows of data across their surfaces. The navigations console, previously dormant, now burst into activity, displaying maps and trajectories that danced and shifted in mid-air.
Wraith scanned the information rapidly, his training allowing him to absorb and analyze the data at an impressive speed. He turned to address the group, his expression serious, reflecting the gravity of their situation.
"We need as much intel as possible from these bastards," he stated firmly, meeting the eyes of each team member in turn. "When we're en-route, I'll make the formal introductions."
The promise of more information hung in the air as a new urgency took hold. Each member of the team understood the reality—control of the transport was only the first step. The real challenge lay in what came next; using this ship to infiltrate further into enemy territory and gather the intelligence necessary to halt, or at least disrupt their operations.
With Wraith’s guidance, they began to prepare for departure. The ship's alien technology was now at their command, thanks to the AdaptiSync Modulator’s seamless integration. This temporary victory gave them a crucial advantage, but the road ahead remained fraught with danger and uncertainty.
As they settled into their roles, checking their equipment and securing the ship's systems for takeoff, the weight of their mission rested heavily on each of them. Yet, there was a shared resolve, a collective determination that bound them together. They were no longer just soldiers from disparate units; they were a unified force, poised to strike at the heart of an enemy that had brought war to their doorstep. The journey ahead would test them all, but for now, they had a direction, and they had each other.
Now securely en route aboard the commandeered enemy transport, Wraith took the opportunity to formalize the introductions between his GRID operatives, Eclipse and Tor, and the rest of Fireteam Valor. The atmosphere was charged with a mix of tension and anticipation as Wraith laid out the broader scope of their mission.
"Eclipse and Tor aren't here just to help destroy the ship," Wraith began, his voice steady and commanding. "This ship holds information too valuable to let it burn before we can extract it. Any intelligence we gather could give us a crucial advantage over our enemies, possibly even access to new technologies."
He paused, ensuring his next words were clearly understood. "I am implementing GRID directive Clandestine. I am taking over this operation to ensure its completion. We will still destroy this ship, but only after we complete our prime directive."
Rourke, always skeptic of GRID’s overriding protocols, couldn’t hide his discontent. "This is such bullshit," he muttered under his breath.
Eclipse, overhearing Rourke's comment, turned to face him squarely. Her expression was both stern and unforgiving, yet her eyes hinted at a deeper understanding of the common ground they shared. "Your feelings on the matter are not our concern," she retorted sharply. "But we are all on the same team. We may have different prime directives, but make no mistake, the outcome will be the same."
With the air slightly cleared, Wraith began to distribute the assignments with precision. "Royce will be with Eclipse and Tor. You are to find the data control room. It’s critical that you secure access to the enemy’s intelligence network."
Turning to the rest of Fireteam Valor, Wraith continued, "I will accompany you to the ship’s reactor room. That’s where we blow the nuke. If their ship’s reactors are anything like ours, we can enhance destructive capability of the nuke, using their own technology against them."
He laid out the final part of the plan with clinical detail. "Once we have what we need, we’ll set the nuke’s remote activation. When we’ve reached a safe distance from the ship, we’ll detonate the nuke.”
As the commandeered enemy transport neared the immense warship, the atmosphere among the team was one of quiet tension mixed with a palpable sense of urgency. The docking bay loomed ahead, a gaping maw that seemed to swallow whole swathes of shadow and light. Wraith’s strategic maneuvering had paid off; the Obsidian forces, deceived by their own protocols, believed them to be one of their returning crafts. The bay doors opened, welcoming them into the heart of the enemy stronghold.
Inside the cockpit of the transport, Wraith and Argus shared a moment of silent acknowledgment—a nod of respect for each other’s roles and efforts in the mission’s critical juncture. It was then that Wraith turned to Argus with a more personal piece of news, his voice carrying a softer edge amidst the mechanical hum of the ship's systems.
"And Argus, you’d also be happy to know your son is going to make it. He is going to make a full recovery and is already somewhat coherent," Wraith revealed, watching closely for the lieutenant’s reaction, “Tough kid, that’s for damn sure.”
The weight of the news visibly lifted from Argus’s shoulders, his usually stern features softening with a profound relief. He exhaled deeply, a sigh that seemed to carry with it the burdens of fear and uncertainty he had been shouldering. "Thank you," Argus managed to say, his voice thick with emotion. "That’s the best news I could have hoped for."
As the transport secured itself in the docking clamps of the enemy warship, the team prepared for the next phase. Wraith briefly laid out the final check for their equipment before disembarking. "Check your gear one last time. Remember, the data control room and the reactor are our primary targets. We stick to the plan, we get the intel, we set the nuke for detonation, and we get out. Keep it tight."
