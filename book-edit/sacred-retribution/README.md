## Planned canon beats (Obsidian saga)

### "The family's last stand" — Drakken's discovery (mirror of human Ch11 "Tragedy")
- **The human book SHOWS the tragedy; the Obsidian book does NOT.** The slaughter — the **hate-engine** — is depicted on the **human side** (Ch11 "Tragedy": Argus and the aftermath, his critically injured son). The **Obsidian side does NOT re-show the killing**; Drakken simply **discovers** the aftermath, framed for **respect and honor, not shock.** (Per-book rule: hate lives only in the human book; the Obsidian book earns respect.)
- **Never named as Argus's family — but the reader realizes it.** The text never states whose home it is. Instead, **descriptive callbacks** (the home as established in **Ch1**, specific objects, the wife & daughter, the boy's signs) make the *reader* think *"oh shit — this is Argus's family,"* while **Drakken never knows** (✅ locked: tragic irony). Works on two levels: standalone = "a brave human family"; cross-saga = the gut-punch.
- **The killers = a ROGUE Titan Vanguard UNIT** (✅ author ruling — NOT the Dra'Kor Vhel'Tan). A unit of a normally *honorable* order gone rogue, butchering civilians — to Drakken, *worse* than a known brute: a betrayal of the honor the Vanguard embodies.
- ⭐ **SCALE — far more than one home (✅ author ruling):** the rogue unit slaughtered **the entire civilian populace in its vicinity** of New Eridanus; the Argus home is just **the one scene the reader recognizes** inside a mass atrocity. And **every city hit in the simultaneous assault on Trident saw something similar.** This is the **human book's hate-engine** (shown there, in full); the Obsidian book reveals it as **rogue and dishonorable** — what honorable Obsidians like Drakken are sickened by. *(Possible thread: honor breaking down = early Void-corruption seep, and/or part of why Kyr's Vanguard is "off track" — flagged, not asserted.)*
- **The youngling's stand (✅ author detail):** in this household a **human youngling slew TWO Obsidian warriors** before the family fell — a *commendable counterattack* that astonishes Drakken (a *soldier's* family, itself a recognition clue). Drakken finds the **two warriors dead/dying** — he scowls at any still breathing, **no honor for them** — beside the slain wife & daughter, but **no youngling body** → he assumes the boy died too. ⭐ **That youngling is Junior** (gravely wounded, out of sight) — he lives; Argus finds him after. Drakken never learns the boy survived, or whose son he is.
- **Drakken's reaction:** honors the dead humans (warrior's respect for how hard they fought); **denies the dying Vanguard any honor** — a scowl, no Vor'kara, left to bleed out in contempt. ⭐ A root of his "newfound respect for humans" → the **Lok'Sha O'Ka with Argus** → the **alliance pull** (the very thing the Void works to prevent).
- **Junior survives (canon — he's the Harbinger / Lyrana's peace partner).** Drakken finds **no third body**, only signs a boy fought and is gone, and assumes he crawled off to die — **he's simply wrong.** Junior is gravely wounded and out of sight; **Argus finds him alive afterward** (human Ch11). Working sequence: Drakken sweeps through first → Argus arrives after.
- ⚠️ **CRAFT DEPENDENCY (human side) — ANCHORS LOCKED (Ch1):** the recognition fires on **two distinctive objects already planted in Ch1**: **(1) Kat's one-eared stuffed rabbit, "Sergeant Hops"** (she believes toys come alive, leaves them as bait), and **(2) Junior's hand-built model freighter with a scavenged radio** — the very device that first intercepts the Obsidian transmissions ("Vor'kara," "Dra'Kor Vhel'Tan"). **Ch11 (Tragedy) must echo these in the aftermath**, and the Obsidian-side Drakken-discovery scene then describes them **WITHOUT naming** (a one-eared rabbit in the rubble; a child's model ship) so the reader recognizes the family Drakken never identifies. ⭐ **Bonus:** Junior — the youngling who later fells two warriors — is the same boy whose radio first caught the enemy.

### "Something is off with Vrakar" — the FIRST corruption Easter egg (Obsidian saga)
- **Drakken brings the atrocity straight to Vrakar.** Reporting the rogue Titan Vanguard's mass slaughter — and the commendable human resistance (a youngling who slew two warriors) — Drakken expects the *true* Vrakar's response: **fury** at his own troops' dishonor, and a warrior's **respect** for the valor.
- **Vrakar's reaction is WRONG (the tell):** a **lack of care at first**, then a **belated, oddly intense but misdirected upset** — feeling *without* the right action. Per the corruption mechanic (lore §3), his **judgment is caged** — he can no longer make the honorable *decision* (execute the rogue unit) — even as the corruption **amplifies his emotion.** The **decoupling of intense feeling from right action** is the tell: the old Vrakar *acted* (eye-for-an-eye); this one emotes and does nothing.
- **Drakken's private unease (POV beat):** ~*"I do not understand Vrakar of late. Normally he would be furious. He showed not an ounce of feeling for a commendable stand — one human household where a youngling felled two of our warriors before they fell."* The first time **Drakken senses something is off.**
- ⭐ **Physical tell — the skin:** Drakken notices **Vrakar's skin coloring has subtly changed** — the **same corruption-tell that visibly *leaves* him at death** (when Lyrana watches his skin return to normal; see Vrakar in the lore bible). Drakken **will not mention it — not his place/duty.**
- **Joins the "sensers":** Drakken now stands with **Lyrana** and **Ujio** — three loyal Obsidians who feel the wrongness in Vrakar and cannot/will not name it (none knows it's the Void).
- **FIRST on-page corruption Easter egg** for the reader. *(Corruption onset itself dates to ~2838 / Sahk V'lok; this is its first narrative TELL in the saga.)*
- ⭐ **Locks the Kyr thread:** the rogue Vanguard unit is **why Kyr's Titan Vanguard is "off track"** (the bible's Kyr reprimand) — Kyr is charged with reining them back to honor.
- ⭐ **The precedent that makes it land (✅ author-canon):** Vrakar & Drakken go back to the **War of the Five Suns** (Drakken helped him win it). Drakken has **only ever seen Vrakar handle this honorably** — once, during the **Kresh Rebellion**, Drakken reported **another rogue unit's slaughter of innocents**, and Vrakar **put that unit to death, eye-for-an-eye.** So the indifference now isn't a vague feeling — it's a **direct reversal of a precedent Drakken witnessed himself.** *(Tragic: the corruption has hollowed the man who once executed his own troops to avenge the innocent.)* ⭐ **Timeline hinge:** Vrakar's **corruption began *during* the Kresh Rebellion** (onset ≈ its defeat / the Sahk V'lok capture). The eye-for-an-eye was the **uncorrupted** Vrakar *earlier* in that rebellion — maybe the last honorable judgment Drakken saw — and the rot has **deepened over the ~50 years** since. New Eridanus shows how far gone he is.
