Chapter 16: Shadows Over New Eridanus
Date: December 17, 2888
Time: 0530, Galactic Standard Time (GTC)
Location: Heart of New Eridanus
In the war-torn heart of New Eridanus, the silence of the early morning was deceptive. Fireteam Valor, alongside Fireteam Ironclad and their attachments, moved with ghost-like precision through the rubble. Stealth drones hummed softly overhead, their sensors sweeping the path ahead to ensure that the route was clear of enemy patrols. The air was thick with the scent of ionized particles and smoldering debris.
Kaine, having taken on the role of the team’s primary scout since Tessa's injury and subsequent evacuation, was a blur among the shadows. His movements were fluid and assured as he darted from one building to another, climbing to vantage points that overlooked their planned route. From these heights, he relayed crucial information back to the teams—silent gestures indicating clear paths or potential hazards.
His role had never been more critical. With Tessa gone, much depended on his keen observation skills and his ability to stay one step ahead of the Obsidian forces. His headset buzzed quietly, a secure line to the team leaders, where he whispered concise updates.
"Three hostiles on the road, two blocks north, moving east," Kaine reported, his voice a low murmur. His eyes never stopped scanning the horizon, the digital overlay in his visor highlighting points of interest.
Below, Lieutenant Argus, leading Fireteam Valor, nodded slightly at Kaine’s report. He turned to his team, his hand signals crisp in the dim light. Argus raised up a fist, signaling the teams to hold. They would let the hostiles pass to maintain the element of surprise.
In the midst of this silent communication, the group's pace was steady but cautious. Every corner they turned, every alley they crossed was a potential ambush point. The stealth drones played a crucial part in their strategy, not just as lookouts but as distractions if needed. Their route had been meticulously planned to avoid enemy concentrations, utilizing data collected over days of continuous drone surveillance.
As dawn began to break, the grey light cast long shadows across the debris, giving the city a spectral appearance. Fireteam Valor and their allies were shadows themselves, melding into the environment, becoming part of the city’s broken landscape as they moved toward their objective.
As the teams progressed stealthily toward their objective to commandeer an enemy transport ship, their reliance on a Neuro-Morphic Interface Pad, or simply NMIP, was crucial. This device provided instantaneous operational mastery of any vehicle it interfaced with, excluding language decryption.
Amidst their silent trek, Kaine spotted an Obsidian scout who had detected their presence. The enemy reached for his communicator, but Kaine swiftly intervened by lobbing a Whisper Grenade—courtesy of Wraith. This stealth grenade released a localized electromagnetic pulse designed to disable electronic devices and incapacitate organic beings without noise.
The grenade landed silently, unleashing a subdued flash near the scout. As the Obsidian collapsed, Kaine quickly approached to confirm the takedown. He unsheathed his combat knife, its blade black and honed to a deadly precision. Kneeling beside the fallen enemy, he carefully placed his hand over the scout's mouth to stifle any sound. With a precise and quick thrust, he punctured the alien's throat, directly through the center. He twisted the knife and sliced outward, a dark blue mist following the momentum. Dark blue blood poured out of the wound aggressively as the alien convulsed.
The Obsidian's eyes widened, and a gurgling whisper escaped as its life faded. Kaine kept his hand firmly over the alien's mouth, silencing its dying breaths. Withdrawing his knife, he noticed the blade smeared with the alien's blood, an eerie hue unlike any human blood. Kaine wiped the blade clean against the Obsidian scout's armored suit, the rough material effectively scraping off the alien residue.
With the area secured and the threat permanently neutralized, Kaine signaled the all-clear to Fireteam Valor. Their mission proceeded with renewed caution, every member aware of the stakes should they be discovered.
As the light of dawn filtered through the smoke-laden skies above New Eridanus, the tension among the ranks of Fireteam Valor and their allies was palpable. The objective, confirmed by the stealth drones—a series of fast fighters and heavy transports—was located roughly five-hundred meters away, embedded deep within a heavily fortified Obsidian encampment.
Lieutenant Argus surveyed the terrain through his tactical visor, his voice crackling over the comms as he radioed in the coordinating instructions. "Ironclad Six, this is Valor Six. Take your team, the OGDF and Orbital Infantry Troopers west to flank. Stealth drones have visuals on the objective. We'll approach from the north and meet you on the other side."
Acknowledging the command, Lieutenant Charles, leading Fireteam Ironclad, confirmed with a terse nod and a quick, "Copy that, Valor Six. Moving out." His team moved with precision, spreading out to cover more ground and encircle the target.

At the center of Ironclad's formation, secured in a hardened case, rode the package—the single tactical warhead Valor had pulled from the Delta bunker. With the fleet that once carried such ordnance now ash in orbit, it was the last nuclear weapon left on Trident—the Coalition's one and only shot at gutting the warship from within. Ironclad would carry it to the objective; Valor would clear the road. That was the plan.
As both teams began their trek through the ravaged cityscape, the quiet shattered. Without warning, elite Obsidians in dark charcoal armor shot through with menacing purple emerged from hidden positions—the hum of red energy swords waking, the whine of energy projectiles splitting the air. These were not the foot soldiers Valor had cut down before. They moved with terrifying coordination, and they had been waiting.
The first volley fell on Ironclad. Caught in the open, Charles's team scrambled for cover with their OGDF and OI counterparts, and before they could consolidate, a well-placed energy blast sheared through a crumbling wall and brought it down across the street—a barricade of rubble and fire that cut Lieutenant Charles and his men off from the rest of the world.
"Charles!" Argus was already moving. His comms answered with nothing but static, the Obsidian countermeasures warbling every channel into noise. Through the smoke on the far side of the collapse he could see the strobe of Ironclad's muzzle flashes, hear the distant hammer of human weapons under the deeper thrum of alien ones. They were alive. They were pinned. And they had the only warhead on Trident sitting in a hardened case in the middle of them.
Argus didn't hesitate. "We are not leaving them. We break through and we get them out." He carved the street with two fingers. "Harlan, Royce—left flank, on me. We push up the boulevard and make ourselves the loudest thing out here. Rourke, Kaine—right, through the market row. You come in behind their line and crack it open. We catch these bastards between us and Ironclad and we punch a hole. Move."
They moved. Argus's flank drew the worst of it, Harlan's Shredder roaring as he seated the white-phosphorus drum he saved for the bad days, the rounds igniting against Obsidian armor and turning the boulevard into a curtain of chemical fire that even the elites flinched from. Royce fought at his shoulder, steadier than the green kid who'd shipped out of Gladius, and for a moment the plan was working—the enemy turned toward the louder threat, and on the right flank a gap opened.
Rourke felt the strike before it landed. That was the shape of his gift and its cruelty both: the half-second of foresight that showed him the blow a breath early, never long enough to stop it, only long enough to know. Wrongness bloomed across the market row like heat off an open furnace, and he hit Kaine across the chest and drove him backward—"Down—DOWN—"—a heartbeat before the world stood on its edge.
The blast took the whole row. Heavy ordnance, or a gun off the warship itself; Rourke never knew. He only knew the street threw him, and when the ringing thinned he was alone, half-buried in a gutted storefront with a mountain of fresh rubble where the right flank had been. Kaine's transponder still pulsed on his HUD—alive, thank God, but on the far side of the collapse now, shoved back toward Argus with the others. The pincer was broken. The rescue was broken. There was only Rourke, a wall of fire at his back, and ahead of him through the smoke the muzzle-flash of Charles's stand, flickering slower.
On the other side of the rubble, Argus's flank ground to a halt against fire and fresh Obsidian guns. He could not push through. He could not raise Rourke or Charles. He had a city collapsing around two of his people and not one way left to reach either of them.
It was Kaine who found the only door left. Hauled back from the blast, blood sheeting from his temple, he grabbed Argus by the harness and pointed at a half-collapsed service gate behind the wreckage. "There—sir, there. Old maintenance tunnel, runs under the whole sector. Nobody's mapped it since I was a kid down here. It's the only way out that isn't through them." He held Argus's eyes and made himself say the rest. "We can't reach them, sir. We try, we die in this street, and it's for nothing."
Above the boulevard a Razor Wing screamed in on Argus's emergency call, gatling guns shredding the Obsidian line and a missile gutting their camouflaged comms hub. The interference died in a heartbeat—and for the first time in ten minutes the channels came clean.
Rourke heard the static lift. He keyed his comm, caught Argus's voice ragged in his ear ordering him back, and looked at the way behind him: fire, and rubble, and nothing. Then he looked ahead, at the slowing flashes of the last human guns in the street, and his foresight gave him nothing about living—only that cold, certain pull he'd long ago stopped arguing with. There was one direction left to him, and it was forward.
"Well," he said, to no one. He checked his rifle, checked the C5 still on his belt. "Shit."
Then he went to get Charles.
It was the worst hundred meters of a long, hard life. Rourke fought through the gut of the Obsidian line alone—low, fast, every filthy trick a Staff Sergeant hoards across twenty years. Two rounds behind a warrior's knee, his knife into the gap above its collar. He felt the cold press of an enemy Mindreaver before he saw it, that particular wrongness scraping the edge of his mind, something reaching for him the way he reached for the world—and he didn't give it the time to finish. He came through the wall instead of around it, graviton arrestors screaming, had the thing tackled and a charge clamped to its chest before it understood he was in the room. "Not today," he told it, and rolled clear, and the C5 took it and the squad beside it in a thunderclap that blew his path open to the cordon closing on the last position in the street.
He came over the rubble into the eye of it—and there, ringed by Obsidians, bloodied and swinging a scavenged energy blade like a man already at peace with the bill, was Charles.
Charles saw him, and something crossed his ruined face that was almost a laugh. "Rourke? The hell are you doing in here?"
"Worst rescue in Coalition history." Rourke set his back against Charles's and brought his rifle up, and for the first time since the wall came down, neither man was alone. "Figured you looked lonely."
"My team's gone." Charles said it with the flat calm of the already-dead. At his feet sat the hardened case, the warhead's timer dark and waiting. "There's no way out of this one. I've got the nuke. I mean to take every last one of them with it."
"Yeah." Rourke dropped an empty mag and slapped home his last. He could feel the whole shape of the moment, thick and raw the way the bad ones always came to him—no foresight needed for this part. "Yeah, I figured. Set it."
Argus's voice broke through then, clean and desperate, catching them both. "Charles—Rourke—I read you, I've got you, hold position, we're coming back—"
"Negative, Valor Six." Charles, flat and final. But Rourke leaned into the pickup beside him.
"He's right, boss. Don't you dare." Rourke's voice was almost gentle. "We're cut off, we're out of road, and the only warhead on the planet is sitting right here in the middle of more of these things than I can count. You come back in, all you do is die with us and hand them the bomb. Get Harlan and the kid and Kaine home. That's the job now."
"Rourke—" Argus's voice cracked on the name.
"It's alright, Mark." Quieter, the banter gone out of it for a breath. "I saw it coming. I always see it coming. This one I don't mind so much—going out doing something that counts." Then, because he was Rourke to the end: "Tell Harlan he still owes me a C5. And tell Tessa—ah, hell. She'll know."
"Honor Ironclad and Valor both," Charles said into the comm. "Make damn sure these things remember what we cost them." And he bent and woke the timer, and the numbers began to fall.
He cut the line before Argus could answer.
Behind them, in the dark mouth of the tunnel, Kaine dragged Argus the last step out of the street. Argus fought him for half a second—one more look back, toward the place the comms had gone quiet—and then the soldier in him took the weight off the man, and he let the tunnel swallow them. Harlan and Royce came after, and the dark closed over what was left of Fireteam Valor, and behind them the last two Praetorians in New Eridanus squared up to die.
The countdown blinked in both their HUDs. Two minutes.
"How do you want to play it?" Charles asked, rolling a wrecked shoulder, the blade humming in his fist.
"Loud," said Rourke. "Let's make two minutes cost them a year."
The Obsidians closed—a ring of elite warriors snarling in the ruin—and the two Praetorians met them back to back. Charles fought like a man with nothing left to guard but the thing at his feet, the energy blade carving armor and neck and limb in strokes gone economical and final. Rourke fought the way he'd fought his whole life: half a step ahead of the world, his foresight buying the inches that kept the blades off his throat, his rifle barking until it ran dry and then his own blade out and working. For ninety seconds they were better than that worn-down age of the galaxy had any right to field—two men against a horde—and they made the enemy buy every meter of the circle in blood.
But it was arithmetic, and arithmetic doesn't care how well you fight. A claw opened Rourke's side. A red blade caught Charles across the thigh and dropped him to a knee; Rourke hauled him up by the harness and they kept swinging. The seconds fell. One of the elites pressed in close—taller than the rest, its armor worked with intricate marks, narrow yellow eyes burning behind the visor and jagged fangs bared in something almost like a smile. Charles met it blade to blade. Rourke buried his last knife in the gap of its arm. Still it came.
Thirty seconds.
Bloodied, surrounded, both of them barely upright, Rourke spat red into the dust and started to laugh—a real one, the same laugh that had needled Harlan across a hundred miserable camps. "You want to know the worst part?" he said, to Charles, to the closing ring, to the yellow-eyed thing in front of him. "I always figured I'd be the one of us to die old."
Ten seconds.
The yellow-eyed Obsidian leaned in until Rourke could see his own ruined face in its visor. Charles got a hand on the case. Rourke got a hand on Charles's shoulder, and held on.
"Vor'kara, you ugly son of a bitch," Rourke said—the one word of their tongue he'd ever bothered to learn, thrown back in its face like a curse—and spat blood across its visor.
Both men were still smiling when the warhead hit zero.
The ground trembled violently as the tactical nuke detonated, unleashing a blinding flash of white light that obliterated everything within a two-kilometer radius. The shockwave rippled outward, flattening what remained of the overrun urban terrain. Buildings near the epicenter crumbled into clouds of debris, while vehicles and rubble were hurled into the air like toys in a storm. The fireball bloomed upward, casting the ruined city in a hellish orange glow.
In the protective depths of the tunnel, Fireteam Valor braced against the shaking ground, the force of the blast resonating through the earth. Dust rained down from above, and the walls groaned under the stress, but the reinforced structure held firm. Argus pressed his back to the cold stone, gripping his rifle tightly as the roar of the explosion began to fade.
When the tremors subsided, Kaine risked a glance toward the tunnel's exit. "Two klicks," he muttered, staring at the faint glow of the distant fireball. "That’s what, a quarter of the city?"
"Not the whole thing, but enough to wipe out those bastards on the ground," Harlan replied, shaking dust off his shoulders. There was no grin behind it now.
"Ground forces are gone. At least that worked," Argus said, his voice low. He moved to the edge of the tunnel and peered out cautiously.
The aftermath of the explosion was a grim tableau. The two-kilometer radius surrounding the detonation site was a wasteland of molten asphalt and shattered concrete. The Obsidians’ once-overwhelming ground forces had been erased, their bodies reduced to ash and twisted remnants scattered across the devastation. The oppressive growls of the enemy had been replaced by an eerie silence, broken only by the crackle of fires still burning in the ruins.
Yet above it all, the Obsidian warship hovered, unmoved and unscathed. Its immense shadow still loomed over the city, defying the destruction below. The pulse from the blast had barely flickered across its hull, the warship’s alien technology shrugging off the disruption as if it were nothing. Blue arcs of energy coursed briefly along its underside before its systems stabilized, and the faint hum of its engines resumed with chilling steadiness.
And it had not fired. Through the whole battle, through the nuke, the warship had not loosed a single shot at them. It simply hung there, and from its underside long lances of pale light reached down into the earth beneath the city—not weapons, not bombardment, but something patient and deliberate that bored steadily into the ground and did not stop. Whatever the warship had come to New Eridanus for, it was buried beneath the city, and it was not them.

Kaine stared up at it, his voice low. "It’s still up there. It didn’t even swat at us." He shook his head slowly. "What the hell is it doing?"

"I don’t know," Argus admitted. And that was the part that crawled under his skin—not the ship, but the indifference of it. They had burned a city to ash at its feet and it had not so much as turned to look.

For a long moment nobody could find anything to say. Then Harlan, because somebody had to, put words to the plan they'd come here to run. "So we still get a warhead into its belly. Same plan, right?" He looked from Argus to the dark of the tunnel and back, and the answer was already on the lieutenant's face. "...Right?"

"The warhead's gone, Harlan." Argus's voice was a flat, careful thing. "Charles had it. It went up with him." He made himself say the rest. "Rourke too. He fought his way in to them when the rest of us couldn't get within a block. They took it up together." He let the silence carry the weight of it. There was no second warhead, no commandeered transport that meant anything now, no way left to reach that hull and crack it open—and the two men who might have found a way anyway were ash in the same crater as the horde they'd taken down with them.

For a long moment nobody spoke. The mission they had buried friends for was simply gone, and the warship had won the day by refusing to fight for it.

Argus’s communicator crackled—a clean signal now, the interference dead since Razor Wing flattened the enemy comms hub. "Valor Six, this is Gladius Six." Captain Orion’s voice came through clipped and urgent. "Abort and fall back to base. All teams, all sectors—I am recalling everyone to Gladius. Do not engage that warship. Get to the rally point and get home. That’s an order, Mark. Something’s coming, and it’s bigger than New Eridanus."

Argus shut his eyes for half a breath. Then he keyed back. "Copy, Gladius Six. Valor’s coming home."

He turned to what was left of his team. They had heard it. Nobody argued.

"You heard the man," Argus said quietly. "We move. Fast and quiet, back through the tunnel, back to base." He looked up one last time at the thing in the sky, at the pale beams still drilling patiently into the bones of his city. "We don’t get this one. Not today."
