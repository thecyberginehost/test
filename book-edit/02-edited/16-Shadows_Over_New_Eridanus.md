Chapter 16: Shadows Over New Eridanus
Date: December 17, 2888
Time: 0530, Galactic Standard Time (GTC)
Location: Heart of New Eridanus
In the war-torn heart of New Eridanus, the silence of the early morning was deceptive. Fireteam Valor, alongside Fireteam Ironclad and their attachments, moved with ghost-like precision through the rubble. Stealth drones hummed softly overhead, their sensors sweeping the path ahead to ensure that the route was clear of enemy patrols. The air was thick with the scent of ionized particles and smoldering debris.
Kaine, having taken on the role of the team’s primary scout since Tessa's injury and subsequent evacuation, was a blur among the shadows. His movements were fluid and assured as he darted from one building to another, climbing to vantage points that overlooked their planned route. From these heights, he relayed crucial information back to the teams—silent gestures indicating clear paths or potential hazards.
His role had never been more critical. With Tessa gone, much depended on his keen observation skills and his ability to stay one step ahead of the Obsidian forces. His headset buzzed quietly, a secure line to the team leaders, where he whispered concise updates.
"Three hostiles on the road, two blocks north, moving east," Kaine reported, his voice a low murmur. His eyes never stopped scanning the horizon, the digital overlay in his visor highlighting points of interest.
Below, Lieutenant Argus, leading Fireteam Valor, nodded slightly at Kaine’s report. He turned to his team, his hand signals crisp in the dim light. Argus raised up a fist, signaling the teams to hold. They would let the hostiles pass to maintain the element of surprise.
In the midst of this silent communication, the group's pace was steady but cautious. Every corner they turned, every alley they crossed was a potential ambush point. The stealth drones played a crucial part in their strategy, not just as lookouts but as distractions if needed. Their route had been meticulously planned to avoid enemy concentrations, utilizing data collected over days of continuous drone surveillance.
As dawn began to break, the grey light cast long shadows across the debris, giving the city a spectral appearance. Fireteam Valor and their allies were shadows themselves, melding into the environment, becoming part of the city’s broken landscape as they moved toward their objective.
As the teams progress stealthily toward their objective to commandeer an enemy transport ship, their reliance on a Neuro-Morphic Interface Pad, or simply NMIP, is crucial. This device provides instantaneous operational mastery of any vehicle it interfaces with, excluding language decryption.
Amidst their silent trek, Kaine spots an Obsidian scout who has detected their presence. The enemy reaches for his communicator, but Kaine swiftly intervenes by lobbing a Whisper Grenade—courtesy of Wraith. This stealth grenade releases a localized electromagnetic pulse designed to disable electronic devices and incapacitate organic beings without noise.
The grenade lands silently, unleashing a subdued flash near the scout. As the Obsidian collapses, Kaine quickly approaches to confirm the takedown. He unsheathes his combat knife, its blade black and honed to a deadly precision. Kneeling beside the fallen enemy, he carefully places his hand over the scout's mouth to stifle any sound. With a precise and quick thrust, he punctures the alien's throat, directly through the center. He twists the knife and slices outward, a dark blue mist following the momentum. Dark blue blood pours out of the wound aggressively as the alien convulses.
The Obsidian's eyes widen, and a gurgling whisper escapes as its life fades. Kaine keeps his hand firmly over the alien's mouth, silencing its dying breaths. Withdrawing his knife, he notices the blade smeared with the alien's blood, an eerie hue unlike any human blood. Kaine wipes the blade clean against the Obsidian scout's armored suit, the rough material effectively scraping off the alien residue.
With the area secured and the threat permanently neutralized, Kaine signals the all-clear to Fireteam Valor. Their mission proceeds with renewed caution, every member aware of the stakes should they be discovered.
As the light of dawn filters through the smoke-laden skies above New Eridanus, the tension among the ranks of Fireteam Valor and their allies is palpable. The objective, confirmed by the stealth drones—a series of fast fighters and heavy transports—is located roughly five-hundred meters away, embedded deep within a heavily fortified Obsidian encampment.
Lieutenant Argus surveys the terrain through his tactical visor, his voice crackling over the comms as he radios in the coordinating instructions. "Ironclad Six, this is Valor Six. Take your team, the OGDF and Orbital Infantry Troopers west to flank. Stealth drones have visuals on the objective. We'll approach from the north and meet you on the other side."
Acknowledging the command, Lieutenant Charles, leading Fireteam Ironclad, confirms with a terse nod and a quick, "Copy that, Valor Six. Moving out." His team moves with precision, spreading out to cover more ground and encircle the target.

At the center of Ironclad's formation, secured in a hardened case, rides the package—the single tactical warhead Valor pulled from the Delta bunker, the Coalition's one and only shot at gutting the warship from within. Ironclad carries it to the objective; Valor clears the road. That is the plan.
As both teams begin their trek through the ravaged cityscape, the quiet is abruptly shattered. Without warning, elite Obsidian forces clad in dark charcoal armor with menacing purple hues emerge from hidden positions. The morning silence is broken by the hum of red energy swords activating and the distinct whine of energy-based projectiles whizzing through the air.
These were not the typical Obsidian foot soldiers that Fireteam Valor had encountered before. These enemies moved with a terrifying coordination and lethal efficiency, wielding advanced weaponry that pinned down the Praetorians with suppressive fire.
Caught in the sudden onslaught, Fireteam Ironclad scrambles for cover with their OGDF and OI counterparts, but the environment offers little protection. As they try to return fire and maneuver, a well-placed energy blast shears through a crumbling wall, effectively cutting off Lieutenant Charles and his team from their allies.
Isolated and pinned, Charles attempts to rally his team, shouting commands over the roar of continuous energy fire. "Get security up! Defensive positions!" He barks orders, trying to make sense of their rapidly deteriorating situation. They return fire, but their standard issue weapons hardly match the advanced tech and skill of their adversaries.
Lieutenant Charles quickly realizes the gravity of their situation; they are trapped with no immediate way out and no feasible path to rejoin Fireteam Valor. The communication lines crackle with interference, further isolating them as they face an increasingly desperate scenario.
Argus struggles to communicate with Charles. His communicator crackles with static, the signal intermittently warbling under what he suspects is an electronic countermeasure deployed by the Obsidians.
Clutching the device, Argus attempts another call to Charles, desperate to coordinate their scattered teams. Each attempt is met with more static, his frustration building with the unreliability of their connection. He glances around the desolate landscape, his team taking cover among the ruins, alert for any sign of enemy movement.
Meanwhile, Kaine, stationed high in the remnants of an old communications tower, oversees the unfolding scenario. Through his tactical visor, the city below him is laid out in stark detail against the grey light of dawn, every movement highlighted, every threat tagged and tracked. His strategic vantage point provides him with a broad view, perfect for guiding his team through the treacherous urban terrain.
With a clear line of sight to the pathway ahead, Kaine identifies an optimal escape route—an old tunnel entrance cleverly concealed by nature and debris. It's a place from his youth, known only to him and a handful of others, its entrance hidden from plain view and absent from any official map. A perfect hidden passageway that leads directly to the city’s underbelly.
Kaine quickly switches to Argus’ direct line-of-sight channel to avoid the interference, his voice calm but urgent as he transmits the coordinates to Argus. "Valor, reroute to grid Echo-Five, Tango-3. Use the service gate behind the collapsed sector.
As Fireteam Valor adjusts their course, the sudden crack of a high-energy rifle pierces the morning stillness. An Obsidian scout, utilizing stealth technology, deciphers their movement and opens fire. The shot, a vivid streak of blue plasma, zips dangerously close to Harlan, who ducks behind the remnants of a shattered wall.
Reacting swiftly, Harlan checks the ammo drum of his SHR-3663R Portable Chain Gun. Today, the circumstances call for his custom ammunition—white phosphorus-tipped rounds, reserved for dire situations. He slides the drum into place, the rounds entering the chamber with a satisfying snap.
As the Obsidian scout repositions for another shot, Harlan braces The Shredder against his shoulder, takes aim, and unleashes hell. The heavy machine gun roars to life, spitting death with each pull of the trigger. The white phosphorus rounds find their mark, igniting upon impact. The scout screams as the chemical burns through his armor and flesh, a sickening smell of burning metal and flesh filling the air.
"What the hell did you put in that Shredder?" Royce yells, ducking instinctively as he watches the horrific effect of the rounds.
"Best not to ask questions," Harlan replies, his voice even amid the chaos, eyes scanning for more threats as they continue their tactical retreat.
Suddenly, Rourke senses a dangerous familiarity—a presence that his Mindreaving abilities identify as another Mindreaver. Without hesitation, he channels his psychic energy, concentrating on locating the hidden enemy. His senses lead him to a nearby building. With a burst of his graviton arrestors, he charges, slamming through the wall with kinetic force that sends debris flying.
On the other side, the Obsidian Mindreaver is momentarily stunned by Rourke’s explosive entry. Before the enemy can recover, Rourke tackles him to the ground. In a fluid motion, he pulls a C5 charge from his belt and attaches it to the Obsidian’s leg, while stomping down on it’s opposite leg, the sound of a crack and horrifying, guttural scream fill the air.
"Clear out!" Rourke shouts, sprinting away from the imminent explosion.
The charge detonates with a deafening boom, obliterating the Obsidian and sending shockwaves through the nearby structures. Harlan, watching the scene unfold, shouts over the ringing in his ears, "You owe me a C5 charge, Rourke!"
"Put it on my tab," Rourke replies with a grin, brushing off his armor as he rejoins the team, the casual banter masking the severity of their situation.
As the team regroups and pushes toward the hidden tunnel entrance, their calm demeanor during the intense firefight and strategic retreat underscores their seasoned experience in combat, setting the stage for their harrowing journey through the city's underground passages.
With their path to the hidden tunnel now clear, Lieutenant Argus quickly checks his communicator once more. His previous attempts to reach Ironclad Six were fraught with static and frustration, but now, with the Obsidian scout threat temporarily neutralized, there was a momentary pause in the electronic interference. Taking advantage of the lull, Argus makes a critical decision. He can't reach Ironclad Six directly, but he can call in air support.
Holding the communicator tightly, he dials in the frequency for Razor Wing support. "Razor Wing Command, this is Valor Six. Requesting immediate close air support at grid coordinates Juliet-Nine, Kilo-Four. Ironclad Six is pinned down and unable to advance. Authorization code Delta-Foxtrot-Niner-Zero. Execute tight strafe runs."
"Valor Six, Razor Wing One. Acknowledging receipt. Coordinates locked. Engaging in thirty seconds.”
With the call successfully transmitted, Argus turns back to his team, signaling them to quicken their pace towards the tunnel. The mission to sabotage the hovering Obsidian warship looms heavily over them, a ticking clock that none can ignore. They needed to commandeer the Obsidian transports hidden within the city’s industrial sector—a daring plan that would give them a fighting chance to bring down the warship.
Meanwhile, Razor Wing One, roars into the airspace above Ironclad Six’s last known coordinates. As he crosses into the area, his communicator begins to crackle with static, a clear sign that the Obsidian's electronic countermeasures were still active. Despite the disruption, his targeting systems remain functional, distinguishing friend from foe with high precision—a testament to the Galactic Forge Coalition’s engineering.
The Razor Wing swoops low, the twin engines of Razor Wing One screaming through the air. He triggers the first strafe run, releasing a barrage of high-caliber rounds from the underwing gatling guns. The rounds tear through the Obsidian lines, mowing down enemy after enemy as he skillfully maneuvers the aircraft through the onslaught of anti-aircraft fire.
On his second pass, the Razor Wing locks onto a heavier target—an Obsidian communications hub, camouflaged within the city ruins. He fires a guided missile, watching as it streaks across the sky and slams into the target. The explosion is massive, sending shockwaves through the area, and with it, the static interference abruptly ceases.
Back on the ground, Argus hears the sudden clarity in his earpiece. "Ironclad Six, do you copy? This is Valor Six. Razor Wing One has engaged. Advance to your secondary objective. We are proceeding to commandeer the Obsidian transports. Meet at the rally point uploaded to your tactical display ASAP."
With the path now clear and communications restored, Fireteam Valor presses forward. The weight of their task hangs over them, but the successful air support lifts their spirits. They slip through the city's underbelly via the old tunnel network, moving swiftly and silently, unseen by the enemy forces still reeling from the Razor Wing's devastating attack.
Amidst the chaos, the distressed voice of Ironclad Six, Lieutenant Charles, crackled through the communicator, each word punctuated by gunfire and the guttural shouts of Obsidian forces. The situation was dire, the sound of his last round firing echoed a grim reality.
"Damn. Out of ammo," Charles gasped, the weight of his situation heavy in his tone. "Valor Six, it’s only me, now. All units leave the area, Razor Wings included. I’m detonating our nuke."
Before Argus could digest the grave news, he quickly interjected, determination lacing his voice. "Don't you do it, Ironclad Six. We need you. We can get to you!"
"Negative, Valor Six," Charles replied, his voice strained under the weight of command. "We're cut off and my team's gone. There's no getting back to you—and I'll be damned if I let these things crack open a live nuke and learn how we make them. Get your men out, Argus. Don't waste a single one of them trying to drag a dead man home."
Static crackled through the comms, followed by a sharp intake of breath from Argus. "We’ll find a way! There’s always a way!"
"Shut it, Argus!" Charles snapped, his voice hardening. "I’m not walking out of this, and neither are these Obsidians—I'm taking every last one of them with me. You want to honor Ironclad? Get our people home, and make damn sure these things remember what we cost them."
He cut the line before Argus could respond, tossing the communications unit aside. The silence felt heavier than the chaos around him. Charles’s chest heaved as he surveyed the battlefield. He was the last one standing.
The nuke’s countdown timer blinked steadily in his HUD. Two minutes.
He grabbed the energy blade from a fallen Obsidian and activated it with a sharp hum. "Come on, you fuckers." he muttered, gripping the weapon tight. "Let’s dance."
The Obsidians closed in, a horde of their elite warriors forming a circle around him. Their guttural snarls echoed off the crumbling ruins. Charles lunged first, the energy blade slashing through the nearest enemy’s chest before it could react, its body falling in a heap. He pivoted, catching another mid-charge, the blade carving through its exposed neck, the sound of the wound cauterizing as it carved its path.
The rest of the Obsidian charged in overwhelming force.
One of them landed a strike, its claws raking across Charles’s side, but he barely flinched. He reversed the blade and drove it backward, impaling the creature through its abdomen. Another swung at him, a massive red energy sword narrowly missing his head as he ducked and rolled. He came up swinging, cleaving the attacker’s arm clean off before finishing it with a downward thrust through the chest.
The seconds ticked down. Charles’s armor was scorched, his muscles screaming with every movement, but he didn’t stop. His blade flashed in the chaos, the Obsidians falling one after another. Yet, for every enemy he killed, two more seemed to take their place.
An energy projectile caught him in the shoulder, spinning him to the ground as his armor melted down to his skin, a wave of burning pain almost sending him into shock. He groaned, dragging himself to his feet just as another enemy charged with a massive blade. Charles sidestepped, twisting to avoid the blow, then plunged his energy blade into the alien’s back.
Sixty seconds.
His vision blurred as blood dripped into his eyes. His helmet had been torn away, leaving his face exposed to the biting wind and smoke. He could smell his burnt skin now. He wiped his forehead with a trembling hand, muttering to himself. "Keep going. Just keep going."
Another Obsidian barreled into him, knocking him into a pile of rubble. Charles roared, driving his blade into the creature’s side. It shrieked, collapsing on top of him. He shoved its lifeless body aside, only to find three more advancing on his position.
"Goddamnit," he spat, pulling a sidearm from his hip. The weapon barked, dropping two of them before the third ripped it from his hand. Charles swung wildly with the blade in his other hand, catching it across the face and sending it sprawling.
Thirty seconds.
The Obsidians hesitated now; their guttural voices low as they recognized his defiance. Bloodied, battered, and surrounded, Charles stood tall, his grip steady on the energy blade. "What’s the matter?" he growled, his voice dripping with venom. "Come on, you ugly sons of bitches! You wanted this!"
Charles roared as he charged to the closest enemy, swinging with every ounce of strength he had left. The blade carved through armor, flesh and bone as he fought like a man already dead. Claws raked his body, plasma energy seared his skin, but he kept moving, cutting down every alien in reach. One finally knocked the blade from his hand, and another drove an energy dagger into his side as he blocked the other incoming energy blade by reaching up to grab the enemy’s wrist. He staggered but refused to fall.
Ten seconds.
The Obsidian leaned in close to his face; he could see its narrow, yellow eyes through its helmet visor. He could see the alien’s jagged fangs as it smiled. Charles spat blood in the Obsidian’s face as it flinched and made an inaudible, guttural sound.
Meanwhile, Kaine, aware of the impending detonation, rushed into the protective embrace of the tunnel with the rest of Fireteam Valor. The structure shook as the countdown neared zero, the anticipation of destruction palpable in the air.
Charles smiled as the nuke finally detonated.
The ground trembled violently as the tactical nuke detonated, unleashing a blinding flash of white light that obliterated everything within a two-kilometer radius. The shockwave rippled outward, flattening what remained of the overrun urban terrain. Buildings near the epicenter crumbled into clouds of debris, while vehicles and rubble were hurled into the air like toys in a storm. The fireball bloomed upward, casting the ruined city in a hellish orange glow.
In the protective depths of the tunnel, Fireteam Valor braced against the shaking ground, the force of the blast resonating through the earth. Dust rained down from above, and the walls groaned under the stress, but the reinforced structure held firm. Argus pressed his back to the cold stone, gripping his rifle tightly as the roar of the explosion began to fade.
When the tremors subsided, Kaine risked a glance toward the tunnel's exit. "Two klicks," he muttered, staring at the faint glow of the distant fireball. "That’s what, a quarter of the city?"
"Not the whole thing, but enough to wipe out those bastards on the ground," Rourke replied, shaking dust off his shoulders.
"Ground forces are gone. At least that worked," Argus said, his voice low. He moved to the edge of the tunnel and peered out cautiously.
The aftermath of the explosion was a grim tableau. The two-kilometer radius surrounding the detonation site was a wasteland of molten asphalt and shattered concrete. The Obsidians’ once-overwhelming ground forces had been erased, their bodies reduced to ash and twisted remnants scattered across the devastation. The oppressive growls of the enemy had been replaced by an eerie silence, broken only by the crackle of fires still burning in the ruins.
Yet above it all, the Obsidian warship hovered, unmoved and unscathed. Its immense shadow still loomed over the city, defying the destruction below. The electromagnetic pulse from the nuke had barely flickered across its hull, the warship’s alien technology shrugging off the disruption as if it were nothing. Blue arcs of energy coursed briefly along its underside before its systems stabilized, and the faint hum of its engines resumed with chilling steadiness.
And it had not fired. Through the whole battle, through the nuke, the warship had not loosed a single shot at them. It simply hung there, and from its underside long lances of pale light reached down into the earth beneath the city—not weapons, not bombardment, but something patient and deliberate that bored steadily into the ground and did not stop. Whatever the warship had come to New Eridanus for, it was buried beneath the city, and it was not them.

Kaine stared up at it, his voice low. "It’s still up there. It didn’t even swat at us." He shook his head slowly. "What the hell is it doing?"

"I don’t know," Argus admitted. And that was the part that crawled under his skin—not the ship, but the indifference of it. They had burned a city to ash at its feet and it had not so much as turned to look.

Rourke spat into the dust. "So we put a warhead in its belly and find out. Same plan." He glanced at Argus, and then he saw it land on the lieutenant’s face. "...Argus."

"The nuke was Charles’s." Argus’s jaw tightened. "Ironclad carried the only one we had. It went up with him." He let the silence answer the rest. There was no second warhead, no commandeered transport that meant anything now, no way left to reach that hull and crack it open. The one weapon that could have ended the thing had died taking a horde of Obsidians to hell with it—and the ship that killing was meant for hung above them, untouched and unbothered.

For a long moment nobody spoke. The mission they had buried friends for was simply gone, and the warship had won the day by refusing to fight for it.

Argus’s communicator crackled—a clean signal now, the interference dead since Razor Wing flattened the enemy comms hub. "Valor Six, this is Gladius Six." Captain Orion’s voice came through clipped and urgent. "Abort and fall back to base. All teams, all sectors—I am recalling everyone to Gladius. Do not engage that warship. Get to the rally point and get home. That’s an order, Mark. Something’s coming, and it’s bigger than New Eridanus."

Argus shut his eyes for half a breath. Then he keyed back. "Copy, Gladius Six. Valor’s coming home."

He turned to what was left of his team. They had heard it. Nobody argued.

"You heard the man," Argus said quietly. "We move. Fast and quiet, back through the tunnel, back to base." He looked up one last time at the thing in the sky, at the pale beams still drilling patiently into the bones of his city. "We don’t get this one. Not today."

Fireteam Valor melted back into the dark of the tunnel, leaving the dead behind them—Ironclad’s and their own—and above the ruin of New Eridanus the Obsidian warship hung exactly as it had, unhurried and uncaring, lances of light sinking into the earth as it reached for whatever it had come so far to take.
