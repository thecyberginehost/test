Chapter 15 – The Artifact
Date: December 16, 2888
Time: 2148 Galactic Standard Time (GTC)
Location: Stealthed Pegasus Transport – Over the drop zone
As the transport neared the peak of their ascent, Strickland's command cut through the hum of the engines, crisp and authoritative. "Get to your pods, men. We're on the clock."
One by one, the members of Death Platoon made their way to the singular pods vertically lined in the cargo bay, the anticipation building with each step. Dutch, with Old Faithful in tow, struggled momentarily to maneuver both his bulky frame and the heavy machine gun into the confined space of his pod. With a grunt of determination, he finally settled in, the weapon cradled awkwardly against his side, a testament to his unwillingness to part with it, even for a moment.
Now securely encased in their pods, the team awaited the final go-ahead. Strickland's voice crackled over the radio, "Gladius Six, Viper Platoon is ready for drop."
"You're clear to drop," Orion's voice came back, steady and sure.
Strickland turned his focus back to his team, the tone of his voice shifting to one of unwavering professionalism. "Thirty seconds to drop, men. Brace for downward thrust."
The silence that followed was palpable, each member mentally preparing for the adrenaline-fueled descent. Then, Strickland began the countdown, "Five, four, three, two, one..."
With a deep, resonating boom, the pods were released, catapulting away from the safety of the transport. The stealth technology of the Pegasus briefly flickered as each pod exited, momentarily exposing them to the void before vanishing against the backdrop of space.
The descent was nothing short of harrowing. As the pods hurtled toward the planet, the initial calm of space gave way to the violent buffeting of atmospheric entry. The sleek design of the pods, optimized for stealth and speed, cut through the air, their surfaces heating up as they encountered atmospheric friction. Inside, the members of Death Platoon were jostled relentlessly, the turbulence testing the limits of both man and machine.
Despite the chaos, the pods' simple yet effective concealment measures paid off. From below, the swift passage of the pods was virtually undetectable, blending seamlessly with the night sky. The Obsidian aircraft, patrolling diligently, remained blissfully unaware of the intruders descending into their territory.
Through the small, reinforced viewports of their pods, the team could make out the warship still hovering over New Eridanus and other enemy aircraft in the distance. The sight was a stark reminder of the formidable enemy they faced, yet the obscurity afforded by their pods offered a fleeting sense of advantage.
As they neared the surface, the pod-mounted Graviton Arrestors activated, fighting against the pull of gravity to slow their descent. The pods shook violently, the sound of the thrusters and the whine of the arrestors filling the cramped space. The exterior of the pods glowed with residual heat, casting an eerie light within. Each member of the platoon, isolated in their pod, watched the digital display count down the meters to impact.
The final moments before landing were filled with anticipation. The arrestors, though not as refined as those in Praetorian armor, managed to mitigate the worst of the impact. The landing, while far from graceful, was effective. One by one, the pods touched down on the surface of their drop zone, roughly five kilometers northeast of New Eridanus, their arrival cloaked in secrecy thanks to the night and their pods' concealment technology.
As the dust settled and the sounds of their harsh landings faded into the night, Death Platoon was ready. Hidden from the eyes of the Obsidian patrols, they prepared to emerge from their pods, the mission ahead clear in their minds. The professionalism and precision that defined them were now their most valuable assets, as they stepped into the heart of enemy territory, unseen and undeterred.
Death Platoon exited their pods, quickly regrouping under the cover of darkness. Their first task was to secure their additional weaponry and equipment, stored in a separate, larger pod designed specifically for their arsenal. With practiced efficiency, they opened the weapons pod, distributing its contents among themselves, ensuring each member was fully equipped for the mission ahead.
After equipping themselves with the weapons and gear from the supply pod, Strickland turned his attention back to the pods that had carried them and their equipment to the surface. With a practiced motion, he activated the silent self-destruct mechanisms embedded within each pod. This action was not taken lightly, but it was a necessary precaution to ensure that no trace of their landing, or their mission, would be left behind for the Obsidian forces to discover. The technology within these pods, though primarily focused on stealth and minimal impact, included a failsafe designed to obliterate the pods without leaving a trace. This would prevent the enemy from reverse-engineering any of their advanced technology or using the pods' arrival points to track their movements.
The silent self-destruct mechanisms were designed to engage a rapid disintegration process, consuming the pods with a chemical reaction that left no debris or detectable signatures. The process was so efficient that within minutes, the only evidence of the pods' existence would be the barest distortions in the soil, soon to be erased by the planet's natural elements.
This precaution underscored the clandestine nature of Death Platoon's operations. Stealth was not just a part of their approach; it was integral to their survival and the success of their mission. By erasing their entry points, Strickland ensured that their presence on New Eridanus remained a shadow, giving them the element of surprise against an adversary notorious for its vigilance and technological prowess.
Strickland signaled for the team to move out. The cave system that would lead them beneath New Eridanus and closer to their objective awaited, and with it, the heart of their mission. The destruction of the pods was a final testament to their resolve: leave no trace, take no prisoners, and let nothing stand between them and their objective.
As Death Platoon advanced under Trident’s night sky, their movements were ghostly silent, guided by the faint glow of the planet’s twin moons. Strickland, with a silent command of his hand, signaled his team forward toward the cave system that housed their objective—an ancient artifact of immeasurable power.
An enemy patrol ahead, clearly tasked with securing the area around the cave, was oblivious to the deadly shadows approaching. The team deployed a stealth drone, which mimicked the Obsidian language, creating a distraction. The garbled communications it emitted drew the guards' attention, making them prime targets for a silent assault.
Dutch, despite his usual preference for a direct confrontation, embraced the stealth required for the mission. As one of the guards separated from his group, lured by the drone's sounds, Dutch positioned himself behind a large boulder. The guard approached, his weapon held loosely, confident in the solitude of his surroundings. In a swift motion, Dutch reached out, his large hand covering the guard’s mouth. With the other arm, he executed a chokehold. There was a brief, muffled struggle as the guard's body went limp, deprived of oxygen. With the alien showing signs of unconsciousness, Dutch flexed his bicep even more so, then swiftly snapped its neck. Dutch gently lowered it to the ground, ensuring the enemy’s armor did not clank against the rocks.
Simultaneously, Strickland approached another guard from behind. Using a garrote wire, he looped it swiftly around the guard's neck. With a quick pull, the wire tightened, cutting off the guard's airway. The guard's eyes widened in panic, his hands clawing at the wire ineffectually. Within seconds, he slumped to the ground, Strickland easing his descent to prevent any noise.
Another guard, drawn away by the drone's chirping, walked directly into an ambush. As he neared the source, Axe stepped silently from the shadows, a combat knife in hand. The knife was plunged deep into the side of the guard’s neck, severing arteries with clinical precision. Blood flowed silently over the blade, absorbed by the thirsty ground, as the guard was eased down without a sound.
With the patrol neutralized and the bodies concealed under the sparse underbrush, Strickland motioned for the team to proceed towards the cave. The stealth drone, having served its purpose, was retrieved quietly.
The entrance to the cave loomed before them, dark and foreboding. The team entered, the air inside cool and damp, a natural labyrinth of stone that could echo any misplaced step. They advanced with meticulous care, guided by Strickland's hand signals—each motion measured, each breath held a little too long.
Their approach to the artifact was as silent as their kills, the darkness of the cave swallowing any lingering echoes of their passage. Each step deeper into the cave brought them closer to the artifact, and to the potential to change the balance of power in the galaxy. Their initial engagement had been flawlessly lethal, a testament to their deadly expertise and silent coordination.
As Death Platoon approached the cave entrance under the concealment of night, Strickland signaled for a pause, his hand raised in a tight fist. The team instinctively crouched low, blending further into the shadowy rocks that dotted the landscape.
"Deploy the drones," Strickland whispered into his comm unit, his voice barely audible. In response, two small stealth drones, sleek and nearly silent, detached from the back of the platoon's equipment carrier. Their tiny rotors whirred as they lifted into the air, quickly becoming invisible against the dark sky except for the faint red glow of their navigation lights, which were visible only to the infrared cameras each team member had equipped.
As the drones approached the cave entrance, Strickland pulled out his datapad, activating the screen to view the live feed from the drones' cameras. The screen flickered for a moment before displaying the grainy outlines of the cave's rugged mouth. "Initiate scan," he commanded softly.
The automated intelligence system integrated within the drone responded instantly, processing the video feed to map the cave's entrance and the first twenty meters of its interior. The drones moved stealthily, their cameras panning across the jagged rocks and deeper into the shadows. The feed on Strickland’s datapad showed a clear path initially, the intelligence system highlighting potential hazards and plotting a tentative route forward.
As the drones ventured deeper, the screen suddenly showed a figure stepping out from a side passage—a lone Obsidian soldier, noticeably shorter than others they had encountered. The soldier paused, seeming to sense something amiss. It turned directly toward the drone, its eyes narrowing.
Without hesitation, Strickland tapped a command on his datapad. From a side compartment, one of the drones silently launched a dart—an engineered neurotoxin designed to incapacitate quickly. The dart struck the Obsidian soldier in the neck, and he staggered back, clutching at the tiny projectile before collapsing to the ground, motionless.
"Clear," Strickland announced after a tense few seconds, the drones confirming no further immediate threats. He gestured to the team, and they advanced into the cave, their steps measured and silent.
As they entered the shadowy expanse, the drones continued their reconnaissance, flying deeper into the network of tunnels. Strickland kept one eye on the datapad, watching the map expand as the automated intelligence system processed the incoming data. Each chamber and passage were carefully logged, potential threats marked, and escape routes outlined.
The drones would be crucial in navigating the labyrinthine cave system, their ongoing scans providing a tactical advantage in the unfamiliar and potentially hostile environment. With every meter they covered, Death Platoon moved closer to uncovering the secrets hidden deep within the cave, guided by the silent sentinels that flew ahead.
Once Death Platoon was completely inside the cave, Lieutenant Strickland signaled his team to form a security perimeter in the dead end of a side passage the drones located near the cave’s entrance. The spot provided strategic cover and an excellent rally point for the team to regroup and reassess. Each member of Death Platoon took their position, weapons at the ready, eyes scanning the dark crevices of the cave for any sign of movement.
Strickland then moved to a slightly secluded spot, crouching down to pull out his encrypted communicator. He opened a direct, encrypted line to Captain Orion.
"Gladius Six, this is Viper Six, over."
The line crackled slightly before Orion's voice came through, clear and authoritative. "Viper Six, this is Gladius Six. Status?"
"Gladius Six, we have successfully navigated to the first rally point. Coordinates to follow. Proceeding with mission parameters. Over."
"Copy that, Viper Six. Be advised, the central chamber of the cave system contains an undisclosed GRID bunker. Coordinates are being sent to your datapad now. Secure the artifact and proceed to the bunker for exfiltration."
Strickland nodded, even though Orion couldn’t see him, acknowledging the new information. "Understood, Gladius Six."
Before ending the transmission, Orion added, "Viper Six, seal the cave entrance. Cut off any potential pursuit or escape routes for the Obsidians."
"Roger that, Gladius Six. Working. Viper Six out." Strickland switched off the communicator and looked up to his team, relaying the new orders. A ripple of sly smirks passed through the team. Dutch, in particular, grinned broadly, his delight barely contained.
“Time to ensure our guests don’t leave the party early.” Dutch commented, his voice low but filled with glee.
The rest of the team shared Dutch’s sentiment, appreciating the strategic advantage this action provided. There was a shared satisfaction in knowing their enemies wouldn’t have an easy way out. Strickland, acknowledging the team's morale boost, began directing the setup for the explosives that would seal the cave entrance.
Dutch reached into his pack with a deliberate motion, pulling out a few C5 charges. As he inspected each one, making sure they were ready for deployment, he couldn't suppress a grin. "Nothing like a good old-fashioned cave in," he chuckled, handling the explosives with the ease of long familiarity.
He turned his attention to his weapon, Old Faithful, cradling it in his arms like a cherished companion. Gently, he ran his hand along the barrel, a silent moment of appreciation for the countless battles they had seen together.
Strickland, watching this brief display of attachment, remarked with a hint of amusement in his tone, "Always the sentimental type, Dutch. But don't worry, after we retrieve the artifact, there's no reason for stealth. We’re blowing our way out."
Dutch's smile widened at Strickland’s words, his eyes lighting up with anticipation. "Oh, yeah!" he said, his voice a low growl of excitement. "Old Faithful is waitin’ for some action. She gets cranky when she’s kept quiet too long."
His comment drew a few chuckles from the team, easing the tension as they prepared for the next phase of their mission.
Lieutenant Strickland surveyed his team, each member meticulously preparing for the impending assault, deeper into the cave system. His gaze lingered on each individual, reflecting on the relentless, unwavering spirit they brought to every mission. The steely resolve in their eyes, the readiness to leap into action—it reassured him that he couldn’t have chosen a better team.
They were warriors through and through, always eager for the next engagement. Strickland admired their relentless pursuit of the mission objectives, their adherence to orders without hesitation, regardless of the moral complexities such commands might entail. This was a team that thrived under pressure, that found camaraderie in the face of danger, and that respected the chain of command with a nearly zealous fervor.
He loved their professionalism, their almost uncanny ability to remain undetected until the moment of explosive action, and their dark humor that kept the grim realities of their work at bay. As he pondered this, his lips curved into a proud smile, an unspoken acknowledgment of their shared bond and achievements.
Just then, Dutch interrupted his reverie, "Cover!" His voice was sharp, snapping Strickland back to the present. Each team member instinctively ducked and shielded themselves as Dutch planted the C5 charges at the cave entrance.
With a quick flick of the detonator, the charges exploded in a controlled but powerful blast. Rocks and debris thundered down, effectively sealing the cave entrance with a rumbling finality that echoed through the cavernous passages.
As the dust settled, Dutch glanced around at the team, a mischievous glint in his eyes. "Think they heard that?" he quipped, his voice tinged with humor, referring to any Obsidians who might still be lurking nearby.
The team, momentarily relaxed in the wake of their successful barricade, allowed themselves a brief chuckle before refocusing on the mission ahead. With the entrance now sealed, they were cut off from any immediate pursuit or escape—it was time to delve deeper and confront whatever lay in the heart of the cave system.
Lieutenant Strickland surveyed the now-sealed entrance briefly, ensuring no debris posed a risk of shifting, before turning back to face his team. He tapped the side of his helmet, activating his own gear with a knowing smile. "Now everyone's favorite part," he announced, his voice echoing slightly off the cave walls, "activate enhanced night vision. Let's move out."
One by one, the members of Death Platoon engaged the night vision systems integrated into their helmets. This was no ordinary night vision. The technology embedded within their helmets' smart systems operated beyond the traditional light amplification model. Instead, it utilized advanced quantum imaging technology that detected anomalies in the local quantum field—a technique that allowed them to see in complete darkness without any light source. This sophisticated system could essentially 'feel' the environment, creating a visual representation based on the disturbance of quantum particles caused by objects in the vicinity.
This cutting-edge capability was a reward for the numerous covert operations the platoon had conducted on GRID’s behalf. It was a level of technology not even the elite Praetorians were privy to, giving Strickland and his team a significant tactical advantage in environments like this.
"Move out," Strickland commanded, his tone now strictly business as he led the team deeper into the cave. They moved in a tight formation, each step deliberate, their path revealed through the ghostly greenish hue of their visors. The cave walls loomed large around them, ancient and imposing, their secrets now laid bare under the gaze of their advanced optics.
As they delved further into the darkness, the air grew cooler, and the passage narrowed. The steady drip of water in the distance was almost comforting in the oppressive silence. Each member of the team was hyper-alert, their senses tuned to pick up any sign of danger, now that they could see without light.
With the entrance securely blocked, their only way was forward, deeper into the cave system's heart. Strickland’s confidence in his team was unshakable; they were more than just soldiers—they were a finely tuned unit equipped with the best technology GRID could offer, ready to face whatever lay ahead in the depths of New Eridanus’s underground cave systems.
