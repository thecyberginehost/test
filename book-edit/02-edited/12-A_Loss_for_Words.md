Chapter 12 – A Loss for Words
Date: December 16, 2888
Time: 1733 Galactic Standard Time (GTC)
Location: Outskirts of New Eridanus
Fireteam Valor, along with their OGDF attachment, cautiously navigated through the suburban outskirts of New Eridanus. The once vibrant neighborhood was now a grim mosaic of destruction. Rubble lay scattered where homes and buildings once stood.
Lieutenant Argus led the group, each step taking them closer to what used to be his neighborhood. The further they went, the more his unease grew, palpable to those around him. The landscape was littered with empty alien drop pods, eerie markers of the invasion that had taken place.
Every step towards his family's residence tightened the knot in Argus's stomach, his mind racing with all the possibilities of what he might find. The thought that his home might lie in ruins, like the rest of the neighborhood, weighed heavily on him. The silence of the team was a heavy cloak, everyone lost in their own thoughts about the devastation surrounding them and what it meant for their mission.
As they edged closer into the heart of Lieutenant Argus's neighborhood, the air was punctuated by an unsettling quiet. Suddenly, Argus froze, a distant noise catching his attention. With a practiced ease, he raised his hand, signaling the team to halt. His gestures were precise, commanding silence and caution as they sought cover amidst the ruins.
Rourke, quick to adapt, slid into position beside Argus, his voice barely a whisper, "What is it?" Argus, ever vigilant, pressed a finger to where his lips would be beneath his helmet, urging silence. With a subtle movement, he nodded towards the source of the sound—beyond the remnants of a neighborhood playground that once echoed with the laughter of his children. It was in the treeline, hidden from view, that his battle armor’s sensors had detected the rustling.
Around them, Fireteam Valor and their OGDF counterparts adhered to the silent command, securing their positions with practiced precision. Each member found refuge behind what remained of the suburban landscape: overturned vehicles, fragmented walls, and the hollowed shells of once-cozy homes. They maintained a vigilant security perimeter, eyes sharply scanning the horizon, weapons at the ready, each soldier an embodiment of quiet determination.
The tension in the air was palpable, the suspense stretching into long moments that felt like hours. Then, as suddenly as it had begun, the suspense shattered with the emergence of figures from the treeline. Not the feared Obsidian invaders, but three Human soldiers. Their appearance was familiar, and then it hit Argus like a brick, they were Orbital Infantry Troopers. The engraving found on their armor’s left shoulder plate signified they were survivors that escaped the destruction of the GFC Vindicator.
In the eerie stillness of Lieutenant Argus's ravaged neighborhood, the appearance of the Orbital Infantry Troopers stirred a complex mix of relief and apprehension among Fireteam Valor and their OGDF allies. Knowing the paramount importance of avoiding friendly fire, Argus acted swiftly and decisively to signal their friendly status to the newly arrived soldiers.
Using the tactical display within his helmet, Argus quickly accessed his armor’s emergency beacon. He activated a series of low-frequency pulses—designed to be detected by standard military communications gear without alerting enemy forces. These pulses, a morse-coded message of "all green," were a subtle yet effective beacon of their presence and intent.
As the OI soldiers cautiously approached, their weapons lowered in response to Argus's signal. Once they had regrouped, the soldier in the lead spoke first. “Sir, I’m Sergeant Banks. I’m surprised to see anyone alive.” The tension eased.
Argus responded in a quiet dialogue and gestured at himself, "Lieutenant Argus, STRIKE Team Valor, Praetorians. What happened to you, Sergeant?”
Sergeant Banks looked weary but resolute. "We were aboard the GFC Vindicator when those bastards came out of Gravspace. It was chaos," he began, his voice carrying the weight of recent horrors. "Captain Harris gave the order to evacuate when it was clear we wouldn't survive. The navigations officer plotted our escape pods to scatter across Trident’s northern hemisphere."
Sergeant Banks paused; his gaze distant as he recounted the harrowing descent to the planet's surface. "The drop was hell. Most of our people didn't make it. And those who did... we've been fighting skirmish after skirmish. We had twenty-three with us including myself, but we're all that’s left," he gestured to himself and his two companions.
Argus, moved by the sergeant's account, responded with a somber nod. "I'm sorry for your loss, Sergeant. You’re not alone now. You're welcome to join us against these bastards."
“We would love nothing more than to send them all to hell,” Sergeant Banks said with a look of vengeance on his face. He and his men exchanged brief looks, a silent conversation passing between them before they nodded in agreement. 
They continued towards Argus’s home. Every step he took towards his home was painful. The closer he got to his home, the more devastation he could see.
Lieutenant Argus's emotional journey culminated in heartbreak once he was close enough to see his home—or rather, what was left of it. The remains of the house barely recognizable under the twilight sky.
Pushing through the rubble with a sense of urgency driven by a distant cry for help, Argus was confronted with a scene that shattered his heart. There, amidst the debris, lay Julia and Kat, lifeless, their stillness more piercing than the chaos around. Nearby, an alien lay in a pool of its own blood, a grim marker of the struggle that had taken place. Though grief clawed at his soul, Argus's attention was drawn by the persistent cries of his son, buried somewhere under the wreckage.
Guided by the sounds of distress, Argus found himself at a collapsed pillar. Frantically, he began to move chunks of concrete and twisted metal, each movement fueled by a desperate hope. Then, his hands uncovered Junior, his son's body bruised and battered, a horrific gash across his abdomen telling of the cruelty he had faced.
Lieutenant Argus unsealed his helmet and set it down next to Junior, dirt and sweat covering his face. He gently picked Junior up with both hands, Junior’s head resting gently on the armor plate of his father’s forearm.
Holding Mark Jr. tenderly, Argus tried to offer comfort amidst the unspeakable horror. "It's okay, Junior, daddy's here," he whispered, trying to swallow the lump in his throat.
"Daddy, I got the one that got Mommy and Kat. I'm sorry," Junior's cries and faint words cut through Argus, a mixture of pride and profound sorrow filling him.
"You have nothing to be sorry about, Junior. I'm so proud of you for being so brave. I'm so sorry I wasn't here, I love you so much." Argus replied, his voice breaking under the weight of his regret.
"It's okay, Daddy. You were fighting the monsters. I love you too," Junior murmured, his voice growing fainter, a serene acceptance in his tone that was beyond his years.
At those words, Argus felt his son’s lifeforce slipping away. The realization that Junior might not make it, despite his brave fight, deepened the chasm of his grief. "No, no, no, Junior, not you too!" he cried out as Junior slipped into unconsciousness.
The team formed a protective cocoon around Argus and his son, their presence a silent promise of shared sorrow and unwavering support. In the fading light, amidst the ruins of his home, Argus held his son, a moment of profound grief shared by all who stood witness.
As he cradled his son, believing these might be their final moments, a medic from the OGDF quickly ran to them. After a quick but thorough examination, she spoke with urgency, “We need to get him back to Gladius ASAP. He’s unconscious, but barely hanging on. I’ll patch his wound up the best I can, to give him more time. Put him down, sir. Let me work. I got this!"
Lieutenant Argus gently placed Mark Junior down, his movements tender despite the turmoil raging within him. His wife and daughter were gone. His son, badly wounded, might not even make it to Gladius in time to receive the proper care. Argus would never be able to hold them, listen to their laughs, cry with them, or do anything with them, ever again.
With all of these thoughts surging through his head, he was overcome by a tempest of grief and rage. He turned his attention to the lifeless alien nearby—the embodiment of the force that had torn his world apart.
With a primal scream, Argus unleashed his fury upon the fallen foe. His fists became instruments of vengeance, raining down upon the alien with unrelenting force. Each punch was a cathartic release of his anguish, blood and alien viscera splattering with every blow, as if trying to obliterate not just the creature's form, but its very existence. His screams filled the air, a raw expression of pain that words could barely capture.
Kaine, witnessing this outpouring of grief and rage, moved to intervene, concerned for Argus's well-being. However, Rourke held him back, understanding the need for this moment of release. "Let him get it out, Kaine," Rourke advised with a grave nod, his voice steady despite the emotional turmoil that enveloped them all.
As Argus continued his assault, the alien's visage became unrecognizable, his blows only ceasing when exhaustion and sorrow overtook him. The silence that followed was filled with his ragged breaths and the echo of his cries of vengeance, promises of retribution for the irreparable losses suffered.
"I'm going to kill every last one of you pieces of shit," he yelled, his voice hoarse with emotion. His threats to the enemy were visceral, a vow of vengeance not just spoken but felt deep within his soul. 
This raw display of anguish and anger resonated with everyone present, a somber reflection of the collective pain and resolve that fueled their fight against an unforgiving enemy. In the midst of the devastation of his personal world, Argus' vows of vengeance echoed the sentiments of many who had suffered at the hands of the invaders, a fierce determination to fight back against the darkness that had descended upon them.
After his rage subsided, exhausted and sorrowful, Argus returned to his son, the medic’s reassurances echoing in his mind. He quickly contacted support. "This is Valor Six, I need a Razor Wing for immediate medevac at my coordinates. It’s urgent."
The call was swift, the response immediate, and soon the distant throb of the Razor Wing’s engines grew louder as it approached their location. The team, relieved yet somber, prepared Mark Jr. for transport, their actions efficient.
As they loaded Mark Junior into the Razor Wing, Argus's promise to exact retribution on their enemies resonated with all present—a somber reflection of the collective pain and resolve that fueled their fight against an unforgiving enemy. In the midst of devastation, their mission continued, each member grappling with their personal losses even as they prepared to press on.
With one last goodbye to his son, Lieutenant Argus, tears flowing down his dirt-ridden cheeks, said to Mark Junior, “Stay alive son. You’re always going to be a hero, my hero. I’ll be standing by you soon enough. You are a hero. Never forget that. I love you.”
Mark Junior, becoming more coherent heard his father and was able to weakly reply, “Daddy, you’re the hero. Get them for Mommy and Kat. I love you too.” he said as the Razor Wing’s engines roared and soared away.
Under the weight of his grief, Argus found himself grappling with the painful duality of his duties as both a soldier and a family man. The battlefield demanded his unwavering focus, yet the loss of his family had torn open a chasm in his soul that could not be ignored. He understood the critical nature of their mission and the stark reality that any delay could cost more innocent lives. However, in his heart, Argus knew he was not fit to lead—not until he could find a semblance of peace amidst the turmoil.
With heavy steps, Argus approached the lifeless form of his wife, Julia. He lifted her gently, cradling her with a tenderness that belied the strength of a soldier. His destination was a solitary tree in what remained of their backyard, a place that held memories of simpler, happier times. Julia had loved to sit on the swings there with their children, laughing and playing when the skies of New Eridanus were clear, and the air was filled with the promise of peace.
Witnessing Argus's solemn procession, Rourke instinctively knew what needed to be done. He moved to Kat's side, lifting her with a gentleness that echoed Argus's own, joining in the poignant march to the tree. Together, they moved through the ruins of what had once been a home filled with love and laughter, now reduced to ashes and memories.
The tree stood defiant amid the devastation, its branches reaching skyward as if in silent tribute to the resilience of life. Beneath its shade, Argus laid Julia down, her final resting place chosen with a heart-wrenching intentionality. Rourke followed suit, placing Kat beside her mother.
For a moment, time seemed to stand still. The only sounds were the gentle rustle of leaves in the breeze and the quiet sobs of hardened soldiers confronted with the fragility of life. In this sacred space, amidst the shadows of loss, Argus and his team shared a moment of profound mourning, a testament to the bonds that tie humanity together in the face of unspeakable sorrow.
This brief respite under the tree was more than a gesture of farewell; it was a solemn pledge. A pledge to remember those lost, to fight in their name, and to carry their memory into every battle. As they stood together, a makeshift family forged in the crucible of war, each soldier silently vowed to honor the sacrifices made, with the hope that their actions might pave the way for a future where such tragedies would no longer befall those they swore to protect.
In the solemn quiet of their backyard, beneath the lone standing tree that bore witness to their grief, Rourke's voice gently broke the silence. "Any words for the fallen, sir?" he asked, his tone respectful, knowing the importance of honoring those who had been lost.
Argus hesitated; the weight of his sorrow heavy upon his shoulders. He knew that finding words to say goodbye, though heartbreaking, might also bring a sliver of solace, not just to him but to those standing with him. 
First, he knelt beside Julia, his fingers brushing through her hair, which felt as soft as ever, a reminder of the small intimacies of their life together. 
"Julia," he started, his voice a mix of love and regret, "I remember the day we got lost on the back trails of Verdant Pass. You laughed, saying it was the best detour we ever took... I'd give anything to be lost with you again." After a pause filled with memories, he gently kissed her forehead, a silent farewell.
Turning to his daughter, Kat, a soft smile found its way through Argus's grief as he remembered her joy. "That damned 'Toy Story,' Kat. You made us watch that Old Earth movie every weekend, and I pretended it was a chore... What I wouldn't give to watch it over and over with you again." His voice cracked as he leaned in to kiss her forehead, each word a testament to the deep love and the moments they shared, forever cherished.
Lastly, he spoke to his son. Although his son was still on the flight to Gladius, Argus felt the need to speak to him all the same. 
Argus stood up straight and looked into the sky, directly at the looming alien warship overhead, "Junior, you were braver than anyone I've ever known. You stood tall, protecting your mom and sister... I couldn't have asked for a better son." His voice trembled as he grappled with his deepest regret. "I'm sorry I wasn't here to protect you. But goddamn it, I promise I'll never stop until they all pay. Junior, you better heal up. I think this war’s just getting started. And you need to be ready." His final words were a vow, not just to his son, but to himself—a promise of vengeance for his family and for every soul taken by the invaders.
As Argus stood, his resolve hardened, his grief morphing into an unwavering commitment to the fight ahead. The personal tragedy that had unfolded beneath the tree solidified the bond among those present, a shared determination to honor the fallen not just with words but with action. In this moment of profound sorrow and solemn promises, the path forward was clear: they would fight, not just for revenge, but for a future where such losses would never be endured again.
Lieutenant Argus stood with Fireteam Valor, OGDF attachments and OI counterparts amidst the remnants of what had once been his home. The burial was over, his family's graves mere mounds under the tree. The alien warship, a looming specter in the sky, was now the source of his agony, the architect of his despair. 
Lieutenant Argus, with a cold resolve shimmering in his eyes, addressed Fireteam Valor, "Men, it's time. We are reassembling at rally point Delta," he announced, his voice unwavering, every syllable a testament to his determination. 
Fireteam Valor immediately understood the gravity of Argus's command. These warriors, forged in the fires of countless battles, needed no further explanation. Argus added fiercely, "If these bastards want war, then we'll give them war." 
The access to rally point Delta, a classified bunker located under a local coffee shop and roughly one-hundred meters underground, was ingeniously concealed. To the untrained eye, the old jukebox in the corner of the shop was nothing more than a relic from a bygone era. However, a specific sequence of buttons pushed on the jukebox would activate a mechanism, causing the floor beneath it to slide open, revealing the entrance to the bunker. This method ensured that Fireteam Valor's movements remained inconspicuous to anyone and anything, safeguarding the secrecy of their mission. The bunker, originally intended as an emergency fallout facility, ironically housed one of the last portable nuclear devices left in the Galactic Forge Coalition’s arsenal—a single warhead, with no promise of another. Argus's plan was clear and audacious in its simplicity: get that one nuke aboard the alien warship that hovered like a specter of doom over New Eridanus and blow it out of the sky from within. One device. One chance. The stakes were monumental, but the resolve of Fireteam Valor was unbreakable.
As Lieutenant Argus and Fireteam Valor navigated the devastated streets of New Eridanus, their advance towards the designated rendezvous remained unhampered, despite the heavy shadows that clung to their hearts. Argus's helmet radio, a lifeline to the broader conflict, crackled to life with a message from Ironclad Six.
"Ironclad Six, this is Valor Six. Go ahead, over," Argus transmitted, his voice steady amidst the chaos.
"Valor Six, Ironclad Six. We've successfully secured our sector and are currently escorting civilians to evac location Tango in sector twenty-three, break,” he paused, “ Additionally, we've been informed our attached OGDF platoon, Viper, is being recalled to Gladius as an armed escort for the civilians on the Pegasus transports. Viper Platoon will be redeployed, over”
The mention of Viper Platoon and their imminent redeployment tugged at Argus's memories—a flash of combat on planet Crete alongside Lieutenant Strickland and his men. The brutality of their methods against the insurgents, though effective, had always set Argus on edge, a stark contrast to his values despite their shared goals. Because of their brutality, it was Lieutenant Argus who dubbed them Death Platoon, a name that Lieutenant Strickland and his men embraced.
"Copy that, Ironclad Six. Good work. Ensure those civilians are safe. Once your baggage has shipped out, we're regrouping at rally point Delta. Over," Argus responded, already plotting the next phase of their operation.
There was a brief pause before Ironclad Six’s voice returned, tinged with anticipation, "Sir? We going in hot?"
Argus couldn't help but smile grimly at the question. "In a manner of speaking. I have an idea, and we have work to do," he said, his mind racing with the logistics of what was to come. The mission to infiltrate the alien warship loomed large in his thoughts—a daring plan that could turn the tide of their desperate struggle.
“Roger that. My team will be there as soon as possible. Ironclad Six out.”
