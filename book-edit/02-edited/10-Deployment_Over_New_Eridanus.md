Chapter 10 – Deployment Over New Eridanus
Date: December 16, 2888
Time: 1350 Galactic Standard Time (GTC)
Location: New Eridanus

As the STRIKE Transports reached the upper atmosphere, the view from their windows revealed a scene of utter devastation. The planet below was engulfed in chaos, a tapestry of destruction that stretched as far as the eye could see. Massive explosions dotted the landscape, sending mushroom clouds billowing into the atmosphere, a stark testament to the ferocity of the invasion. The sky itself seemed angry, painted in hues of red and orange, reflecting the apocalyptic state of the planet. Even the alien warships were so large, they seemingly dwarfed Trident’s major cities.
The turbulence inside the transports was intense, the vehicles shaking violently as they pierced through the disturbed atmosphere. The Praetorians, strapped tightly in their seats, could only watch in horror as enemy warships descended through the clouds, their ominous silhouettes growing larger and more menacing. One massive ship already hovered over New Eridanus, casting a dark shadow over the city, and from what Lieutenant Argus could see, there was another over Titan City.
As the transports began their plummet towards the destination, the alien forces detected their presence. Suddenly, the sky was alive with the deadly dance of energy projectiles aimed at the descending transports. The STRIKE Transports' automated systems sprang into action, executing evasive maneuvers with robotic precision. Over the transport’s communications channel, a calm, artificial voice announced, "Punching it in three, two, one." With a sudden and forceful boom of acceleration, the transports dove even faster, the G-forces pressing the soldiers into their seats as they desperately tried to evade the relentless barrage.
The scene outside the windows was a blur of motion, streaks of light and explosions filling their field of view, a deadly fireworks display in the sky. Then, in a heart-stopping moment, Fireteam Shadowhawk’s transport, despite its evasive efforts, was hit by a massive anti-air projectile. A blinding flash of light and a deafening explosion filled the sky as the hull of the transport was torn open.
The Praetorians in the neighboring transports witnessed a sight that chilled them to the bone. They saw their comrades from Shadowhawk being sucked out into the open sky, engulfed in flames as they fell towards the surface. An entire team of elite soldiers lost before even setting foot on the battlefield.
The loss sent a wave of shock and grief through the Praetorians, but it also hardened their resolve. They understood the stakes of their mission more than ever. The transports continued their rapid descent, navigating through the chaos with a desperate urgency. Each soldier prepared for the imminent landing, their minds focused on the task at hand, but their hearts heavy with the loss of their fellow Praetorians.
As they broke through into the lower atmosphere, the transports stabilized slightly. The enemy's presence loomed large, and the battle for New Eridanus – and for their very survival – was about to begin.
Inside Fireteam Valor’s STRIKE Transport, the atmosphere was thick with tension and fear. The grim spectacle of Fireteam Shadowhawk’s transport being destroyed had left its mark, casting a shadow over even the most seasoned soldiers. The rookie, visibly shaken by the horrific scene, voiced his fear, his words trembling, "Holy shit! Did you see that? They... they didn't even have a chance...we’re going to get blasted out of the sky and we won’t even have a chance to fi…"
Before he could continue, Harlan, sitting across from him, reacted swiftly. With a sharp, decisive movement, he reached across and smacked the rookie’s helmet firmly. "Get ahold of yourself!" Harlan's voice was a mix of stern command and fiery determination. "You're a goddamn Praetorian. We live. We fight. We die. That's it. Focus."
The rookie, stunned by the suddenness of Harlan's action, nodded quickly, his eyes hardening as he swallowed his fear. The rest of the team, witnessing this exchange, silently acknowledged Harlan's words. A hard reminder of their duty and the reality of their profession.
The cabin of the transport was now filled with a renewed sense of resolve. Each Praetorian checked their gear, mentally preparing for the battle that awaited them. The fear remained, a natural response to the unknown and the dangerous, but it was now tempered by the unyielding spirit of the Praetorian creed.
Fireteam Valor, united in their purpose, readied themselves for the imminent engagement. They were Praetorians, the elite of the elite, and they were prepared to face whatever challenges lay ahead with courage and determination.
As Fireteam Valor's STRIKE Transport plummeted through the atmosphere at a blistering twelve-hundred kilometers per hour, the intense velocity pushed them past the OGDF dropships and Razor Wings below. Skimming dangerously close to the surface, they flew low, possibly evading the enemy's radar. But above them, the sky was an inferno, lit up with relentless enemy fire trying to bring them down.
In the cabin, the Praetorians braced against the tumultuous descent, their bodies tense and eyes fixed on the maelstrom outside. The transport shook violently under the strain, the sound of anti-aircraft fire reverberating through the metal hull.
Suddenly, a catastrophic explosion ripped through the air. Fireteam Honor’s transport, leading the formation, was struck by a direct hit. The impact was devastating. A blinding flash and a deafening boom filled the sky as the transport burst into a ball of fire and debris. The aftermath was horrifying – fragments of the transport and its passengers were scattered across the sky, some colliding with the other transports in a gruesome rain of metal and flesh.
Inside Fireteam Valor's transport, the scene was one of shock and horror. The rookie, pale-faced and wide-eyed, stared at the carnage. Others winced or turned away, the brutality of what they'd just witnessed etching itself into their memories.
Argus, seeing the despair and fear in his team's eyes, knew he must act to rally their spirits. He stood up, his voice cutting through the chaos. "Listen up!" he shouted. "This is what we trained for. This is why we fight! Down there is where they die!” Argus paused as he simultaneously reached above his head to pull down the emergency deployment lever, just in case they were hit with an anti-aircraft projectile, “We live! We fight! We die!”
His eyes burned with a fierce determination under his visor as he continued, "Those bastards think they can take our planet and our families. We kill them all. No goddamn mercy! Shoot anything that isn’t human! Do you get me?”
“Sir, yes, sir!” Each member of Fireteam Valor screamed in unison.
His words resonated in the cabin, igniting a fire in the hearts of his men. Faces hardened, grips tightened on weapons, and a steely resolve replaced the fear. They were Praetorians – they would not waver, no matter the odds.
The transport continued its rapid descent, cutting through the sky, a lone bullet heading towards its target. The enemy fire intensified, a relentless attempt to stop them, but the Praetorians were undeterred. They were ready to face whatever awaited them on the ground, united by a common purpose and the unyielding will to fight.
As Fireteam Valor’s STRIKE Transport neared its destination, the soldiers inside spotted enemy vessels – similar in design to their own transports – hurtling towards the surface. It was a clear sign: enemy soldiers were deploying, and an immediate firefight upon landing was inevitable.
A massive concussion blast ripped through the sky, catching the last two transports in its wake. The force of the explosion knocked them violently off course. Inside, the Praetorians were thrown against their restraints, the world outside a blur of spinning chaos. The transports' systems flickered and failed, their normal functions crippled by the blast.
Argus, amidst the disarray, made a split-second decision. "Manual override!" he yelled. "Opening the drop hatches, now!" The soldiers scrambled to comply, their hands working frantically over the controls. With a groan of stressed metal, the hatches swung open, exposing them to the open sky.
"Activate graviton arrestors, full downward thrust on my mark!" Argus commanded, his voice cutting through the roar of the wind. "Brace for high impact, and goddammit, watch for the enemy when we hit the ground. They’re waiting for us!"
The boom of the manual release echoed through the transports as the Praetorians were dropped from the sky. The graviton arrestors engaged with full force, creating a jarring, yet controlled fall. They plummeted towards the surface, falling from a height of four-hundred meters high. Although the graviton arrestors were working overtime to ensure the Praetorians stayed controlled, the landing promised to be anything but gentle.
As they fell, enemy fire erupted from below. Alien soldiers, having spotted the descending Praetorians, opened fire, their weapons spewing deadly energy projectiles into the sky. Two Praetorians from Fireteam Ironclad were hit mid-fall by a large energy blast, their bodies erupting in fiery explosions, fragments scattering into the wind. Tessa, too, was struck in her armor’s upper left breastplate, piercing her armor and wounding her; a searing pain shot through her body as she was knocked unconscious by the concussion of the projectile that hit her. As Tessa’s battle armor sensors detected her unconsciousness, the suit’s automated guidance system activated. The guidance system barely kept Tessa’s graviton arrestors from flailing on the deadly plunge to the planet’s surface.
They hit the ground hard, the impact sending shockwaves through their bodies, the force of the hard drop sending kinetic energy outward from their graviton arrestors with a resonating boom. Before they could fully recover, they were under a hail of enemy fire. Argus, with battle-hardened reflexes, unclipped his weapon from his breastplate holster and immediately zeroed in on an enemy soldier to his right. He fired a burst of rounds, hitting the alien squarely in the chest. The impact sent a spray of dark, viscous blood into the air, the alien's body crumpling to the ground in a heap.
Vega immediately rushed to Tessa's side, her movements swift and purposeful amidst the chaos. As she knelt beside Tessa, she quickly checked her helmet display, which showed Tessa's vital signs. Although weak, Tessa was still alive.
Vega's hands worked quickly, removing the damaged portion of Tessa's breastplate armor to assess the wound. Beneath the armor, she found a deep gash, the edges of the wound jagged and raw. Blood seeped from the injury, staining Tessa's combat suit. Vega knew she needed to act fast.
As Vega reached for her medical kit, she noticed that Tessa's suit's auto-aid system was damaged, rendering it useless in this critical moment. Without hesitation, Vega began to manually administer first aid. She realized that she did not have time to clean the wound properly. She provided first-aid as best she could under the circumstances, applying pressure to stem the bleeding, and administered a temporary sealant to cover the injury. "Stay with me, Byrne," she muttered, more to herself than to her unconscious patient, her hands never slowing. "I told you I'd always be here to sew you back together. So don't you dare quit on me — not on my watch."
In the midst of her life-saving efforts, an alien sniper from a building fifty meters away had them in its sights. Suddenly, a shot rang out. Vega's body jerked violently as a projectile pierced through her backplate, exiting cleanly through the center of her chest. She slumped sideways, her hands sliding from Tessa's wound. The medic who kept them all alive was gone before she came to rest against the rubble.
Rourke, reacting instinctively, zeroed in on the sniper's position. With a vengeful precision, he fired his weapon, eliminating the alien threat with a well-placed shot. "Vega!" he yelled, his voice a mix of anger and sorrow.
Rourke was momentarily frozen by the shock of losing Vega. Vega lay motionless, her death a reminder of the dangers the Praetorians faced. Rourke's quick action had prevented any further casualties, but the loss of Vega weighed heavily on him. Rourke’s foresight had not been active during that moment. He blamed himself for Vega’s death.
To his left, Tessa gasped for air as she regained consciousness. Her adrenaline was so high, she didn’t notice her wound or her damaged battle armor. She came to her senses and unclipped her weapon. An alien noticed her stumbling to her feet and erratically fired, missing all shots. Tessa engaged the alien with ruthless efficiency. Her shots tore through the alien's arm, severing it cleanly. The alien screamed in agony as its limb hit the ground, its weapon clutched in its severed hand, still erratically firing. Tessa didn't hesitate, sending another round through the alien's skull, silencing it instantly.
Nearby, Rourke targeted an enemy taking cover behind fallen debris. He fired, his rounds ripping through the cover and piercing the alien's head, a mist of blood and brain matter painting the wreckage.
The rookie, amid the chaos, managed to pull out a timed grenade. With a shaky yet determined hand, he lobbed it towards a group of three enemy soldiers taking cover in a crater, twenty meters away. The grenade landed in their midst, and for a brief moment, there was a tense pause before it detonated with a deafening roar. The explosion was gruesome – the enemies were torn apart, their bodies disintegrating in a shower of blood and gore.
The air was thick with the smell of burnt flesh and the unfamiliar stench of alien blood. The ground was littered with the remnants of the fallen enemy, a brutal testament to the Praetorians' efficiency.
In the sky, enemy aircraft swooped down in strafing runs, their weapons blazing. The Praetorians scattered, firing upwards, their bullets piercing the hulls of the enemy ships. One of the aircraft spiraled out of control, crashing to the ground in a fiery explosion that sent debris flying in all directions.
The battle was relentless, the Praetorians fighting with a grim determination, each aware that their survival and the success of their mission hung by a thread. They moved as one, lethal unit trained for exactly this kind of hellish combat. They were fighting not just for their lives, but for the citizens still trapped in New Eridanus, and all those who called it home.
As Argus swiftly assessed the chaotic battlefield, he realized the necessity of a strategic fallback. "Fireteam Valor, on me! We're going to regroup. Covering fire on my mark!" he commanded. The team, well-versed in combat tactics, began a coordinated bounding withdrawal to a nearby structure.
The building, a remnant of the city's once-thriving district, stood partially ravaged by the earlier alien assault. Its exterior, marked with blast scars and blackened by fire, provided a stark contrast to the sleek and advanced designs of the surrounding architecture. Windows were shattered, and the facade was crumbling in certain areas, but the structure's core appeared sound enough to offer temporary refuge.
During their retreat, the Praetorians engaged the enemy with lethal efficiency. Harlan, spotting an alien soldier emerging from the rubble, quickly raised his rifle and fired. The shot landed squarely in the alien's torso, sending it reeling backward with a guttural cry, its dark blood splattering against the debris.
Tessa, finally realizing the pain right under her left collarbone as the adrenaline subsided, took aim at another enemy soldier attempting to flank them, her arms shaking as she lifted her weapon. She squeezed the trigger, and her bullets tore through the alien's leg, causing it to collapse with a shriek of agony. She finished it with a precise shot to the head, silencing it permanently.
The rookie spotted two more enemy combatants advancing. He steadied his aim and fired in quick succession. The first alien was hit in the shoulder, spinning it around before a follow-up shot found its head. The second alien was struck in the chest, the impact lifting it off its feet before it crashed to the ground lifelessly.
As the team reached the building, they swiftly cleared the entrance, ensuring no enemy remained inside. The interior was a shadow of its former self. Charred furniture, broken glass, and debris littered the floor, a ghostly reminder of the people who once occupied this space. The air was thick with the smell of smoke and the lingering tang of alien blood.
Inside, Lieutenant Charles quickly organized the remaining Ironclad members to secure the south side. They set up defensive positions, using broken furniture and wall fragments as makeshift cover.
On the north side, Argus and Fireteam Valor established their stronghold. They kept their weapons trained on the shattered windows and the broken doorway, ready for any enemy incursion.
Despite their reduced numbers, the Praetorians' resolve remained unbroken. They were a dwindling force, but each was a formidable fighter in their own right. The team members checked their ammunition and prepared for the next confrontation. They were battered, but not beaten. Their spirits fueled by the urgency of their mission and the memory of their fallen comrades.
In this improvised fortress, amidst the ruins of a city under siege, they waited, ready to defend their position and counterattack when the opportunity arose. The battle for New Eridanus was far from over, and the Praetorians were determined to fight to the bitter end.
As Fireteam Valor settled into their makeshift stronghold, the radio crackled to life. "Valor Six, this is Pegasus Two, over," came the pilot's voice, tense but clear over the comms.
Argus quickly grabbed the radio. "Pegasus Two, this is Valor Six, go ahead, over."
The pilot's voice returned with urgency, "ETA to your position is ten minutes. Is there a secure landing zone? Over."
Argus glanced out a shattered window at the war-torn landscape. "Negative, Pegasus Two. No secure zone available. Be advised, you'll be coming in hot, but we'll ensure it's as secure as possible, over."
His eyes scanned the surrounding area and settled on a building adjacent to their position. It appeared structurally sound, perhaps the best option they had for a landing zone under the circumstances. "Pegasus Two, adjust your approach for a landing on the building adjacent to our position. It's the best shot we have. Have Razor Wings on standby for cover fire, over."
"Roger that, Valor Six. Adjusting approach and coordinating with the Razor Wings. Pegasus Two out."
As the teams maintained a vigilant 360-degree perimeter defense around the building, occasional enemy combatants approached their position. Each time, the Praetorians responded with swift and deadly precision. A soldier from Fireteam Ironclad, positioned by a broken window, spotted an alien soldier attempting to sneak up from the rubble. He took careful aim and fired, the shot landing in the alien's head, dropping it instantly.
Meanwhile, Argus and Charles met inside to quickly strategize. The sound of sporadic gunfire and the occasional alien shriek from outside punctuated their planning.
"Charles, I need you to take Ironclad up as high as possible in this building. Provide overhead cover and keep an eye out for any more incoming hostiles," Argus instructed.
"Roger," Charles replied, nodding. "We'll give you the cover you need."
"Valor will move to the adjacent building and secure a landing zone on the rooftop. We need to ensure those transports can land safely," Argus added, his voice resolute. “Execute.”
Charles and Argus then split up to execute their plan. Fireteam Ironclad began their ascent, moving cautiously but quickly up the building's stairwell, ready to set up an overwatch position. Their footsteps echoed in the hollow, damaged corridors, offices and restrooms. A stark reminder of the once-civilian nature of their battleground.
Outside, Fireteam Valor, led by Argus, tactically maneuvered through the debris and chaos towards the adjacent building. They moved in a tight formation, covering each other's movements, their eyes constantly scanning for threats. The sounds of battle raged around them, but their focus was singular – secure that rooftop for the incoming Pegasus Transports. The fate of the civilians and the success of their mission depended on it.
As Fireteam Valor quietly and swiftly moved towards the adjacent building, the ambience was eerily calm outside. The only sounds punctuating the silence were the distant, precise shots from Ironclad's sniper, Specialist Brooks. His role was crucial, providing overwatch and taking out enemies from a distance as the rest of his team provided additional overhead security.
Suddenly, Brooks's voice crackled over the radio. "Valor Six, be advised, enemy presence detected at your objective. I count at least four hostiles," he reported.
Argus acknowledged the intel with a curt "Copy that, Ironclad Three. Keep us covered."
As Fireteam Valor breached the building, they were immediately met with enemy fire. Shots zipped past them, narrowly missing. The team reacted instinctively, returning fire with deadly accuracy. The two hostiles guarding the door were taken down in a matter of seconds, their bodies hitting the floor with heavy thuds.
The remaining two enemies were firing from a flight of stairs above. Harlan, without hesitation, activated his helmet visor’s thermal imaging. "Fuck this," he muttered under his breath. He spotted the heat signatures of two enemies through the ceiling and quickly prepared a charge of Plasma C-5, a powerful explosive designed to accurately blast through specific areas of a structure without compromising their overall integrity.
"Take cover!" Harlan yelled to his team, a hint of excitement in his voice. He planted the charge on the ceiling, quickly stepping back but keeping his Shredder aimed upwards.
"Fire in the hole!" Harlan shouted as he pressed the detonator. The charge exploded with a deafening roar, blowing a hole in the ceiling. The enemies above were caught completely off-guard, tumbling down through the blasted opening, disoriented and vulnerable.
Harlan didn't miss a beat. As the enemies fell through the hole, he unleashed a barrage of bullets from his Shredder. The rounds tore through the aliens, shredding their bodies in a spray of blood and guts. The smell of blood and alien entrails filled the air, mixed with a foul odor that hinted at their fear and desperation in their final moments.
"Fuck yeah, Harlan!" Rourke exclaimed, a grin spreading across his face amidst the carnage. "That'll get you some more Red Man come R&R!"
"All in a day's work," Harlan replied, chuckling darkly. "You could say the situation got blown out of proportion." His laugh echoed in the now-quiet building.
Tessa, shaking her head at the macabre humor, couldn't help but interject, "Jesus, guys. What’s with the jokes right now? Seriously, you two have to learn time and place," she cleared her throat and nodded her head towards the outside of the building, “If I didn’t know any better, I’d say there’s a war going on out there.”
Harlan waved her off as he bent down to the fallen enemies, “These guys are easier to kill than the ones we fought at Charr. What gives? Not that I’m complaining or anything…”
“Cut the chatter. We still need to secure the landing zone. We can figure that shit out later. Let’s move!” Argus said firmly.
Despite the grimness of their situation, the team couldn't help but feel a surge of triumph. They'd cleared the immediate threat and could now focus on securing the landing zone for the Pegasus transports. 
Fireteam Valor, still operating with tactical precision, ascended the three flights of stairs to reach the top of the building. As they emerged onto the rooftop, their eyes were immediately drawn to the menacing sight in the sky. About five kilometers away, a massive alien warship hovered ominously over New Eridanus. It was emitting a strange radiation beam towards the surface, the purpose of which was unclear to the team. The sight was both awe-inspiring and terrifying.
Adding to the sense of impending doom, hundreds of enemy dropships were descending from the sky like a swarm of deadly locusts. Each one represented an additional threat, and the scale of the invasion became painfully clear.
"You guys seeing this?" Rourke asked, his voice a mix of disbelief and concern.
Tessa, her expression grim, added, "How the hell are we going to evacuate the civilians with just us and the OGDF teams? GFC didn’t really think Trident’s defense forces through."
Argus, looking towards the direction of his home, replied with a determined yet somber tone, "We won't be able to save them all, but we will save enough. My wife and kids are still out there. We have to be enough."
The team members, understanding the gravity of the situation, unanimously agreed, "Sir, we go to them first."
“I planned on it.” Argus said, a hint of regret and sorrow in his voice.
Rourke, scanning the horizon, asked, "How far from here?"
"Two kilometers north," Argus responded, his gaze fixed in the direction of the looming warship.
“My family is tough. My son, especially. He knows how to work all the weapons in the vault, but we need to work fast. They won’t last long with those bastards swarming the city,” he paused, studying the potential route they would need to take. “If we encounter any civilians on the way, we take them as well." Argus said, his voice reflecting both concern for his family and the determination to carry out their mission.
As they finalized their plan, the distant sound of the engines grew louder. "Pegasus Two, you're clear for landing. Marking the position with a strobe," Argus called over the radio.
Moments later, two Pegasus transports and a single, slightly damaged Razor Wing appeared in the sky above the building. The transports landed smoothly on the rooftop, despite the compromised Razor Wing, which showed signs of damage from protecting the transports during their flight; the other Razor Wing had been destroyed.
The OGDF platoons disembarked swiftly and efficiently, each soldier alert and ready for action. A quick headcount confirmed that all OGDF personnel were present and accounted for. The Razor Wing was now hovering nearby, its damage evident but still operational.
The rooftop was now a hive of activity, with Praetorians and OGDF soldiers coordinating their next objective. The sense of urgency was palpable; every second counted in their race to evacuate civilians and reach Argus's family.
As the teams prepared to move out, they knew the journey ahead would be fraught with danger. The enemy warship loomed ominously in the distance, a constant reminder of the overwhelming odds they faced. But the Praetorians and OGDF were resolute, driven by a sense of duty and the knowledge that lives depended on their actions.
As the situation on the rooftop stabilized, the two Pegasus transports prepared to depart back to Gladius. The engines of the transports rumbled to life, a deep, powerful sound that echoed across the rooftop. One by one, they lifted off, their bulky frames surprisingly agile as they ascended into the sky. The Razor Wing, despite its noticeable damage, followed suit. Its pilot radioed in to Argus, "Valor Six, Razor Wing Kilo will be on standby. I'm in stealth mode, thirty seconds out from your position at all times. Over."
Argus acknowledged with a quick, "Roger that. I’ll call if I need you. Over." He watched as the aircraft disappeared into the distance, reassured by the knowledge that they had aerial support if needed. The resilience of the Razor Wings was well known; a little damage wouldn't easily ground these formidable machines.
Turning his attention back to the mission, Argus motioned for his team and the OGDF soldiers to regroup. 
"Let's move down to the second floor. We'll set up our mobile TOC there," Argus instructed. The Praetorians, along with the OGDF platoons, quickly descended the stairs. The second floor, though scarred by the battle, offered a relatively secure environment. Windows were shattered, and there was scattered debris, but the overall cover and concealment was sound.
Upon reaching the second floor, Argus and the team leaders started organizing their forces. "Nonessential personnel maintain security on the rooftop and ground level. We can't afford any surprises," Argus ordered.
Royce and Harlan headed back to the rooftop to provide overhead security, their eyes constantly scanning the horizon for any signs of enemy movement. Simultaneously, a squad from the OGDF took position on the ground floor, securing the entrance and ensuring no enemy forces could infiltrate the building.
As the teams dispersed to their posts, Argus's attention was drawn to Tessa. She was trying to mask her discomfort, but her movements were stiff, and her face was etched with pain. He was about to speak when an OGDF medic stepped forward, noticing the same signs.
The medic, a sturdy man with a serious demeanor, approached Tessa. "I'm Sergeant Fisk," he introduced himself, his voice firm but not unkind. "Tessa, is it? Let me have a look at that wound."
Tessa hesitated for a moment, reluctant to divert attention from the mission, but nodded in acknowledgment. Sergeant Fisk gently examined her wound, his skilled hands working deftly as he removed the temporary sealant Vega had applied earlier.
As he peeled away the sealant, the extent of the wound became apparent. It was a deep gash, angry and red around the edges, a clear sign of infection setting in. "This should've been cleaned before sealing," Fisk muttered, his brow furrowing in concern.
He looked up at Tessa, "This is infected, and it's not something we can treat adequately in the field with your suit's auto-aid damaged. You need to get to a hospital, or you're at risk of blood poisoning."
Tessa barely heard him. Her eyes had drifted to the discarded sealant in the medic's tray — that rough, hurried patch, slapped on by hands that had known they were out of time. Vega's work. She scanned the ruined second floor and didn't find her; when she found Rourke instead, he wouldn't meet her eyes. That was answer enough.
The patch holding her together had been the last thing Elara Vega ever did. Tessa lay back against the rubble and let it settle into her, cold and final — because outside, the war was still grinding on, and it wasn't going to give her the time to grieve.
Tessa's expression was a mix of frustration and resignation. She knew the medic was right, but leaving the mission went against every instinct she had as a Praetorian.
Argus, overhearing the conversation, stepped over. "Sergeant Fisk, do what you can for now. Tessa, I’ll have the Razor Wing get you back to Gladius. This is not up for debate. Hurry up and get fixed. And get into new battle armor once you’re ready."
Tessa, with a grimace replied, “Yes, sir. I’ll be back asap!”
Fisk administered a basic cleaning and a stronger, more effective temporary sealant, offering some relief from the pain and preventing further infection for the time being. Tessa nodded her thanks, gritting her teeth against the discomfort.
Argus tuned into the frequency of the Razor Wing, which was still maintaining a stealth position nearby.
"Razor Wing Kilo, this is Valor Six," Argus called into the radio, his voice calm but urgent.
"Valor Six, this is Razor Wing Kilo, send it." came the crisp reply.
"I need an immediate evac for one of my team back to Gladius. Can you transport her to the medical sector in the northeast wing landing zone of the Gladius HQ?" Argus explained the situation succinctly.
There was a brief pause as the pilot assessed his current status. "Affirmative, Valor Six. I can make the run. I'll be at your location in less than thirty seconds. Have your wounded ready for immediate extraction."
"Copy that, Razor Wing Kilo. We'll have her prepped and ready. Valor Six out." Argus put down the radio and turned to Tessa, who'd been listening intently.
"Tessa, we've arranged for your immediate evac back to Gladius. The Razor Wing will be here shortly. They'll take you straight in to get fixed," he informed her, his tone reassuring yet firm.
Tessa, understanding the necessity of the situation, nodded in agreement, though the frustration at leaving her team in the midst of a critical mission was evident in her eyes.
As they waited for the Razor Wing's arrival, the team gathered around Tessa, offering words of encouragement and assuring her they would handle the mission. Despite the challenging circumstances, the sense of camaraderie and mutual support among them was unmistakable.
Soon, the sound of the Razor Wing's engines became audible as it approached their location. The pilot skillfully maneuvered the aircraft into position for a swift extraction. Tessa was carefully helped aboard, and with one last look at her team, she was whisked away into the sky, heading back to Gladius for the urgent care she needed.
As the Razor Wing's engines faded into the distance, Harlan, never one to miss an opportunity for a bit of dark humor, turned to the remaining team members with a wry grin. He got on the Razor Wing’s comms channel after commandeering Lieutenant Argus’s radio, "Well, looks like Tessa's taking an early vacation. Must be nice to get a ride back to base while we do all the hard work."
Surprisingly, the comms channel opened and Tessa's voice crackled through, dry and even. "Oh, absolutely, Harlan. Don't have too much fun without me — and do me a favor, try to hit what you aim at for once." A breath. "Save a few of those bastards. I'll want a full report on everything I missed."
Her voice dropped then, the humor draining out of it. “And sir — get your family out safe.”
“You know I will,” Argus said, a hint of doubt and unease in his tone.
The exchange served as a brief respite from the tension, a reminder of the deep bond and camaraderie that existed among the team members. It was these moments of light-heartedness that helped them cope with the stress of their chosen profession.
With Tessa on her way to receive medical attention, Argus refocused on the mission at hand. The teams were ready to proceed, their resolve undiminished despite the setback. They knew what they needed to do and were prepared to face the challenges ahead.
With spirits slightly lifted, the team refocused. Argus, with a final glance at the position where the Razor Wing had been, signaled for the team to move out. The mission awaited, and despite the challenges they faced, the Praetorians were ready to confront them head-on.
