Chapter 9 – Reign of Fire
Date: December 16th, 2888
Time: 1030 Galactic Standard Time (GTC)
Location: GFC Base - Gladius
In the war room aboard the Gladius, a somber mood prevailed as the members of Fireteam Honor and Fireteam Valor were engrossed in the tactical displays. They watched with a mix of awe and concern as Admiral Greenwich's fleet, though dwindling, valiantly engaged the enemy, taking down numerous hostile ships in the process.
The radio suddenly crackled to life, cutting through the tense atmosphere. "Gladius Six, this is Shadowhawk Six," Lieutenant Barkley's voice came through, heavy with the weight of recent events. "The Liberation is lost. We had to scuttle it to avoid enemy capture. Right before we hit Trident's atmosphere, we spotted a lone enemy vessel using invisibility tech. Its destination is unknown."
Captain Orion, maintaining his composed demeanor, responded, "Shadowhawk Six, understood. What's your current status?"
"We're planetside. Had a rough landing. Air brakes failed, and we had to perform a waterborne landing roughly six kilometers southeast of New Eridanus. Additionally, we have KIAs. I will brief you in person, sir," Barkley reported, his voice reflecting the gravity of their losses.
"Copy that, Shadowhawk Six. Can you provide your exact coordinates for extraction?" Orion inquired, ready to coordinate the rescue.
"Wait one," Barkley replied, a brief pause ensuing as he accessed the automated navigation system. "Coordinates marked on your display now.”
"Received, Shadowhawk Six. We're arranging transport for your extraction now. Stay sharp and hold your position," Orion instructed, his voice resolute.
"Roger that, Gladius Six. Shadowhawk Six out."
The radio went silent, leaving a heavy air in the room. The team members exchanged solemn glances, each acutely aware of the dire situation and the sacrifices already made.
Orion turned to the Crew Chief of the Pegasus Transport ship – Chief Sussex. " We need to organize a rapid extraction for Shadowhawk Six. They're vulnerable out there, and time is not on our side. We can't afford to lose more lives."
“Already have my team getting the birds ready to fly,” Chief Sussex said with a determined tone.
“Good. Fly hard, Chief.” 
The Chief turned around, took his helmet out from under his arm and firmly placed it on his head, and ran out of the War Room.
Captain Orion arose with a renewed sense of urgency, opened multiple communications channels to coordinate with the transport units and plotted the fastest route to Lieutenant Barkley's coordinates. “Less work for the Chief when he winds up his transport,” he thought to himself.
As the situation in the war room unfolded, the sound of engines coming to life signaled the commencement of a crucial mission. The Pegasus class dropship, a robust and agile craft, began to hum with activity above them. The war room, positioned directly below the launch and landing pads, subtly vibrated with the resonance of its engines.
Concurrently, the distinct, high-pitched whine of a Razor Wing fighter cut through the ambient noise. Its role was unmistakable: to escort the Pegasus dropship.
Amidst this backdrop, Harlan, standing by a console, reached into his pocket and retrieved his tobacco pouch. In an uncharacteristic display of tension, he took an unusually large pinch of Epsilon Wintergreen Longcut and placed it in his mouth. This action, more pronounced than usual, was a clear indicator of the heightened stakes, even for a veteran like Harlan.
Rourke, noticing Harlan's action, commented quietly to Tessa, "I’ve never seen Harlan so uptight."
Captain Orion, observing these small but telling reactions, addressed the room with a steady and confident voice. "The Pegasus Transport is ready, and the Razor Wing is set for escort. It's imperative we bring back Fireteam Shadowhawk and Fireteam Ironclad safely. They hold critical intelligence from The Surgeon and updates on Admiral Greenwich's engagement above the planet. Our strategy moving forward hinges on this information."
He paused to ensure his message was absorbed, then added, "Valor and Honor, prepare for the next mission brief as soon as the team’s land. Review your protocols and be ready to act swiftly based on the new intelligence. Precision and readiness are key in this upcoming operation."
With this, the war room became a hub of focused activity. Communications with the pilots of the Pegasus Transport and the Razor Wing were streamlined and efficient, ensuring coordinated and successful operations.
Outside, the engines of another Pegasus dropship powered up, emitting a deep, resolute thrum that mirrored the determination of each team inside. A second Razor Wing, poised and lethal, got ready for takeoff, its engines providing a sharp counterpoint to the dropship’s steady rumble.
In these moments, the war room and the aircraft above were synchronized in a singular, vital mission – the safe extraction of Shadowhawk and Ironclad. This operation showcased the intricate dance of teamwork and precision that characterized military endeavors, highlighting the unwavering spirit and commitment of those who serve.
The members of Fireteam Valor and Fireteam Honor, caught in the tension of awaiting their next orders, found a momentary escape in the camaraderie that often unites soldiers in such times.
Rourke, seizing the moment to lighten the mood, looked at Harlan with a playful smirk. "Harlan, I can smell that candy-ass Epsilon Wintergreen shit from here." he joked, his laughter echoing in the room.
Reaching into his pocket, Rourke produced a bag of Red Man chewing tobacco and tossed it to Harlan. "Try some Red Man, Harlan.”
Catching the pouch, Harlan raised an eyebrow in surprise. "Red Man? I thought Red Man died with Old Earth."
Rourke grinned, "Let’s just say, I know a guy in logistics who knows a guy. Some seed vault on Old Earth had a stash of tobacco seeds. They're growing it in agricultural stations now. Rare as hell, but nothing beats the original, eh?"
Harlan chuckled, the tension in the room easing slightly. "Thanks, Rourke. Didn't know you were such a connoisseur. Let me switch this crap out!" His retort was light, the camaraderie evident. 
Harlan casually leaned over to the garbage nearest Tessa, spitting out the wintergreen with a practiced ease before reaching for the bag of Red Man.
Tessa, observing this, wrinkled her nose in mock disgust and quipped, "Really, Harlan? Could you at least pretend to be civilized? You're making me reconsider my life choices just by watching you. Should have joined STARCOM Fleet, instead!"
Her comment drew a fresh round of chuckles from the team, and Harlan, with a grin, retorted, "Tessa, in the field, manners are the first casualty. You should know that by now."
He opened the bag of Red Man, carefully selecting a pinch. "But I'll try to keep it classy just for you," he added, winking at Tessa as he placed the chew in his mouth, ever-so delicately, a tiny dribble of Red Man saliva peeking through the left side of his mouth.
Rourke laughed, "There you go, Harlan. Embracing the old ways. Tessa, you'll get used to it. It's just another day,” he paused and glanced over at Harlan, “Dammit Harlan, don’t take all of it!”
Harlan let out a chuckle, “Don’t worry, I only took half!” He exclaimed, throwing the bag of Red Man back to Rourke.
After Rourke caught the Red Man, he shifted his attention to Royce, "Hey, rook," Rourke called out, a mischievous glint in his eye. "I've only ever seen you chew gum. How about letting your balls drop for a change?" He sauntered over to Royce, holding the bag of Red Man open just enough for him to get a good whiff.
Royce, trying to maintain his composure under Rourke's teasing gaze, retorted smartly, "I'm just trying to keep my habits as refined as my company, Rourke." Despite his words, he tentatively reached into the bag, thinking about the mistake he was going to make.
Royce attempted to partake in the ritual of chewing tobacco under Rourke's playful scrutiny, cautiously taking the smallest pinch of Red Man, an amount so minuscule it was almost comical.
As he tentatively placed the chew in his mouth, trying to appear nonchalant, it quickly became apparent that he was out of his depth. Within moments, his face twisted in discomfort, and he accidentally swallowed the tobacco. He started coughing violently, the unexpected reaction causing him to spit out the remnants onto the floor.
The room erupted into laughter at the sight, and Harlan, unable to contain his amusement, quipped, "Christ, rook — it's chaw, not chow! Who told you to eat the stuff?" His ribbing only added to the hilarity of the situation.
Royce, still coughing and trying to rid his mouth of the unpleasant taste, managed to retort between coughs, "Screw you, Harlan!" His response, though sharp, was delivered with a half-smile, acknowledging the joke at his expense.
Tessa, amidst her laughter, added her own playful remark, "Well, looks like Royce is fitting right in. Not sure if that's a good thing or a bad thing."
The laughter and banter served as a much-needed release for the team, temporarily lifting the weight of the situation they were in. It was a reminder of the importance of camaraderie and light-heartedness, even in the face of danger.
Off to the side, away from the worst of the noise, Vega watched the others the way she always did — counting heads, reading faces the way she read vitals. She'd clocked Harlan's oversized pinch before Rourke ever said a word, and she'd caught the tremor in Royce's hand when he reached for the bag. Keeping them breathing was the job. Keeping track of them — the small things nobody else bothered to notice — had somehow become the rest of it.
She drifted over to Tessa and, without asking, tilted her chin up to check the half-healed gash along her jaw from Charr.
"I'm fine, Vega," Tessa said, swatting at her hand — but she let her look.
"You're always fine. That's the problem." Vega pressed gently at the edge of the cut and watched Tessa refuse to flinch. "You take shots standing up that smart people take from cover. One of these days I'm not going to be there to sew you back together."
"That's what I keep you around for." Tessa's mouth twitched. "Now quit fussing. You're worse than my mother."
"Someone's got to be." Vega gave her shoulder a squeeze and let it go, already scanning the room for whoever needed her next.
As the light-hearted banter continued in the war room at Gladius, Argus found himself momentarily lost in laughter. However, his mind couldn't help but drift to thoughts of his family: Julia, Kat, and Junior. The concern for their safety amidst the escalating situation grew too strong to ignore. Seizing a moment, he stepped just outside the war room, close enough to return quickly if needed, but far enough to have a private conversation.
He dialed his home number, his heart racing with a mix of apprehension and longing. The phone rang, and after a few heartbeats, Julia's voice came through, laced with worry. "Mark, what’s going on? The news is talking about aliens and a battle above the planet."
Argus took a deep breath, steadying his voice. "Julia, listen carefully. I need you to remember the combination to the safe. I'll be back as soon as I can, but you need to be prepared. I’m not sure how long I’ll be on mission."
Julia's voice trembled slightly, "Mark, are we in danger?"
"Remember our emergency plan," Argus said firmly. "If it comes down to it, use the Nightstalker pistols. If you're within twenty feet of a target, use your pointer finger along the side of the muzzle protectors. The laser sight is there if you need it. Show Kat how to use them, just in case." He paused, knowing what he was about to tell them was classified, but his love for family urged him to speak candidly, “Junior should already know how to use it. And Julia, there’s an incoming alien invasion. They will be planetside today. I’m going to be home as soon as I possibly can.”
From just inside the war room, Captain Orion overheard bits of Argus's conversation. He didn't interrupt, but a look of respect and understanding crossed his face, knowing the difficult balance between duty and family. He knew the situation was dire. Despite hearing classified information being leaked to his Lieutenant’s family, the captain understood. He would have done the same, had his family been stationed on Trident with him. Fortunately for Captain Orion, his family was in the safe confines of their estate on Veridian Prime.
Argus then addressed his children, his voice filled with warmth and concern. "Junior, Kat, I love you both so much. Junior, I need you to be strong and look after Mom and Kat. Kat, help your brother and listen to Mom, okay?"
"Dad, the news said aliens... There are lights in the sky. I'm scared," Junior's voice was small and filled with fear.
"Listen to me, both of you. Be brave, help Mom, and look out for each other. I'll be home as soon as I can. You're strong, and I'm proud of you," Argus reassured them, his heart aching with each word.
"Mark, please be safe. We'll be waiting," Julia whispered.
"I will. Stay safe, lock the doors, and remember, I love you all," Argus responded, ending the call with a heavy heart.
Taking a moment to compose himself, Argus stepped back into the war room, his expression stoic but his eyes betraying deep concern. The team, focused on the upcoming tasks, didn't notice the subtle change in him. Argus rejoined them, ready to face the challenges ahead, his resolve to protect his family fueling his determination.
As the laughter died down and the team started to regain their composure, they refocused on the task at hand. 
Fifteen minutes passed in the war room at Gladius, each minute stretching out like an hour under the weight of anticipation. The distant but unmistakable sounds of the Pegasus dropships and their escorting Razor Wings landing above them finally broke the silence. The arrival of Fireteam Ironclad and Fireteam Shadowhawk signaled a shift in the atmosphere. It was time for focused action and sharp attention.
Captain Orion, a figure of authority and calm, stepped out to meet the newly arrived teams on the launch pad. The heavy thud of their boots on the metal surface echoed in the corridor as he escorted them down to the war room. Along the way, Orion offered sincere condolences for their fallen comrades, his words imbued with respect and empathy.
"If Admiral Greenwich chose you for his personal guard, then truly, you are all 'None Better'," Orion stated, his voice firm yet filled with a warm sense of conviction. His words served as a reminder of their skill and dedication, offering a measure of comfort amidst the losses they had endured.
Lieutenant Barkley and Lieutenant Charles from Ironclad and Shadowhawk walked alongside Captain Orion, their faces etched with the fatigue and intensity of their recent ordeal. They shared details of the brutal encounter with the aliens and the tragic loss of Commander Drexel. "It was hell up there," Barkley said, his voice low. "We had to make a tactical retreat to the escape pods and order a nuclear strike on The Liberation. It was the only way."
Charles added, his tone laced with a mixture of sorrow and determination, "These aliens... they're unlike anything we've faced. We need to be prepared for their brutality. But at least they can be killed." The air was charged with a mutual respect and an unspoken understanding of the sacrifices each had made.
As they entered the war room, Fireteams Valor and Honor rose to greet their fellow Praetorians. Handshakes and solemn nods were exchanged, a silent language of warriors acknowledging each other's experiences and losses.
"Welcome back," Rourke said, his usual joviality tempered with seriousness. "We heard about The Liberation. Tough break."
Harlan, standing beside Tessa, added quietly, "You did what had to be done. You can’t predict what happens when you’re in the shit."
Tessa, her gaze sweeping over the weary faces of Fireteam Ironclad and Fireteam Shadowhawk, said, "You look like hell. Let’s make them pay.”
As the others drifted back toward their seats, Argus caught Charles by the arm. He'd been watching the man since he walked in. Greenwich's own guard, the "None Better" — and they'd still had to stand by and watch everything they were sworn to protect burn. Charles had had a hand in the order to scuttle the Liberation; he'd signed away a piece of the Coalition and every soul who hadn't reached the escape pods, and Argus knew that particular look. He'd worn it himself, once.
"You made the right call up there," Argus said quietly.
Charles held his gaze a beat. "Doesn't make it weigh any less." Then, with the ghost of a tired smile, "But thanks for saying it."
The room settled into a focused hush, all eyes turning towards Captain Orion as he stepped forward to commence the briefing. The faces around him, a blend of stoicism and resolve, were a testament to the unyielding spirit of the Praetorian teams. They stood united, ready to face the next challenge, their determination fueled by the losses they had endured and the unbreakable bond they shared as defenders of their planet and their loved ones.
Captain Orion stood before the assembled Praetorians, his demeanor embodying both the gravity of the situation and the unyielding resolve of a seasoned leader. "Men, women, Praetorians," he began, his voice cutting through the tense atmosphere, "you're all now under my command. As of a few minutes ago, Admiral Greenwich's communications went completely dark. That only means one thing. The fight is coming to the surface."
The room reacted instantly; every Praetorian sat up even straighter, their eyes reflecting a mix of anger and determination. There was fear too, an acknowledgment of the unknown, but it was overshadowed by an unwavering resolve to face whatever came.
Orion continued, delving into the tactical details. "The aliens have destroyed star outposts Echo, Fox, November, Sierra and Zulu, on the outskirts of our star system. No traces left. Then they systematically disabled our long-range communications and automated planetary defense platforms. Fortunately, Admiral Greenwich managed to save a few orbital titan cannons before his fleet was overwhelmed."
He paused, letting the information sink in. "Make no mistake, we still don't know exactly what we're up against. All we know is that they're far more advanced than us, but they can bleed, and they can die! Evacuation protocols have already been successful in Epsilon City, Titan City and some outlying villages. New Eridanus and Crete City are about to be evacuated within the next 20 minutes. As a precaution, all evacuees are being transported to Atlantis Underwater Contingency Station, save for those in Crete City," he paused and looked at each of his Praetorians, “The civilians of Crete City will be immediately escorted to the emergency shuttles to get off planet.”
He looked around the room, his eyes meeting those of his soldiers. "OGDF forces and our Razor Wing squadrons are being armed to the teeth. I suggest you all refit. Look at the soldiers to your left and right. This may be the last time you see each other alive. The enemy may overrun the planet. We might all die before we can escort the civilians to safety. Remember, men, you’re our elite. The GFC’s might. We will all fight and die if we have to!"
At his words, a palpable energy surged through the room. Each Praetorian, fueled by a blend of duty and adrenaline, joined in a unifying battle chant. It was a powerful, motivating sound, echoing the resilience and fighting spirit of those prepared to defend their home against all odds. The chant, a hallmark of the Praetorian ethos, reverberated through the war room, a sound that was more than just noise – it was a declaration of their readiness to face the imminent battle, a testament to their courage and unbreakable will.
Captain Orion's voice boomed across the war room, "MEN! Praetorian Ethos! Go!" In unison, every member of the Praetorian teams stood at attention, their voices rising together in a powerful recitation of their creed. Captain Orion discreetly activated the intercom system of Gladius, broadcasting their unified chant to every soldier waiting to fight, infusing them with the same unyielding spirit.
The Praetorians, their voices resonant and clear, recited:
We are the sword.
We are the shield.
We are the fury of the Galactic Forge Coalition and the guardians of STARCOM Fleet.
In silence, we strike.
In direct contact, we overwhelm and conquer.
We are the first to fight and the last to live.
We are unbreakable, unstoppable.
We live. We fight. We die.
We are Praetorians!


“We live. We fight. We die.” Captain Orion whispered to himself. 
As the final words of the ethos echoed through the base, a palpable sense of determination and unity filled the air. The ethos, more than just words, was a manifestation of their collective spirit – a reminder of their duty, their resilience, and their commitment to defend humanity against any threat.
Captain Orion, looking around at the assembled soldiers, saw the impact of their words. The ethos had not only solidified the resolve of the Praetorians but had also inspired the rest of the soldiers on Gladius, reminding them of the importance of their fight and the strength they possessed as a united front.
The war room, now filled with a renewed sense of purpose and conviction, was ready to face whatever challenges lay ahead. The Praetorians, standing shoulder to shoulder, were a formidable force, prepared to give their all in the defense of their planet and their people.
As Captain Orion's command to refit and prepare for deployment echoed in the war room, the very foundation of the Gladius suddenly shuddered with an ominous ferocity. It started as a low rumble, almost imperceptible, but rapidly crescendoed into a violent shaking that gripped the entire structure. Dust cascaded from the ceiling like a gritty, gray waterfall, coating everything in a fine layer. The ground beneath them quivered and heaved, creating an unsettling sensation of the earth moving under their feet.
The walls groaned under the strain, and shelves lining the room rattled violently. Equipment and personal items that once sat securely were now jostled from their perches, clattering to the floor in a cacophony of noise. Glass objects shattered upon impact, adding to the growing disarray. The once orderly war room now resembled a scene of chaos, reflecting the turmoil unfolding outside.
Through the windows, the Praetorians witnessed a harrowing sight. The sky, streaked with menacing shades of red and orange, was alive with the fire of the enemy's onslaught. Explosive projectiles rained down from above, each impact sending shockwaves that were felt even within the sturdy confines of the base. In the distance, New Eridanus was engulfed in a maelstrom of destruction, its once serene skyline now marred by plumes of smoke and devastating explosions.
The severity of the situation hit with full force. Argus, momentarily paralyzed by shock, stared out the window, his heart racing with dread. Thoughts of his family, their safety now uncertain, flooded his mind. The physical shaking of the room mirrored the turmoil inside him, a tumultuous blend of fear and determination.
Captain Orion, maintaining his composure amidst the chaos, raised his voice to cut through the din. "Men! Fraggo! We take the fight to New Eridanus, now! You have ten minutes to gear up and get your asses to the launch pad. We deploy in STRIKE Transports. We are evacuating the citizens and killing every one of those alien bastards on the way out! Now move it out! Double time!”
Spurred into action by Orion's commands, the Praetorians quickly gathered their bearings amidst the trembling room. They rushed to the arms room, each step deliberate and urgent. The base continued to quiver with each new impact from the ongoing assault.
In the arms room of Gladius, the Praetorian teams engaged in a flurry of activity, each Praetorian focused on their crucial role in the impending mission. The room was alive with the sounds of preparation: the clanking of ammunition, the securing of gear, and the purposeful movement of seasoned soldiers.
Harlan immediately headed to the ammunition racks. He stocked up on specialized ammo, ensuring his Shredder was ready for the intense combat ahead. His eyes then scanned the explosives section, selecting an array of grenades and demolition charges, tools essential for both offense and defense in the unpredictable situations they might encounter. Before leaving the arms room, Harlan grabbed an extra can of his favored Epsilon Wintergreen Longcut, a small comfort in the face of uncertainty.
Each team member was equally focused on their preparations, aware that hydration and sustenance were key in prolonged engagements. They attached hydration packs to their battle armor's integrated water containment systems, ensuring easy access to water without the need to remove their helmets. Liquid food packs were swiftly loaded into their suit's dispensary systems, providing them with nutrient-rich sustenance during the mission.
Ammunition was a top priority. Every soldier grabbed as much as they could carry, mindful of the uncertainty of when they might be able to refit. The variety of ammunition reflected the diverse weaponry of the team, ranging from standard rifle rounds to specialized munitions.
As the Praetorians continued to equip themselves, there was a sense of urgency mixed with an unspoken understanding of the gravity of their mission. They exchanged brief nods and words of encouragement, a nonverbal language that spoke volumes of their camaraderie and mutual respect.
Finally, the teams were ready. Loaded with equipment and mentally prepared for the battle, they headed to the launch pad where the STRIKE Transports awaited. The atmosphere was charged with a blend of determination and anticipation as they boarded the transports, ready to confront the unknown challenges that awaited them in New Eridanus.
The launch pad at Gladius base buzzed with intense activity as the massive metal doors creaked open, revealing the prepped and ready STRIKE Transports. The imposing sight of these transports, coupled with the urgency of the mission, added to the palpable sense of determination among the Praetorian teams.
Captain Orion, his voice clear and authoritative, directed each team to their designated transport. "Valor, Honor, Ironclad, Shadowhawk, you each have your transport. Let's move out efficiently and with purpose," he commanded.
To the right of the Praetorian's STRIKE Transports, three platoons of the Orbital Ground Defense Force were boarding Pegasus Dropships. The distinctive sound of Razor Wings whirring up provided a backdrop of readiness and power.
Orion quickly pulled the Praetorian lieutenants - Lysander, Argus, Charles, and Barkley - aside and introduced them to the OGDF lieutenants. "These are your OGDF counterparts," he stated. "Coordinate with them once you're on the ground. Your first task is to secure a landing zone for the Pegasus Dropships. You'll have Razor Wing support."
Orion's gaze was stern as he continued, "We have three orbital titan cannons at our disposal. They've been reactivated, but it's likely the enemy isn't aware yet. Use them wisely and sparingly. If the enemy detects their use, they will undoubtedly target them. Make every shot count."
With the briefing concluded, the lieutenants broke from the huddle to board their transports. Argus caught Lysander's arm before they split. For a moment neither said anything — they'd run out of Crete jokes years ago. "Watch yourself up there, Sander," Argus said at last. Lysander smirked beneath his helmet. "You too. Try not to make me come bail you out." It was the kind of thing they'd said a hundred times, in a hundred safer places. Then they split for their separate ships, and that was that.
The air was thick with anticipation and focus as the STRIKE Transports and Razor Wings prepared for takeoff.
The Pegasus Dropships and Razor Wings were the first to stir into action. Their engines ignited, sending a chorus of powerful roars and high-pitched whines slicing through the air. One by one, the Pegasus Dropships, each carrying a platoon of OGDF soldiers, lifted off gracefully yet with a sense of urgency, their bulky forms surprisingly agile as they ascended into the sky. Accompanying them, the Razor Wings ascended with sleek, predatory grace, their engines emitting a piercing whir as they took their positions, ready to escort and protect the dropships.
Inside Fireteam Valor's STRIKE Transport, the atmosphere was one of tense anticipation. Each member of the team found their way to their designated drop seats, the familiar process executed with practiced efficiency. The interior of the transport was a hive of activity as the Praetorians strapped themselves in, checking their gear one last time and securing themselves for the imminent launch.
Captain Orion's voice came through the comms, clear and commanding. "All units, this is Gladius Six, comms check in sequence, over." The response was systematic and disciplined.
The OGDF platoons responded first. "Alpha Six, green, over," then "Bravo Six, green, over," followed by "Viper Six, green, over."
The Praetorians followed suit, each team acknowledging in turn. "Valor Six, green, over," came Argus's voice, calm and collected. Fireteams Honor, Ironclad, and Shadowhawk each reported in, their voices steady and resolute.
With all checks complete, Captain Orion's voice returned, laced with a gravity that underscored the magnitude of their mission. "This is it, men. The light is green. Launch in sixty seconds."
Inside the STRIKE Transport, the tension mounted as the countdown neared zero. Argus surveyed his team, each member embodying readiness and resolve. Their faces were masks of focus, a silent testament to their willingness to fight, to sacrifice, if necessary, for the mission at hand.
The timer ticked down, and at the ten-second mark, Argus braced himself, his eyes fixed on the digital readout. "Brace for launch in five... four... three... two... one," he announced, his voice steady over the hum of the engines.
At 'one', the STRIKE Transports' engines unleashed their full power, thrusting the vehicles upward with a sudden, forceful acceleration. The transports shot towards the upper atmosphere, leaving the safety of the base far behind as they rocketed towards New Eridanus.
The rapid ascent was a rush of adrenaline, the sheer force pressing each soldier into their seats. Outside, the sky transformed from a serene blue to the darker shades of the upper atmosphere, a visual marker of their transition from the known to the unknown.
As they breached the atmosphere's edge, the transports aligned for their drop, the Pegasus Dropships and Razor Wings already ahead, cutting through the sky towards the battle zone. The moment was charged with a potent mix of fear and determination, each Praetorian acutely aware of what awaited them below.
