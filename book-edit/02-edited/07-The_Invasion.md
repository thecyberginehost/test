Chapter 7 – The Invasion
Date: December 16, 2888
Time: 0700 Galactic Standard Time (GTC)
Location: Warship – GFC Vindicator | Docked on the Candidate Maintenance Platform
Docked above the planet’s atmosphere at the Candidate Maintenance Platform, the GFC Vindicator, a massive warship in the Galactic Forge Coalition's fleet, was active with preparations for the impending battle. Captain Harris stood at the helm, his commanding presence evident on the bridge. The Liberation was visible in the distance, a clear and stark figure against the backdrop of space.
The crew, a blend of ranks and expertise, worked diligently at their stations. Lieutenant Clarke, the navigations officer, was busy finalizing the course settings, while Ensign Reynolds, the pilot, ensured the ship's controls were responsive and ready. The bridge was a symphony of focused activity as they prepared for the challenges ahead.
Lieutenant Nguyen, the weapons operator, oversaw the ship’s formidable arsenal. "Bastion Missile bays are fully loaded and primed for deployment, Captain. Obliterator Cannons are on standby. All defensive systems are operational and covering The Vindicator in its entirety," he reported confidently.
Ensign Kim stated, "All communication channels are clear and secure, Captain. We’re maintaining a direct line to Liberation and planetside forces."
Lieutenant Park from engineering added, "Engine reactors are currently at seventy-five percent capacity and charging. All systems, including shields and critical operations, are functioning within optimal parameters."
Captain Harris, his gaze fixed on the vastness of space beyond the viewport, nodded at the updates. He always maintained a calm demeanor, a stabilizing force for his crew in the chaos of space warfare.
Turning to the communications officer, he asked, "Ensign Kim, are the Cerebral Pilots in the Neural Bridge? If I had to guess, we'll need them at full capacity. Ensure they have a fully mirrored connection to our bridge."
"Yes, Captain. Cerebral Pilots are in place and their systems are synced with ours. They're ready to provide enhanced maneuvering and targeting assistance when we reach full reactor capacity," Kim responded.
Captain Harris looked around the bridge at his crew, each member ready for the task ahead. He grasped his ever-present cup of coffee firmly, his eyes reflecting a mix of determination and anticipation.
"I don't know what we're going to be up against, but let's melt the bastards!" he declared, his voice resolute. It was a rallying cry, one that echoed the sentiment of every crew member on the Vindicator.
As the reactor's power steadily climbed towards full capacity, the crew of the Vindicator remained vigilant, prepared for the moment they would join the fray. With Captain Harris at the helm, they were ready to face whatever threats lay in wait, united in their mission to protect Trident and ensure the survival of humanity.
Minutes ticked by on the GFC Vindicator as the reactor hummed to full capacity. Poised to lead the fleet with a coordinated gravspace jump to the edge of the star system for a spaceborne patrol, The Vindicator and its companion warships were a formidable sight, representing humanity's might and resolve.
Captain Harris, through the bridge viewport, noticed an anomaly. A lone medium-sized warship materialized seemingly from nowhere, positioned directly in front of The Liberation's bridge. This wasn't one of their own - it was distinctly alien. The ship's design was unlike anything known to human engineering: sleek and ominous, its hull a deep, iridescent black that seemed to absorb light rather than reflect it. The vessel was adorned with angular protrusions, giving it an aggressive, predatory look. Its surface was covered in pulsating light patterns, hinting at advanced technology beyond human comprehension.
Captain Harris's initial reaction was a tight grip on his coffee cup, his knuckles whitening as a wave of anger and concern surged within him. He quickly masked his emotions, knowing well the importance of his role as a steadfast leader. His crew looked to him for guidance and assurance; showing fear or anger was a luxury he couldn't afford. With a deliberate effort, he relaxed his grip, regaining his composure with a practiced ease.
As he watched, the alien ship unleashed a barrage of energy projectiles at the Liberation's automated defenses. The precision was unnerving - the attacks were calibrated to disable the defenses without harming the station itself. Captain Harris realized they intended to keep the station intact, but for what purpose?
Then, elongated vessels began to emerge from the alien warship, moving with purpose towards The Liberation. "They're planning to board," Harris thought, a cold realization settling in. He acted swiftly, signaling the communications officer, "Get me all five GFC warships. Disengage docking sequences immediately. We move to protect The Liberation, now!"
The urgency of his command was met with immediate action. The fleet, regardless of their readiness, began to disengage docking bolts, a rapid response to counter the unexpected alien aggression. Captain Harris's leadership shone in these critical moments - calm and decisive, ready to face an enemy whose motives and capabilities were yet unknown. The Vindicator and its fleet prepared to engage, their resolve as unyielding as the steel hulls that carried them into battle.
Captain Harris, standing resolute at the center of the bridge, immediately took command of the situation, his voice carrying the weight of authority and experience. His eyes surveyed the room, meeting the focused gazes of his crew.
"Ensign Reynolds, set a course for the Liberation, maximum thrust. I want us there yesterday," he ordered, his tone leaving no room for hesitation.
Reynolds acknowledged with a crisp nod. "Setting course, Captain. Engaging maximum thrust."
"Lieutenant Clarke, I need an ETA to weapons engagement range," Captain Harris continued, turning to the navigations officer.
Clarke's fingers danced over his console, calculating rapidly. "ETA to engagement range, 15 minutes and 37 seconds, Captain."
"Ensure we have a continuous update on that timeframe," Harris added, his mind calculating the myriad possibilities of the impending confrontation.
He then turned to Lieutenant Nguyen. "Lieutenant, prime all weapons systems. I want the Obliterator Cannons and Bastion Missile bays ready to fire the moment we're in range. And get me targeting sequences and trajectories in advance. I want options the second we're within striking distance."
Nguyen responded with a determined nod, "Weapons systems priming now, Captain. We'll be ready."
Harris's gaze then shifted to Ensign Kim. "Signal the fighter bay and have the Dread Wing Fighters deployed. I want them escorting our ships and ready for any aggressive maneuvers from the enemy."
The Dread Wing Fighters, a squadron of highly maneuverable and heavily armed spacecraft, were designed for both escort and assault missions. Sleek and intimidating, they were equipped with the latest in GFC weaponry and shield technology, capable of engaging enemy ships with lethal efficiency. Their presence on the battlefield added an additional layer of protection and offensive capability to the fleet.
Kim quickly relayed the order. "Deploying Dread Wing Fighters for escort and combat readiness, sir."
"Keep a continuous channel open to all ships. I want updates every thirty seconds. We need to be in sync the moment we engage," Captain Harris commanded, his eyes scanning the room, ensuring every order was promptly executed.
His crew worked with well-practiced efficiency, each member acutely aware of the gravity of their situation. The bridge was a whirlwind of activity, but under Harris's command, it was a controlled, precise chaos, each action and decision bringing them closer to readiness for the imminent clash.
Captain Harris took a moment to glance out into the vast expanse of space, his mind focused, his resolve unshaken. "We're the shield of Trident today, we must be precise," he said, more to himself than anyone else, a silent promise to do whatever it took to safeguard Trident and its inhabitants.
As the Vindicator closed in on the engagement distance, the tense atmosphere on the bridge was suddenly pierced by the desperate calls from The Liberation. 
Admiral Drexel's voice, strained and urgent, crackled through the channel. "Mayday, mayday! We don't have much time! They're already overwhelming our forces!" His message, interspersed with static, conveyed a dire situation. "The STRIKE team is not able to hold them back!" The desperation in his voice painted a vivid picture of the chaos aboard the Liberation.
Then, a clearer communication came through, directed at Captain Orion. "Orion, I'm sending Fireteam Honor back to Gladius. The Liberation is falling, but we need every Praetorian we've got fighting these sons of bitches when they hit the ground. Godspeed down there, Orion." The background was filled with the cacophony of battle; gunfire, the chilling sound of alien weaponry, and the guttural shouts of their adversaries. 
Drexel's let out a final command before the transmission cut off, "Fall back to the central control room!" was a dark indication of their circumstances.
Captain Harris, observing the scene unfolding from The Vindicator's bridge, initially mistook the objects launching from the Liberation as warning flares. A second glance revealed the truth - it was Fireteam Honor, executing a tactical retreat back to the planet's surface to regroup at Gladius. His heart sank at the realization that the Liberation, once a symbol of their strength and resilience, was succumbing to the enemy.
Harris's grip on his cup tightened for a moment, reflecting his inner turmoil. But as Captain, he knew his role was to lead and inspire confidence. 
With a steady voice, he addressed his crew, "That's our cue. We're one minute out. Prepare for engagement. Nguyen, target their weapons and propulsion systems. We need to cripple that ship without destroying it. We may need intel from it later."
Turning to Ensign Kim, he ordered, "Keep that comms line open to the ground. We need all the information we can get. And signal the Dread Wing Fighters to protect our starboard side. We know damn well there’s more than one ship. We just need to find them."
He then faced the crew, his expression resolute. "Everyone, this is what we trained for. Stay sharp, stay focused. Let's show these invaders they picked the wrong ship to mess with."
As the Vindicator and its fleet advanced, Captain Harris and his crew braced themselves. The first real test of their mettle was moments away, and they stood ready to face it head-on.
"Twenty seconds!" Lieutenant Nguyen's voice cut through the focused silence of the Vindicator's bridge. But in the blink of an eye, a massive fleet of alien ships burst out of  gravspace, a formidable wall of unknown technology and hostility, directly in front of the Liberation and on a collision course with the small GFC fleet.
Captain Harris, his eyes widening in disbelief, dropped his coffee cup. It shattered, splattering coffee across the floor, a stark contrast to his usual composed demeanor. 
“Report!” Captain Harris ordered, “How many ships in their fleet?” 
“Sir,” Nguyen said, his voice crackling with fear, “The targeting system shows eighty warships, thousands of small fighter craft and two flagship vessels”
“Jesus, we don’t have…” Captain Harris was interrupted by his viewport, the alien fleet wasting no time, unleashing a barrage of energy projectiles towards them.
"Evasive maneuvers, now! Cerebral Pilots, you’re on!" Harris commanded, his voice cutting through the sudden onset of chaos.
In the Neural Bridge, the Cerebral Pilots, neurally connected to the ship's systems, sprang into action. Their minds melded with the ship, transforming thought into movement. As the captain and crew braced themselves, the pilots activated the starboard emergency thrusters. The Vindicator lurched violently to the side, narrowly dodging the incoming projectiles. The sudden movement shook everyone on board.They quickly regained their composure, trained to handle such high-stress situations.
However, the GFC Mastodon, a ship to their starboard, wasn't as fortunate. An energy projectile penetrated its shields through to the ship’s bridge, piercing over a kilometer of ship, to the stern. A series of explosions erupted, their muted echoes haunting the vast emptiness of space. The once mighty warship, a veteran of countless battles, disintegrated before their eyes.
The bridge of the Vindicator fell silent for a moment, the crew grappling with the shock and horror of what they had just witnessed. The enemy's technology and firepower were beyond anything they had anticipated - efficient, ruthless, and devastatingly precise.
Captain Harris, pushing down the surge of emotions, regained his focus. The reality was stark - they were vastly outnumbered, but not outwilled. "All ships, focus fire on their lead vessels. Nguyen, keep those cannons firing and target their propulsion systems. We need to slow them down any way we can!"
His voice was the anchor in the storm, rallying his crew back to the task at hand. The Vindicator and the remaining GFC ships regrouped, facing an enemy whose capabilities were now terrifyingly clear. But Captain Harris and his crew were determined to fight, to protect Trident, and to uphold the legacy of those who had just fallen.
Captain Harris, amidst the chaos of battle, turned to Ensign Kim. "Get Admiral Greenwich on the line, now!"
The line crackled to life, and Harris didn't waste a moment. "Admiral Greenwich, this is Captain Harris on The Vindicator. We need an ETA on your fleet, and let me be clear – we're going to need more than just your ships. The enemy fleet has eighty-two ships, sir." Harris's voice was firm, underscored with the gravity of their situation. 
"Understood, Captain," came Greenwich's response, equally weighted with urgency. "We're pushing our engines to the limit. Hold the line, Harris. Reinforcements are on the way. The rest STARCOM Fleet will not be joining us today. We’re all you’ve got."
With the communication line still open, Harris turned to his crew. "All hands, prepare for an aggressive assault. Nguyen, target the flagship in the lead. We need to break their formation."
Lieutenant Nguyen nodded, his fingers flying over the weapons console. "Obliterator Cannons targeting the lead flagship, Captain."
The Vindicator's Obliterator Cannons fired, the powerful blast cutting through the vacuum of space and striking an enemy ship. However, to the crew's astonishment, the ship began to repair itself, the gaping hole sealing as they watched.
"Fire again!" Harris commanded, his voice tinged with a mix of awe and determination. "Transfer twenty-five percent of reactor energy to charge the Obliterator Cannons. Target that flagship again!"
The Vindicator shuddered as power was rerouted, the cannons charging with a surge of energy. With a second, more powerful blast, the Obliterator Cannons fired. This time, the enemy ship was punctured decisively, sent spinning wildly into its adjacent fleet.
The alien ship, unable to repair itself in time, collided with four other warships. The impact was catastrophic. The damaged flagship, along with those it collided with, imploded in a silent, brilliant explosion.
A cheer erupted on the bridge of The Vindicator, but Captain Harris remained focused. "Stay focused, everyone. We haven’t won, yet. Keep firing and keep moving. We need to buy time for Greenwich's fleet to arrive."
As The Vindicator and the remaining GFC ships continued their relentless assault, the crew knew this was only the beginning of a long and brutal engagement. But with Harris at the helm, they had a fighting chance, regardless of them being vastly outnumbered. 
Captain Harris's eyes narrowed as he observed the enemy fleet's movements. Their restraint in unleashing their full might was both puzzling and unsettling. 
"What the hell are they waiting for?" he muttered under his breath, his strategic mind racing through possibilities.
His attention was abruptly drawn to a new threat - hundreds of small, agile fighters swarming out of the enemy carriers like a cloud of deadly flies. Harris's instincts kicked in immediately. “Fire the Bastion Missiles. Fire bays Alpha One through Bravo Ten. Full spread, maximum yield!"
Lieutenant Nguyen responded instantly, his voice sharp and focused. "Alpha One through Bravo Ten, aye, Captain! Firing sequence initiated!"
The Vindicator shuddered as the Bastion Missile bays opened, launching a barrage of missiles that streaked across the void towards the swarm of enemy fighters. Each missile, a marvel of military engineering, was designed to seek and destroy with lethal precision.
Captain Harris then turned to Ensign Kim. "Get our Dread Wing Fighters behind those missiles. I want them to punch through any gaps we create. Tell them to be ready for combat."
Kim's fingers flew over her console, relaying the orders. "Dread Wing Squadron, this is The Vindicator. Follow the missile barrage, engage at close range. Expect heavy resistance."
The Dread Wing Fighters, a squadron of elite pilots known for their skill and bravery, accelerated behind the missiles. They were the tip of the spear, ready to exploit any weakness in the enemy's defenses.
Captain Harris stood firm, his gaze fixed on the forward display as the Bastion Missiles and Dread Wing Fighters closed in on the enemy swarm. The Vindicator and the remaining GFC fleet braced for the enemy's response. Captain Harris, a veteran of countless battles, knew this was a critical moment. The crew of The Vindicator, under Harris's leadership, stood ready to face whatever came next.
In the heat of the battle, Captain Harris made a critical and daring decision. "Reroute fifty percent of reactor power, fifty percent of Bastion Missile power, and fifty percent of Gravspace Drive power to the Obliterator Cannons," he ordered with a steely resolve. "And ensure the Cerebral Pilots interconnect with the projectiles. I want those projectiles to explode only after impact."
The Cerebral Pilots, with their neural connections to the ship's systems, swiftly adapted to the new command. They synchronized with the Obliterator Cannons, preparing to guide the projectiles with unprecedented precision. This maneuver left The Vindicator vulnerable, turning it into a sitting duck in the middle of a brutal space battle. But Captain Harris was playing a high-stakes game, betting on the enemy's intent to study rather than destroy them, aiming to be as unpredictable as possible in the face of overwhelming odds.
The crew watched in tense anticipation as the first Obliterator Cannon round hit the enemy shield and stuck, exploding a moment later. The shield shimmered violently and then dissipated in a burst of energy. The second round made direct contact with the alien behemoth's portside, and upon impact, it exploded with ferocity, tearing a massive chunk from the ship. Enemy soldiers were ejected into the vacuum of space, some colliding against the Vindicator's bridge viewport. It was a victorious but short-lived moment.
To their dismay, the alien ship began to repair itself almost instantly. The damage was significant but seemed to barely hinder it; the shields reactivated as if nothing had happened. Captain Harris, witnessing this, realized the futility of their situation.
The intensity on The Vindicator's bridge reached a fever pitch as Captain Harris and his crew witnessed the other enemy flagship unleash a devastating energy blast that disintegrated the GFC Reign, one of their companion warships. Now, only three GFC ships, including the Vindicator, remained to hold the line until Admiral Greenwich's fleet arrived.
Realizing the aliens had caught on to his plan, Captain Harris knew they had to adapt quickly. "All ships, reroute all power to the bow Obliterator Cannons and reactors. Speed is our priority now.”
On the bridge, Harris addressed the Cerebral Pilots. "You heard me. Evasive maneuvers. Stay sharp; we're counting on you." 
The Cerebral Pilots, the elite of the GFC's arsenal, were more than up to the task. Their neural connection to the ship's systems and the crew on the bridge gave them an unparalleled overview of the battlefield.
Unbeknownst to many on the bridge, the Cerebral Pilots had a unique ability to see through the minds of the crew members, gaining a comprehensive picture of the situation. This connection also allowed them to subtly coerce the bridge crew into executing their commands, ensuring that evasive maneuvers and weapon firings were perfectly timed.
As The Vindicator and the remaining two GFC ships executed their new strategy, the Cerebral Pilots were in their element. The ships danced through space, dodging the relentless barrage of enemy fire with a grace and precision that belied their size. The Cerebral Pilots' extraordinary capabilities were on full display, their actions synchronized with the crew's movements and decisions.
Meanwhile, the Obliterator Cannons, now fully charged and bolstered by the redirected power, were ready. "Fire at my command," Harris ordered, his eyes fixed on the enemy command ship. "We need to break those shields."
The cannons roared to life, unleashing a formidable assault on the alien command ship's shields.
Captain Harris, standing resolute amidst the chaos, knew that every second they held the enemy at bay was a second gained for the ground forces and civilians. It was a desperate, valiant effort, but one that epitomized the courage and determination of the Galactic Forge Coalition’s best in the STARCOM Fleet.

He strode to the viewport, surveying the enemy fleet. It was clear that this was a battle they could not win. The only option left was to buy time for the ground forces and the evacuation of civilians from Trident.
Turning to Lieutenant Clarke, his voice was firm with resolve. "Plot a course for that command ship. We're going to ram it,” he paused, and without hesitation addressed Ensign Reynolds, “Reynolds, full speed ahead!"
A murmur of disbelief swept through the bridge, but Harris was resolute. He walked to Ensign Kim’s station and picked up the ship-wide communications system, addressing his crew with the gravitas of a captain facing the end. 
"Listen up, men and women of the GFC Vindicator, this is Captain Harris. I'll make this quick and to the point. I won’t be coy. We won't be making it out of here alive, no matter what we do. The only thing we can do now is take as many of these sons of bitches with us to hell with us, as we can! I want all crew members to their battle stations. All OGDF and Orbital Infantry forces, you need to get planetside. Link up at Gladius with the others. They’ll need it! Good luck everyone. I’ll see you on the other side. Captain Harris, out!"
Without hesitation, the crew sprang into action, their movements sharp and focused. The OGDF and Orbital Infantry forces quickly began their evacuation, heading towards the escape pods that would take them planetside. Meanwhile, the rest of the crew prepared for their final, desperate maneuver.
The Vindicator, now on a collision course with the alien command ship, surged forward. Every crew member knew what this meant. A final act of defiance. A last stand in the face of an overwhelming enemy.
As the distance closed, The Vindicator's crew held their breath, their eyes fixed on the view ahead. Captain Harris stood at the helm, his gaze unwavering, ready to face the end with the same courage and determination he had shown throughout the battle.
The final moments were tense, the silence on the bridge punctuated only by the hum of the engines and the distant sounds of battle. Then, with a resolute determination, the Vindicator charged towards its target, a beacon of bravery and sacrifice in the darkest of times.
The Vindicator and the remaining GFC warships inched closer to their target, the alien command ship looming large in their viewports. Captain Harris, his face etched with determination, issued a critical order. "Make sure we have our rear emergency thrusters ready and at two-hundred percent capacity. We only have one shot at this. If we fail, we die for nothing."
The bridge crew responded with swift efficiency, adjusting the thrusters for the impending collision. "Only five kilometers until impact," Clarke said, his voice steady despite the gravity of their situation. "ETA on contact?" Asked the Captain.
"ETA one minute, Captain," Lieutenant Clarke responded.
That minute stretched out, each second heavy with the knowledge of their impending sacrifice. The crew members, aware of their fate, worked with a quiet intensity, driven by the hope that their actions would buy crucial time for Trident's defense forces to prepare and re-enable the automated defenses and communications.
"Push the reactors to three-hundred capacity," Harris ordered.
The reactors hummed with a dangerous intensity as they were pushed beyond their limits. 
"Fifteen seconds until impact," Harris said, his voice betraying a hint of sweat on his brow. "Fire the Obliterator Cannon!"
The first shot rang out, a brilliant beam of destructive energy, but the alien shield held. "Fire again!" he commanded. The second shot hit, and this time, the shield broke, flickering out of existence.
"Now! Rear thrusters, full force!" Harris shouted. 
As The Vindicator’s rear thrusters activated, the alien ship unleashed a final, devastating attack. A beam of light, so wide it encapsulated all three GFC warships, shot forth.
Time seemed to slow for the members of The Vindicator. The beam engulfed them, a blinding, inescapable force. Captain Harris's final thoughts were filled with regret, realizing too late that they had been lured into a trap. Their sacrifice, he feared, had been in vain.
From a distance, Admiral Greenwich and his fleet, having just exited gravspace, witnessed the obliteration of The Vindicator and its companion ships. The sight was harrowing, a stark reminder of the brutal reality of war.
Greenwich, a seasoned veteran, stood on the bridge of his flagship the GFC Eden with Cuban cigar clenched between his teeth. These cigars were a rare commodity, reserved for special occasions or moments of impending doom. As he watched the last stand of his old friend Captain Harris and the brave crew of The Vindicator, a mix of emotions washed over him; respect for their valiant effort, sorrow for their loss, and a deepened resolve for the battle that lay ahead.
The Admiral took a long, slow drag on the cigar, its smoke curling up into the filtered air of the bridge. This moment, witnessing the destruction of a significant portion of the GFC fleet, marked a turning point. The Obsidians had shown their might, and now it was time for Admiral Greenwich and his fleet to respond with all the force and cunning at their disposal.
"Set condition one throughout the fleet. Prepare all ships for engagement," Admiral Greenwich ordered, his voice carrying the weight of command and a hint of the heavy burden he now shouldered. "Let’s give them hell! All ships, advance! All crewmembers to your battle stations!"
As the fleet moved forward, the crews steeled themselves for the battle. They knew the odds they faced, but the sacrifice of The Vindicator and its crew had given them something more potent than any weapon – a cause worth fighting for, and a memory to honor.
The Eden, a formidable warship and the pride of the STARCOM Fleet, was a marvel of military engineering and strategic design, dwarfing its companions in both size and capability.
The GFC Eden's defensive and offensive capabilities were unparalleled. Its hull was reinforced with the latest in armor technology, capable of withstanding intense firepower. The ship boasted advanced shielding, three times more robust than any other warship, a testament to Greenwich's connections within GRID. This shielding could absorb and dissipate energy from even the most potent enemy attacks, making the Eden a near-impregnable fortress in space.
Offensively, the Eden was equipped with five Titan Cannons, each three times as powerful as the standard Obliterator Cannon. These cannons were capable of delivering devastating blows to enemy ships, tearing through shields and armor with ease. Their range and precision were unmatched.
Unlike traditional GFC ships, the Eden's crew was unique. The cerebral pilots, typically stationed on a separate Neural Bridge, were integrated directly into the ship's main bridge. This integration allowed for unprecedented synchronization between the ship's systems and the pilots' neural commands.
Furthermore, the bridge was staffed not just with standard crew members but with some of the most powerful Mindreavers known to the GFC. These Mindreavers, capable of extraordinary feats of mental prowess, controlled every aspect of the ship. In times of critical need, they could even extend their influence to Admiral Greenwich himself, enhancing his decision-making and strategic planning capabilities through a shared neural link.
Admiral Greenwich, amidst the chaos of battle, turned to his crew, his voice cutting through the din of the bridge. "Report. I need the status of any remaining GFC warships."
A pause filled the air, laden with tension, as the Mindreaver responsible for telepathic communications through the radio systems concentrated, extending his senses across the battlefield. 
Moments later, he turned to face Greenwich, his expression grave. "Admiral, there are no signs of life from the other GFC warships. It appears we are the only fleet left in space."
The weight of this news settled heavily upon the bridge. The loss was more than just material; it was a profound blow to the morale and fighting spirit of those who remained. Admiral Greenwich's fleet, though formidable, was now the last line of defense between the Obsidian invaders and the planet Trident.
Greenwich absorbed the news with a stoic calm, though the strain was evident in his eyes. "Very well. Then it's down to us and the ground forces," he stated resolutely. "We must hold the line here and give our troops planetside every chance to fortify their positions and protect the civilians."
Turning to his communications officer, Greenwich ordered, "Coordinate with the ground forces. I want continuous updates on their status and any support they need from us."
As the Eden and its accompanying ships engaged the enemy, the Mindreavers worked tirelessly, their mental abilities stretched to their limits. They coordinated the fleet's movements, communicated with the ground forces, and assisted in targeting the powerful Titan Cannons. Their presence on the bridge brought a level of synchronicity and responsiveness that was crucial in the heat of battle.
Admiral Greenwich, standing at the forefront of the last bastion of the GFC's space forces, knew the odds were against them. But he also knew the resilience and determination of his crew and the ground forces. They would fight to their last breath to protect Trident.
Admiral Greenwich, formulating a clear plan in mind, needed to create a diversion to prevent any Obsidian ships from targeting the corvettes on their critical mission. His objectives were precise; the corvettes would use a single emergency thruster for a calculated directional push, then cut off all power, relying on the cerebral pilot assigned to each corvette to control their direction and speed. This stealth approach was a stroke of strategic brilliance, significantly increasing their chances of reaching the orbital Titan cannons undetected.
Leaving the bridge in the capable hands of the Mindreavers, still neurally linked to him for quick communication, Greenwich headed to the quarters of The Eden’s Praetorians. He used the Fasttrack, a rapid transport system capable of moving in multiple directions, designed for swift movement throughout the large ship.
Upon arriving at the barracks, he was pleased to see two Praetorian teams already fully armored and stocking up on ammunition. Staff Sergeant Flannery, noticing his arrival, barked, "Officer on deck!"
"At ease, son," Admiral Greenwich replied calmly. 
He then addressed Lieutenant Charles and Lieutenant Barkley, the Praetorian team leaders. "I have a critical mission for your teams. You are to board the Liberation. Your objectives are to assess the situation, evacuate any survivors, eliminate all Obsidian forces on board, and retake the station. If the Liberation cannot be secured, we'll have no choice but to scuttle it. The data and control systems on that station cannot fall into enemy hands."
The teams listened intently, understanding the gravity of their task. "Understood, Admiral," Lieutenant Barkley responded, his tone resolute. "Fireteams Shadowhawk and Ironclad will not let you down."
With the mission parameters clear, the Praetorian teams prepared to depart. Admiral Greenwich gave them a final nod of approval. "Move out with speed and stealth. Trident is counting on you."
As the teams left to execute their mission, Admiral Greenwich returned to the bridge, ready to oversee the diversionary tactics that would give the corvettes and the two Praetorian teams the best chance of success.
