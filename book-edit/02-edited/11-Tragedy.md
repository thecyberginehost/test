Chapter 11 – Tragedy
Date: December 16, 2888
Time: 1335 Galactic Standard Time (GTC)
Location: Lieutenant Argus's Home, Outskirts of New Eridanus

The room was still and quiet following the end of the call with her husband, Mark. Julia looked at her children, seeing in their young faces a mix of confusion and fear. 
Junior broke the silence with a question that reflected the innocence and concern of a child his age, "Mom, is it true? Are the aliens here to fight us?"
Julia knelt down to their level, her expression serious yet gentle. "Yes, they are, Junior," she replied, her voice steady. "And we have to defend ourselves. I know you're both afraid but remember that your dad is fighting right now to get to us."
Her words seemed to instill a sense of purpose in the children, though the fear didn't completely leave their eyes. Julia then guided them to a secure cabinet, explaining the importance of knowing how to handle the weapons safely and correctly.
As she went through the safety procedures with a Nightstalker pistol, Junior's attention shifted. "Can we get Dad's Battlepulse rifle?" he asked, his tone surprisingly determined. Julia was taken aback by the request, but before she could respond, Junior was already heading to their master bedroom.
He approached the bookshelf and jumped up to reach the top shelf. He confidently pulled on a very old novel called "Starship Troopers." The shelf gave way slightly, revealing the entrance to a hidden, secure room. Junior's familiarity with this secret spot spoke of the many times he must have watched his father.
Inside the secure room, a vault stood, housing an array of weapons – trophies and tools from Argus's previous deployments. Among them were a long-range KA-2B Sniper Rifle, an M56 Battlepulse Rifle, another set of P19-HE Nightstalker pistols, and a prototype magnetic railgun that Mark had been building in his spare time when he was home on leave. Junior's eyes were drawn to the Battlepulse Rifle. It was clear he was familiar with it; Julia recalled, with a mix of pride and apprehension, how Argus, against her wishes, had taken Junior to the range just last month to practice with it.
"Mom, I can use this," Junior said, a hint of eagerness in his voice, mixed with the seriousness of the situation. "Dad showed me how."
Julia watched her son, torn between the desire to keep him safe and the understanding that these were dangerous times, calling for dangerous measures. She nodded slowly, realizing that their survival might depend on such skills. "Okay, Junior. But we have to be very careful. These are dangerous, and we only use them if we absolutely have to."
As she helped Junior with the Battlepulse Rifle, ensuring he remembered the safety protocols and handling techniques, Julia couldn't help but feel a swell of maternal pride. Her children, so young, were adapting to a reality far beyond their years, showing courage that belied their age. She silently vowed to do everything in her power to keep them safe, to see them through this ordeal, and to reunite them with their father.
Junior, with a determination far beyond his eight years of age, gripped the Battlepulse Rifle, its size and weight nearly overwhelming for his small frame. He struggled momentarily, the weapon nearly three-quarters his size, before a realization hit him. "The tripod!" he exclaimed. He had almost forgotten. The last time he held the rifle, his father had attached a tripod to allow Junior to prop the weapon up in a much more stable position.
He quickly retrieved the tripod from the weapons rack and expertly affixed it to the rifle. The addition made the weapon more manageable, and Junior's face lit up with a sense of accomplishment. Julia watched, a mix of surprise and pride washing over her. Her son, still so young, had absorbed more from his father than she had realized.
Kat, on the other hand, was more reluctant. At six years old, the very idea of handling weapons was intimidating. She stood back, her small body tense with apprehension. Julia gently encouraged her, understanding her fear but also recognizing the necessity of familiarity with the weapons for their safety.
"It's okay, Kat. I'll protect you," Junior said, turning to his sister with a protective gaze. His words were simple but filled with the deep bond of sibling love.
Hearing this, Julia felt a warmth spread through her heart, a bittersweet mix of love and worry. She wished more than anything that Mark, their father, were here. He would know exactly what to do, how to make them feel safe. But in his absence, it was up to her, and she drew strength from her children's bravery.
"Alright, let's make sure we're all as prepared as we can be," Julia said, her voice steady. "We're going to stay together, stay hidden, and wait for your dad. He's doing everything he can to come back to us."
Together, they reviewed basic safety protocols once more, Julia ensuring that both Junior and Kat understood the gravity of the situation.
As Julia finalized the preparations with the children, the eerie calm of their home was shattered by the thunderous sound of explosions. The noise was deafening, a cacophony of destruction that seemed to come from all around them. Suddenly, the house began to shake violently, as if caught in the grip of a powerful earthquake. The constant barrage of explosions sent shockwaves through their home, rattling the windows and causing the walls to tremble.
An urgent, loud emergency alarm then pierced through the chaos, its wail echoing throughout the city. This alarm, a harbinger of imminent danger, sent a chill down Julia’s spine.
Acting on instinct, she rushed outside the secure room to the nearest window, her heart pounding in her chest as her children followed suit. The sight that greeted her was one of apocalyptic horror. New Eridanus was under a reign of fire, a devastating barrage from above. She saw houses down the street being obliterated by blue beams of light and explosive ordnance, each impact a violent eruption that tore the structures apart.
Realizing the imminent danger, Julia sprang into action, herding Kat and Junior towards the secure room. Junior struggled with the Battlepulse Rifle, its weight significant for his young age, but he gripped it tightly, determined to carry it himself. Kat's face was pale, her eyes wide with terror, barely comprehending the destruction unfolding around them.
They raced to the secure room, each step heavy with the realization of the danger they were in. The secure room, fortified with three feet of concrete on all sides, offered their only sanctuary. Julia ushered her children inside, her movements quick and purposeful. The room's air vent, leading underground before emerging outside, ensured a constant supply of breathable air, while the sophisticated air filter system blocked any toxic substances, including radiation.
Once inside, Julia locked the secure room door, sealing them away from the nightmare outside. The sounds of their house being torn apart by the alien assault penetrated even the thick concrete walls of the secure room. The crashes and the explosions bled through the concrete in a muffled, ceaseless roar — the sound of their home coming apart just beyond the walls.
In the secure room, fear gripped them all. Kat screamed, her small body trembling uncontrollably. Julia and Junior wrapped their arms around her, trying to offer comfort amidst the chaos. "It's going to be okay," Julia whispered repeatedly, her voice a shaky, but determined anchor in the storm of fear and uncertainty.
Julia looked at Junior, seeing in him the reflection of his father's bravery and strength. Despite the fear in his eyes, he was resolute, a pillar of support for both his mother and sister. In this moment, Julia saw the best qualities of Mark in their son, a mixture of pride and sorrow filling her heart.
Huddled together, they waited, listening to the sounds of their world falling apart. All they could do was hope, cling to each other, and believe in the promise of safety amidst the terror of the invasion.
The sudden cessation of the barrage left an eerie silence in its wake. Julia, her heart pounding, activated the Holocom connected to their home’s security system. Of the fifteen cameras installed around the property, only three flickered to life on the screen. The others, she assumed, had fallen victim to the devastating attack. The images from the functioning cameras revealed a scene of utter ruin. Her once peaceful street was now a nightmarish landscape of destruction, with debris and unrecognizable remains scattered amidst the wreckage of what used to be homes. Survivors, their movements frantic and disoriented, scurried amid the ruins, a vivid display of panic and despair.
After a few tense minutes, Julia made a decision. She needed to see the extent of the damage for herself. Turning to Junior, she instructed firmly, "Stay here and protect Kat. I'll be right back." Despite the fear in her children's eyes, she knew she must assess the situation outside.
Stepping out of the secure room and into what remained of their home, the sight that greeted her was overwhelming. The familiar comforts of their house were unrecognizable, torn apart by the alien assault. She moved cautiously towards the door, her every step heavy with apprehension.
Outside, the scale of the catastrophe was even more harrowing. The neighborhood she knew and loved was unrecognizable, transformed into a war zone. The air was thick with smoke and the acrid smell of burning homes. Julia's heart ached at the sight, a deep sense of loss enveloping her.
Suddenly, a massive shadow engulfed the area. Julia looked up, her eyes widening in horror. A colossal alien warship, its design menacing and unfamiliar, flew overhead. Its immense size cast a long, dark shadow, enveloping the neighborhood in an ominous gloom. The ship slowed, then hovered in the sky, a silent but threatening presence.
Without warning, a beam of energy erupted from the ship, striking the ground a few kilometers away. The impact sent a shockwave rippling through the area, shaking the ground under Julia's feet. She stumbled, catching herself against the remnants of a wall.
Then she saw them – hundreds of objects descending from the ship, growing larger as they approached the planet's surface. They resembled pods, each one a harbinger of further invasion. Julia's mind raced, "No. No, no, no.” She said quickly, “They’re coming to us."
Her thoughts turned desperately to Mark. "Mark, where the hell are you? We need you." She recalled the stories he shared, the information about his missions he was permitted to tell. She understood enough to recognize the vessels as transport ships, likely carrying the alien invaders.
Unknown to Julia, at that very moment, Mark was being transported to New Eridanus, racing against time to join the battle for their planet. But for Julia, standing amidst the ruins of her neighborhood, watching the alien forces descending from the sky, the situation seemed hopeless. Fear, determination, and a deep longing for her husband's safe return mingled in her heart as she retreated back to the safety of the secure room to her waiting children.
Inside, she locked the door once more, her face a mask of resolve as she comforted Kat and Junior. But in her eyes, there was a glimmer of fear for what was to come, a fear for their future, and a silent plea for Mark's return.
The systematic booms that now echoed outside were a chilling harbinger of the alien presence. Julia, her heart pounding with dread, quickly glanced at the Holocom's camera feed. The screen revealed a nightmarish scene unfolding just outside their fortified sanctuary.
An alien transport crashed into the ground with terrifying force, creating a massive crater where her neighbor's yard once was. The door of the transport burst open, and four alien soldiers emerged. Their appearance was menacing, their movements swift and lethal. They opened fire indiscriminately at any survivors in sight, their guttural shouts filling the air with a sound that was both alien and terrifying.
Across the street, another transport struck the ground, and similarly, four more aliens burst out. They moved with deadly efficiency, shooting and shouting, their tactical movements coordinated and precise.
Kat, who had been watching the feed secretly, turned to her mother, her young face etched with fear. "Mommy, they're here now. Are we going to die?" Kat’s voice filled with fear as she struggled to get her words out.
Julia pulled Kat close, wrapping her arms around her daughter in a protective embrace. She gently kissed Kat’s forehead, trying to mask her own fear as she responded with as much calmness as she could muster. "Not a chance," she whispered, her voice steady but her heart racing with fear.
Kat continued to cling to Julia, her small body trembling uncontrollably. Julia extended her left arm out towards Junior. He embraced his mother and Kat without hesitation. Julia held her children tightly, her mind racing for a way out, a plan to keep them safe.
The fear they felt was overwhelming – the fear of the unknown, of the alien soldiers outside, of what might happen next. Julia’s thoughts were a whirlwind of worry for Mark, for their safety, and for the future, “If there is a future,” she thought.
Yet, in the midst of this terror, her resolve to protect her children at all costs only strengthened. She whispered words of comfort to them, trying to believe in her own reassurances, as they waited, hoping for a miracle in the face of seemingly insurmountable odds.
The situation escalated to a terrifying climax when an alien transport landed directly in their yard. The impact was felt within the fortified walls of the secure room, a violent tremor that sent a shockwave of fear through them all. Julia watched the Holocom's feed, her heart sinking as she saw the alien soldiers emerge from the transport. Unlike the others, these aliens were not immediately firing their weapons; instead, they began a methodical search, moving with a chilling purposefulness. It was clear they were hunting for survivors – for anyone left to kill.
The stark realization that hit Julia was paralyzing. They were trapped, with no escape. The secure room, once a haven of safety, now felt like a tomb waiting to be sealed. She watched the screen, her eyes wide with horror, as the aliens methodically scoured the area.
"This is it. If they find us in here, this is it," she thought to herself, a sense of despair washing over her. The very thought of her children being harmed was unbearable, a fear so profound it eclipsed everything else.
Junior and Kat sensed their mother's fear, and it amplified their own. Junior placed a grip on the Battlepulse Rifle and tightened it as hard as he could, while Kat buried her face in her mother's side, Sergeant Hops crushed to her chest, her small body shaking with faint cries.
Julia held her children close, her mind racing for any possible solution, any sliver of hope. But the grim reality of their situation was inescapable. The only thing she could do now was wait, pray, and hope that the aliens didn't discover their hiding place.
In the silence of the secure room, punctuated only by the distant sounds of the alien search and Kat's quiet sobs, Julia closed her eyes and thought of Mark. She wondered if he was safe, if he even knew what was happening to them. The helplessness of the moment was overwhelming, but she clung to the faint hope that somehow, they might still make it through this alive.
The distant, alien cacophony crept closer, an unsettling symphony of guttural commands and the methodical plundering of their home. Through the encroaching terror, Junior didn't break. With hands far too small for it, he wrestled the Battlepulse rifle up onto the table — a weapon built for grown men, unwieldy and complicated in a child's grip. But his jaw was set the way his father's always got before something hard. He steadied the barrel toward the door and waited, ready to fire on anything that came through it that wasn't Human.
"Don't worry, Mom and Kat," he declared, his voice a mixture of bravery and innocence, "I'll keep us safe."
Julia watched, her heart swelling with a tumultuous blend of pride and dread. Junior's determination mirrored his father's unyielding spirit, a trait that had always been Mark's beacon in the darkest times. Kat's embrace of her brother, a physical manifestation of their unbreakable bond, brought a surge of warmth to the cold fear enveloping Julia's heart.
"I love you mommy. I love you Junior," Kat whispered, a simple declaration that carried the weight of their world. Tears breached Julia's resolve as the alien noises intensified, a stark reminder of their precarious reality. Her family's courage in the face of such terror only deepened her fear for their safety. 
The tension increased as the alien's efforts to find Human survivors reached a fever pitch, the noise abruptly halting, casting a shadow of ominous silence.
Junior, with a wisdom beyond his years, muttered, "I think they found us," his small fingers expertly flipping the Battlepulse rifle's selector from safe to automatic, the sound of the soft click echoing in the room.
Julia, embodying the protector, gripped her Nightstalker pistol with a resolve forged in desperation, aiming at the door, her heart racing in anticipation.
Suddenly, a sinister red energy, reminiscent of a sword or handheld weapon, sliced through the door, but didn't bring it down. 
The sight sent Kat scurrying for cover under the table, her brother's calm whisper, "Not yet," her only solace. 
Julia watched, filled with fear and pride as she marveled at Junior's tactical patience, a skill imparted by Mark, now manifesting in their son's poised readiness.
The aliens, thwarted by the door's resilience, unleashed a barrage of frustrated attempts before tearing it down with a device that emitted a high-frequency sonic pulse, designed to disintegrate molecular bonds. 
The door crumbled with a high-pitched noise, and Junior's voice cut through the tension, "Now!"
Junior squeezed the trigger and unleashed a loud, unnerving scream as the rounds left the barrel. His shots were precise, each one finding its mark, toppling the first alien in a spray of unearthly blood. The remaining assailants, caught off-guard, scrambled for cover, one limping from a well-placed shot by Junior.
Amidst the chaos, Julia found herself frozen, the pistol's weight meaningless in her hands as fear paralyzed her. Kat's scream pierced the air, a raw expression of terror. In this moment of turmoil, Julia's instincts as a mother overrode her warrior's resolve, turning to console her daughter, embodying both the protector and the nurturer in the face of an alien threat.
In the heat of the moment, with the alien forces breaching the last bastion of their safety, Julia's instincts kicked into overdrive. Julia watched in what felt like slow motion as a glowing orb arched through the air, its ominous glow forecasting doom. She lunged at Junior, their bodies colliding with the ground in a protective embrace. Her actions were swift, driven by a mother's desperation to shield her offspring from the unknown horrors of alien warfare. With a strength fueled by adrenaline, she flipped the heavy table upright with ease, creating a makeshift barrier that enveloped all three in a cocoon of relative safety beside them. The orb emitted a high-pitched shriek, before unleashing its fury with a deafening boom. 
Julia compared the sound to an explosion happening underwater. The blast didn't roar but whistled, a sound alien in its essence as it devoured the Battlepulse rifle in a voracious display of heat. The room sweltered, the air crackled, and the table succumbed, splintering under the force.
The aftermath left them in disarray. Kat and Junior, dazed and bewildered, struggled to comprehend the chaos. The aliens, undeterred, advanced, their weapons spitting deadly energy towards the fragile remnants of their cover. Julia immediately picked up her Nightstalker pistol and returned fire. Her shots, though desperate and inaccurate, forced the attackers into a momentary retreat. Seizing this fleeting opportunity, she rallied her children, herding them towards the cache of weapons they had prepared for such an apocalyptic scenario.
Amidst the frenzy, a fortunate explosion rent a hole in the secure room's reinforced walls, offering a sliver of escape into the ravaged skeleton of their home. Junior, emboldened, scavenged an alien weapon from the fallen foe he had valiantly killed. The weapon, unfamiliar to operate, confounded him until desperation guided his fingers to the activation mechanism. He fired the weapon sporadically in the direction he heard the alien sounds. Junior could feel the heat from the energy projectiles blasting out of the weapon.
Emerging from the smoke behind him, one of the aliens latched onto the back of his shirt, grabbing what felt like a fistful of his clothing. Junior was hurled with terrifying force into the crumbling remains of their once-safe haven. The impact stole his consciousness, leaving him vulnerable and exposed.
Julia's heart shattered at the sight, her scream a raw, primal sound that tore through the chaos. "Junior! No! You bastard, I'll kill you!" Her challenge was met with a guttural mockery from the alien, a sound that chilled the soul.
Fueled by rage and desperation, Julia reached for the nearest weapon in their arsenal, the prototype magnetic railgun. She hefted the weapon, the weight surprisingly comfortable in her hands. With a cry that merged grief and fury, she unleashed its potential, the weapon's electromagnetic pulse silently hurling projectiles with lethal precision towards the nearest enemy.
In the aftermath of the magnetic railgun's silent verdict, an alien stumbled and fell, uncomprehending of its sudden end. 
Julia, her resolve as unwavering as the steel of her fallen weapon, faced the last enemy. She threw the railgun and pulled out her pistol, pointing it at the final assailant. The creature acted quickly, dodging her pistol rounds with an unnerving grace, its movements a blur of contemptuous agility. It descended upon her, disarming and overpowering her with effortless malice. He knocked the weapon from her hands, while simultaneously wrapping its own hand around her neck. The alien lifted her up off the ground, its hand still tightly wrapped around Julia’s neck. 
It taunted, "Pathetic Humans,” in a low, guttural tone.
Kat, ignited by a raw, untamed rage, launched herself at the monster, her small fists and kicks a flurry of futile defiance. The alien was unconcerned with the benign assault. Her attack, relentless and brave, found a moment of opportunity; a shard of glass, a remnant of their shattered haven, became her weapon. With a cry fueled by fear and anger, she drove the shard into the alien's knee, exploiting a small gap in its armor. The alien's response was swift and brutal, a scream of pain followed by an aggressive kick that sent Kat flying through the air. She let out a piercing scream, the back of her head hitting a large piece of concrete debris as her body sprawled to the ground. She fell silent. The one-eared rabbit slipped from her arms and rolled to a stop in the ash.
Julia started to kick and scream as hard as she could, a fearful and helpless feeling washing over her, knowing she could not save Kat, but the alien just stood there, hand still wrapped around her throat, laughing coldly. Julia managed to strike a blow to the alien’s head, its grip on her throat slightly loosening. Without warning, she saw the alien ignite an energy dagger in its other hand and immediately plunge it into the side of her abdomen. She was silenced as the weapon pierced her, burning its way through her insides.
From the haze of near-unconsciousness, Junior's gaze locked onto a scene of despair. The sight of his mother, impaled by the alien's energy dagger, and the lifeless body of his sister, ignited a primal scream from his lips, a plea woven with pain and disbelief.
Junior's scream, "Mommy! Kat! No!" pierced the chaos, a son's anguished refusal to accept the unimaginable.
With trembling hands, he navigated the ground to find the nearest weapon. He stumbled upon the same weapon they used to penetrate their secure room. His hand found purchase around the hilt as he stood up, his body trembling with fear and rage. Junior activated the alien weapon, as if it was only his thoughts that had activated the destructive energy blade. He could feel the heat of it rolling off the blade as he charged the alien, driving it through the thing's guard and into its chest.
The creature shrieked, a wet and ragged sound of shock and agony. In its final moments, it retaliated, a vicious swipe that sent him crashing into a nearby pillar. The structure gave way, burying him beneath its weight.
The alien, now grievously wounded, staggered forward, each step a testament to its faltering strength. It collapsed, a mere few paces from where it stood, its blood pooling on the cold ground.
Amidst the ruins, Julia felt her own blood going warm and slow beneath her. Her children were quiet now — both of them, quiet. She tried to hold their faces in her mind and found that she could, even here: Junior at the dinner table with a fork still in his hand, Kat and that ridiculous one-eared rabbit. The last thing she did, as the dark closed over her, was think of Mark. Come home, she told him, wherever he was. Come home and make them pay.
