## Transcript (normalized)

Above the council hall of the Primordial Architects, stars pulsed like tired hearts across the galactic map projected in silence. Tendrils of crimson laced the spiral arms. Sectors lost, worlds drowned, souls consumed. It was a slow death, but a certain one.

In the center of the room stood **Xylaris**. Cracks lined his pale gray skin like fault lines on a dying world. His eyes, ever burning, held galaxies of memory within them. The others stood in silence — not from reverence, but because they knew he remembered things they feared to learn.

"Six months," came the voice of **Aetheris**, his armor's ancient AI. "Until full assimilation. Eight at most. The Void will not wait."

The council tensed. The stars seemed dimmer. Xylaris's voice finally broke the silence — low, layered, like echoing truth down a corridor of time.

"The Void, as relentless as they are, do not fully understand what they do or why they do it." He let the words sit, heavy and alien. "Its corruption is born of evil, yet those that were initially consumed into it were not. It is born of belief — a belief etched into the core of its essence, a directive given to it by the hands of the Ancient Ones. The very hands once mistaken for gods."

No one dared interrupt.

"Before the Primordial Architects, as you well know, there was the Celestian Pact. You have all been searching and discovering artifacts from this ancient civilization. I knew them. I have lived among them. They were a civilization of immense beauty and knowledge. They sought what we all crave — ascension, transcendence, freedom from the dying of stars."

He took a step forward, eyes burning brighter now. "But they were not the first to want this. The Ancient Ones found them in their height — entities no longer bound to form, wearing the skin of light and promise. They whispered falsehoods in words shaped like truth."

**Kar'yan Voss's** throat tensed, but he did not speak. This was not his story to tell.

"The Pact believed them. They enacted the ritual of passage, a sacred ascension rite. But it was a lie. They did not rise; they fractured. Their entire collective mind was drawn into a singularity of corrupted will. One mind, one body, one essence." He exhaled sharply. "They became the Void."

Xylaris looked to **Mojah Din'Sha**, to **Kuja‑Wo'Shuk**, to **Kar'yan Voss** — his companions in this final hour. "It does not hunger as we understand it. It seeks union. It believes that by consuming us, it fulfills a directive written into its life essence — that this is the only path forward for existence. It is not evil. It is deluded — which I believe is even more dangerous, as you have already observed."

Silence again. Kar'yan stepped forward, the flicker of pain in his face unmoving. "I volunteer to command the **Eternal Blade**," he said. "To fly into **Solaris Magna** and ignite the core. I will be at the epicenter of the chain reaction." His hands trembled — whether from duty or from the memory of his wife **Taryn**, now the Hive Mind's flesh‑bound host, no one could say.

**Mojah Din'Sha** stepped forward next. "We can't just act. The Void will notice — sense movement in the stars." He turned to the others. "We give it a battle. A spectacle. A final stand."

**Kuja‑Wo'Shuk** nodded. "While we fight here, the true strike lands in shadow."

Xylaris remained still. "Once the Eternal Blade activates **Ignis Protocol**, the stars will begin their deaths. The Void will be drawn to it, unable to resist the gravitational pull of the **Cascade**. That is when we enter **Etherspace** — Aetheris guiding our path — and cross into **Gravspace**."

Aetheris added with emotionless precision: "Synchronization must fall within ±3 milliseconds. Any deviation results in cascade failure."

"And if we fail?" **Gralthar** asked.

Xylaris answered without pause. "Then the Void tears open the Veil. It will breach the interdimensional boundary into which the Ancient Ones are held, and awaken them. The Ancient Ones will return — and what we now call death will be mercy."

No one spoke after that.

Then came a sound. A low hum, a resonance not heard but felt. The far side of the Sanctum bent inward, the light warping like pulled thread.

Aetheris spoke before anyone else. "Vacuum energy destabilization detected. Origin unknown."

Reality fractured. A rift tore itself open at the dais — silent, clean, blinding. From within it stepped three figures: no armor, no weapons, only light and memory — the **Harbingers**. Their forms shimmered with both humanity and divinity. They had once been of the Celestian Pact, once believed the lie, and had defied it.

The first stepped forward, gaze on Xylaris alone. "You spoke truth when truth was forbidden."

The second bowed her head slightly. "And we have come, because the end you now shape is not yours alone to bear."

The third extended a hand. "We stand with you, Xylaris, until the last star burns."

Xylaris stood tall, watching the rift settle behind the Harbingers as silence returned to the chamber. Three stood with him now — echoes of a civilization long damned, a reminder that belief could be reclaimed, that lies could be burned away.

**Kar'yan Voss** looked toward them all, the fire of decision behind his eyes. "In the vastness of space," he said, voice low, "our greatest challenge isn't the distance between stars — but the darkness that lurks between them."
