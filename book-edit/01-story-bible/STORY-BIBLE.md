## 1. LOGLINE & PREMISE

In the year 2888, humanity — having long ago fled Old Earth in the "Great Exodus" to Veridian Prime and colonized the planet **Trident** — is suddenly assaulted by the **Obsidians**, a technologically superior alien race on a holy crusade to reclaim a sacred **artifact** that humans unwittingly activated. Elite Praetorian soldier **Lieutenant Mark Argus**, on shore leave with his family, is recalled to defend his home planet — only for the war to land literally on his doorstep, costing him everything and igniting a personal war of vengeance.

**Core engine of the book:** the collision between Argus the *family man* and Argus the *weapon*. The invasion plot is the body; the family tragedy is the heart.

## 3. WORLD & FACTIONS

- **GFC** — Galactic Forge Coalition (the human military/government).
- **STARCOM Fleet** — the GFC's space navy.
- **Praetorians** — elite special forces ("the sword and the shield"). STRIKE Teams = 4–8 operators. Advanced powered armor.
- **OGDF** — Orbital Ground Defense Force. Special-forces-grade drop infantry (rapid recon/reaction). A cut below Praetorians but elite. (Viper/"Death" Platoon is OGDF.)
- **Orbital Infantry (OI)** — shipboard infantry (e.g., Vindicator survivors led by Sgt. Banks).
- **GRID** — shadowy black-ops/R&D agency on Veridian Prime. All operatives are **Mindreavers**. Cutting-edge tech. Distrusted by line Praetorians. Has an undisclosed relationship with Strickland.
- **Mindreavers** — humans (and some Obsidians) with psychic ability. NOTE: ability is described inconsistently as *precognition ("foresight")* and *telepathy/thought-reading* — **see Discrepancy Report; must be standardized.** Also spelled "Mindweaver" in places — **pick one.**
- **Cerebral Pilots** — neurally-linked ship pilots; can also subtly influence bridge crews.

### Key Places
- **Trident** — the besieged human planet. Capital city: **New Eridanus**. Other cities: Crete City, Epsilon City, Titan City. Atlantis Underwater Contingency Station (civilian shelter).
- **Veridian Prime** — human homeworld/seat of GRID. Orion's & Voss's people are safe here.
- **Gladius** — GFC ground base on Trident (war room, GRID Section-47 two km underground).
- **The *Liberation*** — central orbital defense station (controls automated planetary defenses). **Falls & is nuked Ch8.**
- **Orbital platforms:** Candidate Maintenance/Traffic Platform, Helios outposts (prologue), Huckleberry Automated Defense Platform, Charr Communications Platform.
- **Planet Crete** & **Planet Elysium** — prior battle theaters (backstory). ⚠️ NOTE name collision: "Planet Crete" vs "Crete City" on Trident — **see Discrepancy Report.**
- **Lagrange Rift** — gravitational anomaly region near Trident; site of Helios outposts (prologue).

### Signature Tech / Terms (keep spelling & function consistent)
- **Graviton Arrestors** — armor's controlled-fall landing system ("The Boom," craters on impact).
- **STRIKE Transport** — 4-seat hyper-fast drop craft ("Punching it," "The Boom"). Reaches anywhere on Trident in ~90 sec.
- **Razor Wing** — fighter/dropship (strafe runs, escort, medevac). **Pegasus** — troop dropship.
- **TitanAI** — AI threat/vulnerability-targeting software by Cyber AI Engine (Julia's employer; nice thematic thread).
- **The Shredder** (SHR-3663R chain gun) / **Old Faithful** (Dutch's heavier variant).
- **GravLink** — FTL-bypass comms (fails in prologue).
- Obsidian tech: **red energy swords/daggers**, cloaking, self-repairing warships, FTL-near-surface.
- GRID gadgets: Whisper Charge, Nullifier Node, Insta-Shot, Nano-Sealant, AdaptiSync Modulator, NMIP, blowback gloves (Tor).
- **Obsidian warship:** *The Pillar of Anguish* (the one over New Eridanus, destroyed Ch18).

## 6. PROSE VOICE & STYLE NOTES (to preserve while editing)

**Strengths to protect:**
- Visceral, cinematic combat choreography (the kills land hard).
- Strong gallows-humor banter (Harlan/Rourke/Tessa) — the book's pulse between battles.
- Effective hardware specificity (named weapons/tech give texture).
- The family chapters (11–12) carry genuine emotional weight.

**Recurring tics to watch (cleanup targets):**
- **Tense drift** — most chapters are past tense; Ch9, 13, 16, 18 slip into present tense. **Standardize to past.**
- **Over-narration / restatement** — paragraphs often *tell* the emotion already shown ("This was more than a mission; it was a fight for survival"). Heavy candidate for fluff-cutting.
- **Adverb & abstraction stacking** ("palpable tension," "unwavering resolve," "gravity of the situation") — these phrases recur dozens of times.
- **Repeated beats** — "the weight of X pressed down," "stay sharp," camaraderie-recap paragraphs at chapter ends.
- **Word artifacts from Word** — "Top of Form / Bottom of Form," "ddeChapter," stray double-spaces, "FireFireteam."
- Dialogue is strong in banter, weaker in exposition (characters narrate plot to each other). Exposition dialogue is a prime rewrite target.

## 7. SERIES CANON ADDENDUM (from author — locks several "discrepancies" as intentional)

**These facts are known to the author/series but NOT yet to the reader. Most are seeded here and revealed in later books. Editing must protect these as deliberate setups, not fix them as errors.**

### 7.1 The Obsidians are not one race — they're a **pact of five alien races**
- Five distinct alien races, united under one **"Supreme Being"** — a *leader*, not actually supreme (the title is propaganda/perception).
- **The leader is secretly corrupt.** This is unknown to both characters and readers for now; revealed later.
- **Implication for this book:** the contradicting physical descriptions (elf-like silvery-blue/white-haired in the Prologue vs. grey/clawed/hairless in Ch6 vs. yellow-eyed/fanged in Ch18) can be **canonized as different member-races of the pact** rather than corrected to one look. *Editorial approach (recommended, pending author OK):* keep the variation but make it feel **intentional and controlled** — i.e., be internally consistent *within* a given unit/scene, and subtly imply these are different peoples, so a reader reads "variety of an alien coalition," not "author lost track." See Discrepancy Report B1 (now reframed).

### 7.2 The Artifact arc is a **deliberate cliffhanger** (sets up Book 2)
- **Book 2 opens with Death Platoon** reaching the artifact (picks up the dropped Ch13–15 thread on purpose).
- The artifact is **corrupted** — and it **corrupts those around it.** Affected beings become **"purple, shadowy, angry zombie"-like** creatures.
- The corrupted are the **Obsidians** themselves (the pact turns on itself / is twisted).
- Book 2 contains a **"mini-book within the book"**: the heroes must **un-corrupt the artifact** to stop the spread.
- **Implication for this book:** Ch13–15 should END as a clean, intentional cliffhanger (a clear "to be continued" button on Death Platoon), NOT be paid off here. The Harbinger mythology (50 artifacts, 5 humans) is correct, deliberate, long-game setup. See Discrepancy Report A1 (now reframed as intentional).

### 7.3 Tessa survives *because* she's evac'd — long-game payoff
- Tessa's Ch10 evacuation is **the reason she lives.** Her wound develops a **severe infection** requiring extended care; she **cannot return until Book 2.**
- **She is likely the sole long-term survivor of Fireteam Valor** — the others are on a darker trajectory across the series.
- **Implication:** her "I'll be back asap" line is now **dramatic irony** (poignant — she won't be). Do NOT bring her back in this book. Royce's dying message to her (Ch18) lands harder knowing she's the one who makes it. See Discrepancy Report C2 (now reframed as intentional).
