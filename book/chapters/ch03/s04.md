The two nodes did not seed one crawler each. They seeded a pack.

Aaron felt it before he saw it, the wet shuffle multiplying down the shaft, four sounds where there should have been one. They came over the rail of the flight below in a loose gray string, too-jointed, slits working the stale air, and they did not scatter the way the lone ones had. They moved together. They picked a direction.

"That's wrong," Tess said, handrail up. "They're hunting as a unit. They've got a fix on someone."

"On who," Aaron said, and dropped his sight into the lead crawler.

    > # RIFT SPAWN: hollow-crawler (tier 0).  PACK: 4 linked.
    > # shared behavior: PACK_AGGRO.
    > #   targeting rule: assign threat by NOISE_VALUE in zone.
    > #   each body holds last-heard noise source as TARGET.
    > #   target locks until a louder NOISE_VALUE registers.
    > #   pack converges on highest current NOISE_VALUE.

It clicked open behind his eyes like a latch giving. Not strength. Not numbers. A rule. The pack did not see. It listened, and walked toward whatever was loudest, holding that fix until something louder told it otherwise.

Behind him the torn-thigh man dragged in wet, ragged breaths, the loudest thing on the landing.

"They're tracking the wounded man," Aaron said. "It's noise. Loudest thing wins, until something beats it." The four crawlers strung up the flight, slits flexing toward the rear where Hutch hauled the limping man.

"So we get loud somewhere else," Tess said. Not a question. She had gotten there a beat behind him, from the outside, no overlay at all.

"We get loud somewhere else."

The extinguisher Hutch had set down sat on the lower landing. Aaron took it up two-handed and hurled it side-arm over the rail into the black below. It clanged off the steel stringer and went down end over end, ringing like a dropped bell, louder as it fell.

Every slit on the landing snapped toward it. The lead crawler's TARGET field flipped in his overlay, last-heard noise overwritten, and the pack pivoted as one body and poured down the shaft after a falling can. Away from the wounded. Away from the only people it had been built to find.

"Move," Aaron said. "While they chase it. Quiet."

They threaded the gap. Tess went first along the wall, calling the angle low so only he heard it, here, not there, six seconds. He kept his sight on the pack the whole way down, four TARGET fields fixed on a dead echo. The crawlers reached the bottom and found a can, their aggro hanging there, reassigning, a louder noise hunted and never caught.

It was not a fight he won. It was a fight he declined.

There were others he did not get to decline. Two flights down a crawler came off the wall ahead of the pack's noise, then a second behind it, and these he and Tess and Lena took the ordinary way, the regen window read and called and driven home. The reward color came warmer each time, and on the fourth landing it tipped over.

    [ SYSTEM ]
    Hostile entities eliminated: hollow-crawler (tier 0) x3.

    LEVEL UP.  You are now Level 2.
    +2 Perception.  +1 Wits.

The pressure behind his eyes eased a notch and the dark sharpened, edges resolving that had been smeared a second before. Earned, clean, no level torn loose to buy it.

They cleared the flight to six. Two more crawlers, a slit caved on the count, a window threaded by feel. Tess called the second one's high line before his overlay lit it, and he killed into the gap she predicted, and the bar tipped again.

Level three. One ladder going up at least, while the other sat at its tab of two. He flexed his hands. Steadier than they had been since the carafe shattered in the conference room, a lifetime and three floors ago.

"You called that last one before I had it," he said.

"You killed it before I finished calling it." Tess wiped the handrail on her hoodie. "We're fast on opposite ends of the same thing."

The fire door onto ground level hung at the bottom of the last flight, its grid-threshold flaring. Unsealed. Open. Pale daylight bled around its frame, wrong-colored, smoke in it. Below the door the shaft simply ended.

"That's out," Tess said, and for the first time on the descent something in her voice was not sure. "That's the street."

Aaron pushed the bar. The door swung. The city came in.
