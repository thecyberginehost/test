For a long time after, nothing came.

That was the new shape of things. He had spent the whole climb pulling the layer open with his own two hands, prying where it was sealed, paying the tab to read one more line. Now there was no line to read and no hands to read it with. He sat in the dark and the dark stayed dark, and he understood that this was what blind meant. Not that the world went quiet. That the asking went one way only, and the answer never came back.

So when the answer came, he felt it before he understood it.

It did not surface. Nothing surfaced anymore, and he was past expecting it to. It arrived. Something pressed into the back of his sight, into the part of him that used to be the overlay and was now just a closed room, and it set text there the way you set a glass down in front of a man who cannot reach the shelf. He had never once received. He had only ever stolen. The thing he had eavesdropped on for a season, the thing he had pried and argued with and run experiments against, came to him now, because it could not be eavesdropped on anymore. It had to speak, or it would not be heard. And it spoke.

> [ SYSTEM ]
> SETTLEMENT_TRIAL: resolved.
> Win-condition met on the read of this account.
> Result: the defenders of this settlement hold.
> The species adapts. The wager settles in their favor.
> So measured. So recorded.

He read it the only way left to him, delivered, set in front of him, a gift or a verdict and no way to tell which.

The species adapts. Billions of them. The ones above this floor who never knew the sky was a sky in a bet, the ones who got clean cards and grew on the ordinary ladder and fought their own stairwells with no overlay to cheat by. The ones still alive at the end of the worst day the world ever shipped. They held, on the read of one flagged account that could not read its own sheet anymore. He had flipped a single value on a single mechanic and the whole species came down on the right side of it. The math of that would not fit in a Level 1 body. He sat there and let it not fit.

He had won. He had bought it with everything, and he could not read the receipt.

That was the part that sat strangest. Not the win. The blindness around the win. Somewhere a value had been totted up that said the debt came due exactly here, at the one edit that mattered, on the one account the engine swore it could not parse. Honest engineering, he had told himself the whole way down. Technical debt, the shortcuts compounding until they all came due at once, his own bill, his own hand on every line of it. He believed that. He had built the tab himself, tick by tick, and known the wall was there.

And underneath the believing ran the other reading, the one he could no longer chase to ground. A bug that blinds itself on a timer is one you never have to patch. A leash that ties off the one account you cannot hold any other way. The debt that took his sight at the exact moment he beat the thing that watched him, was that his own honest math, or a fence left standing on purpose, a kindness or a cage worn so smooth he could not feel the seam. He would have read the answer once. He could have pried it open and paid the tax and known. Now the question had nowhere to go. It pushed at the closed room and got nothing back, and it would push at that room for the rest of his life.

He had saved a world he could no longer hear thank him. He sat blind at the bottom of it and held the win and the question both, and let neither of them go.
