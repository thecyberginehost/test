He pressed it.

Not the value. The will to spend himself. That came first, a hinge swinging where for one breath it had held, and then the keystroke was no longer a thing he was about to do. It was a thing he was inside of, the way you are inside a fall once your foot has left the ledge.

The boss came around again. It always came around. Sixteen times now, and sixteen times it had been him, his own hand handed back with the edges filed sharp. The Suffocation cap. The integrity zero. The redirected payout he had inverted one breath ago and watched pin shut, denied to account, the door slammed in his face by a thing wearing his own work. Every old trick patched. He had stopped reaching for the shelf of things he had already done.

So he reached for the one thing he never had.

Marcus held. That was the whole of it on the left, a man on one knee with a shield up by reflex and an arm that would not close. He was buying Aaron seconds with a body that had nothing left, leaning the great slab of himself into the boss's next swing so it came late. Priya had moved up under the shield's lee, hands open and gray-faced and no green in them, holding Marcus together by will where her class had run dry. There was a gap to Marcus's left where a call should have come, the half-beat warning that read the swing before it broke. None came. He worked the gap himself, slower than she had ever been, and did not let himself look at it. Later. If there was a later.

This was their second. They had bought it. He took it the way you take something someone has died handing you, both hands, no fumbling.

He brought the layer up. One more time, and he meant it to be the last. The pressure crested behind his eye, that wrong fullness he had carried since the second night, and the wet click came as the gray comment-text bled up through the failing shield. At ninety-nine it tore as it rose, the layer doubling and smearing, two worlds laid a finger's width off true, and he read through the tear anyway. He had read through worse on the descent.

He went past the boss. Past the patched payout and the patched heal and the patched aggro, all of it his, all of it expected. He went down to the floor of the place, to the grammar he had pried open in the heart of the dungeon two seals deep and read at the bottom and never once written to. CORE_GRAMMAR. The win condition of the whole settlement, the flag that decided when the measurement was satisfied, and it sat there legible to his account, exactly as he had left it. Read. Never edited. No patch had ever closed it because no one had ever made the move. It could not patch a move that had never been played.

It would land for that reason and no other. Not because the god was sloppy. It had read him the whole descent and patched everything he had ever done. The one code it could not pre-empt was the code it had thought was past him, and he had learned to read it. Every nosebleed, every level burned, every floor that hurt going down had bought this. Not a hole left open. Literacy, finally caught up to the floor of the world.

And it bent for him. Only for him. A settlement-bound account could not touch its own win condition, the rule held shut against every other hand in the city. His did not read shut. His read open, the rules folding aside for the one account flagged exempt and not telling him why.

He set the cursor on the value.

Ninety-nine behind his eye. One keystroke past it.

His hand did not shake. The boss came around. Marcus held the half-step. The grammar lay open under his cursor, the whole heart of the thing waiting on a single flip, and he chose it, knowing exactly what crossing a hundred took from him, and began.
