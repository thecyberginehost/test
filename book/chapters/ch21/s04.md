The apex went quiet like a building after the last fan dies.

It happened in layers he could only hear and feel. The boss's weight had stopped its fight with the floor, and into the space where its noise had been came the smaller sounds the fight had drowned all day. Water somewhere, dropping. The tick of cooling stone. Marcus's breathing, ragged, three feet to his left. Aaron sat in his own dark and let it all reach him one piece at a time, because one piece at a time was the only way anything reached him now.

"The light's going," Priya said. She said it slow, like she was making sure of it before she let herself believe it. "Aaron. The rift-light. It's draining out of the walls. It's just going."

He could not see it go. He took it on her voice. He remembered the apex when it opened, cold seam-light bleeding from every joint of the place, the whole district-sized throat of it lit like the inside of a wound that would not close. Now a triage nurse who had never seen the layer under the world was telling him it was closing, and he believed her, because she had earned the right to be his eyes and he had stopped being too proud to lend them.

"There's a way up." Marcus, from one knee. He hauled the words out. "Behind the. Where it came in. Stairs. The seal's open. I can see daylight."

Daylight. The deepest floor in the world, and somewhere above it the sky still ran.

Aaron's body chose that moment to understand what it had been through. The shaking started in his hands and went up his arms and would not stop. A Level 1 body, fifty hit points, ten of everything that used to be thirty. The cold of the stone came up through him with nothing to hold it off. He had spent a fortune to be this small, and sat in the dark and let it shake him.

He had started on a Tuesday. He let himself land on that, because the contrast was the only thing big enough to hold the rest. An office, a dashboard that swore a dead server was healthy while he lost the argument about it. A man who read what systems hid and got soft-deflected for his trouble. The first stairwell, three floors of screaming, no skill and no weapon and only his sight. A crew that could barely clear a flight of stairs without somebody bleeding out at the top of it.

And here. The apex. The deepest, deadliest place the world had grown, the measurement built to settle a bet placed on the whole species, draining quiet around three people who had walked in on purpose and walked out alive. The ground under him would have erased the man from that Tuesday in a breath. It would have erased the crew that fled that first stairwell. It did not. They had climbed down to the bottom of everything and stood up at the end of it.

Three of them stood up.

The fourth was a floor he could not read in time, the surge-gate not far above this one, where his blurred sight came a half-beat late. Tess should have called this clear. She read terrain the way he read code, and she would have named every cold seam in this throat going dark, and she was not here to do it. The win sat in his chest next to the hole where she went. The ground he was shaking on had been bought, part of it, with her. He did not let the triumph go clean. He would not have known how, or wanted to.

"We're up," Marcus said again, like saying it kept it true.

Aaron nodded into the dark. He could not see the place go safe. He could only feel the cold and the shaking and the quiet, and trust the two voices telling him the worst thing the world had ever built had stopped being a threat. He had spent the whole climb insisting he did the trusting alone.

He sat blind at the bottom of the world and let them tell him it was over.
