The boss did not know yet.

It came around for its seventeenth pass the same as the sixteen before, his own work in its face, sure of the floor it stood on. The flag under that floor had turned. It could not feel the turn. Neither could Aaron, not anymore. He had to watch the slow way, with eyes a Level 1 body had given back to him, and wait for it to land on the only creature in the room that did not know the score had changed.

It changed mid-stride.

The sound came first. Not a roar. A wrongness in the air, low, the pitch of a room when the power cuts and every fan you stopped hearing stops at once. The boss's weight broke step. One of its limbs, the long reaching one it had taken from the door he annotated on the second day, came down on stone that should have held it and did not, and the limb went through, and the thing pitched.

"It's stopping," Marcus said. His voice was wrecked, scraped down to a register Aaron had not heard from him. "Kessler. It's. Look at it."

Aaron looked. He could not pry the seam, could not read what the trial wrote into its status as the win-flag counted against it now instead of for it. The grammar of its ending ran somewhere under the floor where he would never see grammar again. He got the outside of it, the part anyone in the room got, and it was enormous.

The boss came apart from the inside out.

Light bled from the joints first, the cold seam-light it had carried since the apex opened, and then the light reversed and ran inward and took the shape with it. A god had built this thing out of his ledger, every trick he ever ran stitched into a single body to enforce the trial against them. Now the trial had nothing left to enforce. It fell in stages, each one dropping a register of sound below the last until the whole of it was just weight finding the floor.

"Priya." Marcus again, sharper. "He needs to. Is he."

"I've got him." Her hand found Aaron's shoulder. Gray to the lips, nothing left in her account, and she spent the last of it propping him upright so he would not miss this with his face in the dust. "Aaron. It's down on the left. The big arm's gone. It's not even fighting it."

He turned his head toward where her voice put it. A blur of failing light and the long settle of mass. The man who had read everything in the building stood in the one room he could not parse and let a triage nurse who had never seen the hidden layer once in her life narrate his own victory back to him.

The grief of it surprised him. He had braced for the dark. He had not braced for needing to be told.

"Tell me when it's all the way down," he said. His voice came out flat. It was the only way he could keep it level.

"It's down." Marcus did not wait long. The crash that answered him was the largest yet, a single concussion that came up through the floor and into the bone of his shins, and the dust rose off the stone in a sheet his ordinary eyes could finally hold, gray, slow in the failing light, the first thing all day they had owned outright. The standing weight that had pressed on the room for the whole fight lifted off them. The air changed. Even blind he felt the air change.

Quiet came down after it like a second collapse.

No one cheered. Priya was crying without sound, holding him and holding herself up with the same grip. Marcus had gone down to one knee behind the shield he could no longer hold square, and stayed there, breathing.

Aaron sat in the dark of his own head with the dust settling on him and listened to the room not trying to kill them. He could not see that the threat was over. He believed it because two people he had spent the climb pretending he did not need had told him so, and that was enough.
