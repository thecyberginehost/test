He flipped it.

One value. Not the boss, not the payout, not any of the sixteen old tricks the thing had eaten. The flag at the floor of the place, the one number the trial checked when it asked itself who had won. CORE_GRAMMAR's win condition, the line that decided who the measurement credited when it closed. He moved it from the god's column to theirs.

Narrow. One value on one mechanic, no wider than the Suffocation cap had been. All its weight was in where it sat.

A settlement-bound account could not have done it. The rule held shut against every hand in the city. His read it open. The terms folded aside for the one flag marked exempt, and did not say why, and he did not ask. There was no time to ask a thing that had never answered.

The bill came as one act.

The progress charge hit first, the forge again, the same drop he had taken when he bought the crew their door. Fifty levels asked. He had thirteen, twelve above the floor, and the account paid them all the way down. Level 13 to Level 1. His HP guttered from a wall to a sliver. Perception collapsed to ten, to the eyes he had been born with. Wits to ten. The thirty-eight it could not collect, the trial carried, because it still could not bill an account it would not parse.

Then the tax.

He had one point of room. Ninety-nine, a single rung to a hundred, and the Tier-3 tax was twenty. He felt it come up past that one point and keep coming, more than the room he had, and there was nowhere for the rest to go but the wall. The ceiling took it. It stopped flush, full. Decode_debt, one hundred out of one hundred, closed.

And the layer began to go.

He had known it would. That was the whole of the choice, made one breath ago with his hand steady. The gray comment-text he had read through every floor of the descent, dimming now, the way a screen dims when the last of the charge bleeds out, except it would not come back on. He read the final thing it would ever show him before the dark closed over the words.

> [ SYSTEM ]
> ANNOTATION ACCEPTED.  Account: KESSLER, A.
> Target: SETTLEMENT_TRIAL, win-condition (value).
> Win-condition revised: trial credits the defenders of this settlement.
> Scope: this account. This settlement.
> Cost charged: 50 Levels.  Paid: 12 (to floor).  Carried: 38.
> Decode tax applied.  decode_debt 100 / 100.
> Operator overlay: offline.

He read offline and the word was gone before he finished it.

The gray went out. Not torn, not strobing, not smeared and recovered as it had on the bad floors. It simply was not there. Behind his right eye the pressure he had hated for nineteen days let go all at once, and the relief was the worst thing he had ever felt, because it meant the door had shut on the room he lived in. For one heartbeat, in the last grain of the dark, he felt the shape of a question he would never read the answer to. Whether the debt had been honest weight he chose to carry, or a leash the thing had left on the one account it could not open, set to blind itself on a clock so it never had to be patched. The shape was there. Then the layer that could have answered it was off, and the question went with it.

He kept everything. He felt that too, in the place where reading used to be. The Suffocation cap, the skill, the door at the heart of the dungeon. Every edit he had ever written still held, locked, none of them taking the win-flag back. Only the eyes that wrote them were gone.

Aaron opened his actual eyes wider, because they were all he had now.

The dungeon came at him flat. No grammar under the floor, no comment-text bleeding up the boss's edge to tell him where it would swing. Just stone, and dust in the bad light, and the smell of blood that was Marcus's and Priya's and his own. To his left a man on one knee with a shield he could no longer hold square. Behind it a woman gray to the lips with nothing left to give. The boss above them, his own work wearing his own face, coming around for the seventeenth time.

And he could not read it. The world was the size of his eyes again, and it was loud, and it was close, and somewhere under the floor a flag he could no longer see had already turned over and begun to count for their side.

He set his feet in the dark of his own head and waited, as the rest of them had waited all along, to find out whether it would be enough.
